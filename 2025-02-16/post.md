# Tule Canyon Truck Trail to CA-74

Feb 16, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [33.465893, -116.634140](https://maps.app.goo.gl/K9idMHW2xjtnQerD7) (Tule Canyon Truck Trail & Terwilliger Road)  
**End:** [33.563808, -116.576832](https://maps.app.goo.gl/6snxKfzvPBHznNJr6) (Pacific Crest Trail at CA-74)

**Distance:** 18.7 mi (up to PCT mile 151.9, 5.7% completed)  
**Time:** 8 hours 37 minutes (start 6:30am, end 3:06pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 3,846 ft (low 3,192 ft – high 5,571 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 2,881 ft (start 3,936 ft – end 4,901 ft)

---

Mr. Prickly Pear kept me very good company today. For the unanointed among you: Mr. Prickly Pear is the endearing name I have given to the prickly pear cactus, a frequent sight in the Southwestern United States. And every time I meet him on trail, I give him a friendly greeting and hope that he is not too prickly today. Fortunately, he was in a good mood today and didn’t prick me. Especially the first half of the trail was lined with plenty of prickly pear cactus, narrow leaf yucca, and cholla cactus.

Today was supposed to be a lot easier compared to the ordeal from last time. And for the most part, that was true – except for the arduous ascent to Lookout Mountain, but more about that later. Given the severe rain just a few days ago, I decided to park along Terwilliger Road and not try my luck on the rugged Tule Canyon Truck Trail. And that turned out to be the right decision once I saw the puddles and mud I would have had to navigate.

I left the hotel at 5:34am, dropped off my bike at 5:50am, and started hiking at 6:30am, just a minute past sunrise. Lots of cirrus cloud in the east turned the sun into a faint yellow ball of light, but my swift pace kept me warm. I passed several private properties on Tule Canyon Truck Trail, one of which had half a dozen guard dogs that were very loudly and aggressively barking at me. I was glad they were all behind a fence.

Crossing into Anza-Borrego Desert State Park, my first stop was Tule Spring, just at the end of the dirt road, at the bottom of Tule Canyon. As I was approaching the last turn, I saw five backpackers having breakfast in front of the concrete water tank. We exchanged a few words, I asked where exactly the spring was and getting to know that it was hard to get to and only a trickle, I decided to skip the scramble down the last few feet. I was surprised to see how little water there was at the bottom of the canyon given that the last week had brought more than 2 inches of rain. But I suppose this was evidence of how parched the landscape had really been.

![Looking across Nance Canyon (33°28’46.08″ N 116°34’26.718″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/281A4519-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Nance Canyon (33°28’49.68″ N 116°34’18.996″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/281A4526-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Retracing my steps for a quarter mile I finally resumed the PCT journey. The trail was undulating the vicinity of Tule Canyon with distant views of Combs Peak from the last hike. After crossing Coyote Canyon Road, the trail dropped down into dry Nance Canyon before switchbacking up the northern side. As I reached the top, I was rewarded with a first glance of the snow capped San Jacinto Mountains. The trail continued meandering north, eventually revealing great views of Terwilliger Valley after crossing a dirt road.

Staying on the western slope of Table Mountain, the trail was mostly keeping elevation by following the contours of the mountain. I was saddened to come across a sign from a former trail angel who had stopped offering services to hikers after repeated criminal activity on their property. Veering northeast, the trail crossed Table Mountain Truck Trail and descended steeply into Alkali Wash, only to switchback up the northern side to Sagebrush Flat.

![Anza-Borrego Desert State Park (33°29’26.238″ N 116°34’2.058″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/281A4540-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The sad end of trail magic (33°30’52.452″ N 116°35’14.982″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/281A4548-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

As I reached the top, I was rewarded by excellent views in several directions. Horse Canyon was to the east, with Vandeventer Flat and the Santa Rosa Mountains in the distance. Burnt Valley was to the west and the San Jacinto Mountains were to the north. Straight ahead was Lookout Mountain, the last highlight of today’s hike. I was yet to decide whether I would take the short spur trail up to the summit and I was going to make that determination on the spot once I got there.

![Terwilliger Valley (33°31’14.358″ N 116°35’32.82″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/281A4551-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Alkali Wash (33°32’1.536″ N 116°35’10.2″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/281A4560-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Anticipating even better views from the summit and telling myself that I should make the best of all available side trips, I decided to tackle the summit. But I was in for an incredibly arduous ascent. Within just half a mile, the trail was ascending 650 feet, first up a ravine, and then just on the bare slope. It was dangerously steep and I had to stop every 50 steps to catch by breath. But my pride kept me going – I couldn’t just turn around half way. I eventually reached the top. The views were somewhat obscured by lots of shrubs, but I did manage to take a few photos. There was a trail register in a rusty metal can next to the summit marker which I signed. The last person had been up here more than a week ago.

![Santa Rosa Mountains (33°32’16.992″ N 116°34’55.164″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/281A4568-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The ascent to Lookout Mountain (33°33’7.32″ N 116°34’29.352″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/281A4582-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I carefully made my way back down and resumed on the PCT. The trail bent north ascending over a saddle and then dropped quickly to cross CA-74 where I reached my bike at the parking lot.

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt with some rocky stretches, well maintained.

**Parking:** With a 4WD you could drive all the way to the boundary with Anza-Borrego Desert State Park where there is plenty of room to park. With a 2WD you can park along Terwilliger Road before the junction with Tule Canyon Truck Trail.

**Weather:** Most sunny throughout the day with a high of 98°F (around 1:30pm, in the ravine on the southern side of Lookout Mountain) and a low of 40°F (on Tule Canyon Truck Trail, about 1.8 miles from Terwilliger Road). Lots of cirrus clouds, especially in the afternoon.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Anza-Borrego Desert State Park and San Bernardino National Forest. No permits required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.2

Difficulty: Moderate  
Scenery: Medium  
Logistics: Easy

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 11.6 mi (1 hour 5 min), 609 ft elevation gain, 10.67 mph average speed

The bike ride back was similar to the “usual” rides I had had before, this time a combination of paved roads and dirt roads. Unfortunately, I had forgotten my helmet in the car, so I had to make due without it. I turned west onto CA-74 and then left onto CA-371 after a mile. A short but steep uphill stretch lead me to a saddle and a few welcome downhill miles. Turning left onto Burnt Valley Road, I left the busy highway. The road had some elevation gain, but mostly moderate enough that I could take it with a slow but steady pace.

Terwilliger Road was unpaved for the first mile, going up and down with muddy puddles of rain water. I was glad to be back on pavement and several miles downhill before the road became unpaved again. The last mile had me worried a bit because this was the stretch where I had previously encountered a guard dog chasing me. But I was lucky and the very same dog was behind a fence today – although still barking aggressively at me together with another dozen dogs. I reached my car at 4:17pm to start my drive back home.
