# Mill Creek Summit to Indian Canyon Trailhead (Day 1)

Jan 10, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.391734, -118.081226](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Wof98oBFBHZGWe7V7) (Mill Creek Summit)  
**End:** [34.379903, -118.189440](https://maps.app.goo.gl/oPtAEa7pUE2kmAcR7) (Messenger Flats Campground)

**Distance:** 16.3 mi (up to PCT mile 430.4, 16.2% completed)  
**Time:** 7 hours 41 minutes (start 7:43am, end 3:24pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 3,795 ft (low 4,910 ft – high 6,398 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 2,850 ft (start 4,910 ft – end 5,855 ft)

---

I was very eager to hike the next section of the Pacific Crest Trail. It had been 3 weeks since I had passed Pacifico Mountain. My plan had been to do several hikes over the holidays. But the weather hadn’t been cooperative at all. On the contrary, Southern California had received torrential rains on Christmas Eve and Christmas Day with some areas getting more than 11 inches of rain in a mere 14 hours. The results had been catastrophic. Widespread flooding and mudslides. Bone dry river beds had turned into 100 feet wide torrents sweeping cars and houses away. Wrightwood, the small mountain town I had stayed at in early December, was buried under several feet of mud. Highway 2 was washed out in several areas leading to a 30 mile indefinite closure. The destruction was heartbreaking. Part of me was glad I had done two more hikes right before the holidays to put me far enough west, closer to Palmdale.

![Aliso Canyon (34°23’58.2934″ N 118°4’56.3163″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9637-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

A few days after Christmas, the weather had cleared up, but Angeles Forest Highway, my access to the next section of the PCT was still closed due to flooding and debris. And when it finally opened up on December 30, the next stretch of 7 consecutive days of rain was just ahead. Fortunately none of that turned into snow in the area I was going to hike. What gave me pause for the next hike though was the high wind advisory in place over the weekend. Winds of 20 to 30 mph with gusts of 50 mph were forecast. And this was going to be my very first backpacking section on the PCT. I was a little worried how the night would go in a tent but my eagerness to continue prevailed and I was prepared to endure whatever nature was challenging me with – and challenging me it did.

![San Gabriel Peak, Strawberry Peak, and Josephine Peak in the distance (34°23’49.3429″ N 118°6’19.249″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9660-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

This section of the PCT from Mill Creek Summit to Indian Canyon Trailhead was just slightly too long to be a day hike, even more so in January when daylight was in short supply. Dividing it into smaller sections that would be doable in a day proved equally difficult given limited access paths. Fortunately, there was a campground in the very middle, so it would be straightforward to camp. Getting water wouldn’t be easy though. There were only limited options, but the recent rains were the most optimal conditions I would ever have.

![Beartrap Canyon with Palmdale in the distance (34°23’10.2709″ N 118°7’42.8699″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9679-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Mount Gleason (34°22’57.6419″ N 118°8’24.5049″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9698-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I left the hotel in Palmdale at 6:13am. My Uber driver Andrew was going to pick me up at Indian Canyon Trailhead at 7am. I arrived well ahead of time and packed the last few things. I decided to bring a second hoodie just in case it was too cold at night. Andrew arrived on time. But his gas tank was empty, so he first had to stop for gas in Acton. That took longer than expected because his credit card was getting declined. I was getting a little worried. He eventually just paid $10 cash which was enough for about 2 gallons. And there was yet another complication. The Uber app was under the wrong assumption that Angeles Forest Highway was still closed. So I had only been able to book a ride to the beginning of the closure, about 3 miles from Mill Creek Summit. I asked Andrew whether he could drive me the remaining 3 miles for an extra $20 cash and he agreed.

![One of the many blowdowns on trail (34°22’48.0111″ N 118°8’31.4823″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9702-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Gleason Canyon (34°22’48.6998″ N 118°9’56.9333″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9720-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

When Andrew dropped me off, Mill Creek Summit was still in the shade even though sunset had been half an hour ago. It was very windy and chilly. I put on my jacket and started my journey. The PCT was heading north, staying above closed Mount Gleason Road, and gaining elevation quickly. The wind gusts were strong and I had to hold on to my hat. Aliso Canyon to the north was the first great view with Palmdale in the distance. The PCT turned west and eventually moved to the south side of the ridge. There was almost no wind here, it was sunny, and the mountains to the south were green. Crossing Mount Gleason Road, the trail moved back to the northern slope and into the shade. Downed trees were blocking the trail in multiple places, evidence of the strong storm from the last few weeks. Initially, they were somewhat easy to pass, climbing over or under the branches across the trail. However, some trees proved to be much harder to get around given the dense vegetation and steep terrain on both sides. It was evident that I was the first person to come along since these trees had fallen onto the trail.

![Looking back towards Pacifico Mountain and snow covered Throop Peak (34°22’48.6998″ N 118°9’56.9333″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9722-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![View from Mount Gleason (34°22’55.5533″ N 118°10’54.6549″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9730-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Crossing Bear Trap Canyon Road 4N24, the PCT paralleled a seasonal creek, then reached the head of Gleason Canyon. The bald Sierra Pelona range was dominating the horizon. Looking west, Mount Gleason soon came into view. The 2009 Station Fire had burned most of the surrounding forest, but it had left a few pine trees on the top and northern side of Mount Gleason intact. I reached a number of switchbacks that opened up views to the east as far as Pacifico Mountain and snow covered Mount Baden-Powell where I had been several weeks ago. A saddle on the north side of Mount Gleason was the highest point for today at 6,400 ft. It was only 1:45pm, but the air was chilly and the constant wind did not make me feel warm despite the multiple layers I was wearing. I considered taking the short detour to the very top of Mount Gleason, but decided against it.

![Pacoima Canyon (34°22’51.3196″ N 118°10’55.1734″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9734-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Messenger Flats Campground – the small patch still covered in pine trees (34°22’46.2573″ N 118°10’55.0059″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9742-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Passing the saddle, the PCT immediately switchbacked down. Views of green Pacoima Canyon to the west were stunning though. And to the north I could see a small area with tall pine trees that was Messenger Flats Campground, my destination for today. The gate to the campground was closed and a large water puddle on the ground was still frozen. The campground was spacious with multiple picnic tables, two restrooms, and several bear proof lockers. I dropped off my backpack but I still needed to get water and there was none here at the campground. About 1.6 miles further on the PCT, there was a spring that would likely flow at this time of the year. I grabbed my water bag and kept going. The trail stuck to the northern side of the ridge, rounding Messenger Peak. It felt great to walk without a heavy pack on the back. I reached the spring and it was flowing well. After grabbing a gallon of water, I followed Moody Truck Trail south and turned onto Mount Gleason Road which was taking me back to the campground. It was uphill almost all of the way and the wind here was very strong, stronger than what I had experienced on the northern side.

![Messenger Flats Campground (34°22’47.616″ N 118°11’22.956″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9756-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![View towards Mill Canyon (34°22’59.7745″ N 118°11’59.8863″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9767-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Back at the campground, I picked a spot right next to one of the restrooms that was providing a bit of protection from the wind and set up my tent. When my air mattress was fully inflated, I laid down and took a break. The sun had already disappeared behind the mountains. It was barely 5pm, but I wanted to make dinner while there was still light. I had brought Mac & Cheese and just needed to boil some water. As luck would have it, I ran out of propane before the water was fully boiling. I thought there was more left in the canister, but apparently not. This was already the second time it had happened to me on the first evening while backpacking. The water was reasonably hot though, so it did its job hydrating the meal which I devoured with great appetite. For the remainder of the evening, I listened to some podcasts and watched a few YouTube videos I had downloaded beforehand. Eventually, sleep took over.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Lots and lots of blowdowns throughout the entire hike, some very difficult to pass given dense vegetation and steep terrain. Significant impact from the 2009 Station Fire throughout most of the hike.

**Parking:** There is plenty of parking at Indian Canyon Trailhead.

**Crowds:** Complete solitude. I did not meet a single person the entire day.

**Weather:** Mostly sunny with blue skies with some cirrus clouds. Low of 35°F / 2°C in the morning near Mill Creek Summit. High of 50°F / 10°C on a wind protected stretch east of Mount Gleason. Very strong winds of 20 to 30 mph with gusts of 50 mph.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Angeles National Forest. San Gabriel Mountains National Monument. Annual Interagency Permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.2

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Moderate  
Logistics: Easy
