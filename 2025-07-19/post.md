# Mission Springs Trail Camp to Onyx Summit

Jul 19, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.161872, -116.784984](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Sn9WNVZwbZvP7s3x8) (Heart Bar Campground)  
**End:** [34.192210, -116.719179](https://maps.app.goo.gl/8witWARqmNiREcyBA) (Onyx Summit)

**Distance:** 20.1 mi (up to PCT mile 252.1, 9.5% completed)  
**Time:** 9 hours 14 minutes (start 5:50am, end 3:04pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 6,131 ft (low 6,862 ft – high 8,778 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 4,489 ft (start 6,862 ft – end 8,504 ft)

---

I was back in Big Bear, staying in the exact same room I had stayed in the last time. This was going to be the first time since April that I would do a bike shuttle again. I got up at 4:45am. As I was driving up CA-38 to Onyx Summit, I could see the mountains to the east starting to glow in orange hues. When I dropped off my bike at the summit, there were two ladies just about to head up to Onyx Peak with their headlamps on. I left the summit at 5:30am and got to the same spot I had parked in the last time, just in front of the gate to Heart Bar Campground.

In anticipation of the higher temperatures later in the day, I had only put on a t-shirt. It was still 50°F / 10°C when I started hiking, so I was a bit chilly. The first mile and a half was the familiar dirt road from last time. After crossing Coon Creek and Heart Bar Creek, I turned right to proceed on Forest Road 1N05. Nature’s force was still on vivid display. I had to cross a large washed out creek where the destruction during Hurricane Hillary had been unimaginable. It was crazy that I had originally intended to drive up this forest road. I suspect it will never see a vehicle again, the cost of repairs would be substantial.

![Sunrise over Sugarloaf Mountain and Sugarlump (34°8’52.704″ N 116°46’50.082″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6519-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

When the road was not washed out, it was actually a fairly pleasant experience. The sun was peeking over the horizon. Great views across the valley featured Sugarloaf Mountain and Sugarlump. At 3 miles, I reached the Aspen Grove trailhead and turned onto Aspen Grove Trail – or rather what was left of it. The trail descended down to Fish Creek which had seen the same devastating flooding as Mission Creek from last time. The ridge on the other side was bare, burned during the 2015 Lake Fire. Aspen Grove Trail had originally crossed Fish Creek and then followed its outline on the western side. Much of the trail was gone now or heavily overgrown.

Getting into the creek bed proved more difficult than I thought. The bed was about 15 feet below me and it took me a moment to find a little side creek that I could use to descend. From there it was up the creek bed. There was still a little bit of water flowing, but nothing to impede me. After half a mile, I saw the remnants of the trail up above. I scrambled to get out of the creek bed and follow the trail. But it was heavily overgrown with very thick boxthorn. I was constantly getting pricked. Eventually, I decided to get back down into the creek bed where walking would be slightly more pleasant.

![Crossing a meadow with hip high grass (34°7’41.616″ N 116°46’30.948″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6530-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

At 5 miles, the creek bed was splitting. I took the left arm, but quickly realized that it would take me quite far from the original trail on the map. So I backtracked and followed the right arm. That however was a dead end at a 10 feet waterfall that I couldn’t climb. So I went back to the left arm. The creek bed leveled out and disappeared forcing me to bushwhack through more boxthorn. A quarter mile later, I reached a meadow that I had to cross to get back to the original trail. The grass was hip high, but I was lucky that it was mostly dry. I made my way through the meadow and was eventually able to find the trail. It was still somewhat overgrown here, but much less compared to the lower reaches of Fish Creek.

I reached Fish Creek Trailhead at 6.5 miles and followed Forest Road 1N05 – yes, the same road I had left earlier, Fish Creek Trailhead was the end of the road. Even up here, the road had suffered some significant washouts. Half a mile later, I arrived at Mission Springs Trail Camp where I had left the PCT last time. This was the perfect spot for my first break. The were a few picnic tables and I ended up lying on one of them and staring up into the pine trees above me.

![Looking down the Heart Bar Creek valley (34°8’28.416″ N 116°44’32.778″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6560-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

It was time to continue on the PCT now. The trail headed north gaining elevation before crossing a saddle at another intersection with Forest Road 1N05. Now on the northern side, I could appreciate the views towards Sugarloaf Mountain and back down to Heart Bar Campground. The trail eventually crossed a saddle north around Heart Bar Creek and started gaining more elevation. To the south, I could see San Gorgonio Mountain, the highest mountain in Southern California. The views down the valley were breathtaking. Rounding the corner to the north side of the ridge, I could look down into Coon Creek valley.

![San Gorgonio Mountain in the distance (34°8’43.632″ N 116°44’45.204″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6561-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![North Fork Mission Creek and Coachella Valley in the distance (34°8’33.54″ N 116°43’39.516″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6591-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail moved across white, granular Furnace marble – an unusual sight – and then rounded to the east of another peak. The view was spectacular. I could see down the entire North Fork Mission Creek valley that I had hiked up the last time. In the distance were Coachella Valley and San Jacinto Peak. The trail crossed back to the north slope and descended to Coon Creek Jumpoff, a spectacular steep granite dropoff at a tributary of North Fork Mission Creek. Just a little further was Coon Creek Cabin Campground where I took another longer break.

Cumulus clouds had started forming, making for even better photos. The trail switchbacked up the south side of the ridge before dropping gently to cross another dirt road and then reaching another creek heading north. The trail soon followed an old dirt road to Forest Road 1N02. Across the road was private property with chicken bickering and a dog barking at me. The trail ascended gently again and eventually reached an old fire road which took me down to Onyx Summit where my bike was waiting.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Forest Road 1N05 is completely washed out in a few places near Heart Bar Creek, but in decent condition further up. Aspen Grove Trail along Fish Creek is mostly non-existent after being washed out. In places where it is not washed out, it is seriously overgrown with boxthorn. The Pacific Crest Trail is mostly in good condition, although also slightly overgrown in a few places.

**Parking:** There is room for two or three cars right in front of the road gate to Heart Bar Campground.

**Weather:** Sunny with blue skies and no clouds in the morning. 50°F / 10°C at the beginning of the hike. Increasing clouds around noon, with temperatures in the sun around 89°F / 32°C. Light rain past 3pm.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Sand to Snow National Monument, including a small portion within San Gorgonio Wilderness. San Bernardino National Forest. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.2

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Very Easy

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 5.8 mi (14 min), 0 ft elevation gain, 25.15 mph average speed, 30.23 mph maximum speed

The bike ride back was a blast and the easiest I have had so far. Zero paddling required since it was all downhill on CA-38. About half way, it started raining and the large rain drops hit my face and arms like tiny needles. Fortunately, it was only a few more minutes to my car.
