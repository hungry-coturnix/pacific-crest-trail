# Coxey Road to Deep Creek

Sep 28, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.290022, -116.978841](https://maps.app.goo.gl/dXtLVHoFRsqAhPBB7) (Coxey Road, near Hanna Flats Campground)  
**End:** [34.266669, -117.168023](https://maps.app.goo.gl/nBpWamdztu7gVbKS8) (North Shore Campground)

**Distance:** 18.1 mi (up to PCT mile 298.5, 11.3% completed)  
**Time:** 7 hours 29 minutes (start 7:30am, end 2:59pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 3,488 ft (low 4,597 ft – high 6,946 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 5,188 ft (start 6,946 ft – end 5,246 ft)

---

It had been 6 weeks since my last PCT hike and I felt quite deprived. Colorado had been an amazing trip and I had somewhat fallen in love with the Continental Divide Trail (which I may end up hiking when I am done with the PCT), but the PCT was still my first love. This section of the PCT came with two challenges. In February 2019, a large storm damaged the area around Splinters Cabin at the confluence of Deep Creek and Little Bear Creek. A [Forest Closure Order](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/sanbernardino/alerts/deep-creek-area-closure-aztec-falls) has been in place since then (and is still valid for at least another 2 years) that closed Splinters Cabin itself and the Splinters Cabin Road thereby preventing any direct trail access to the PCT at the end of this section. My plan around the closed area was to “hike” cross country from the PCT to Crab Flats Road up a dry bouldery ravine for about 500 ft / 150 m. Spoiler alert: It was not as easy as I thought.

The second challenge was how to get back to the car. It would have been possible to cycle back but that would have involved very rugged dirt roads and significant uphill over about 20 miles. So I opted for an Uber ride instead. The challenge here was that Coxey Road was unpaved and the last 2 miles were somewhat rugged as well, so I was worried how far an Uber would actually drive. To my surprise, the assigned car for my reservation was a Tesla and that worried me even more.

![Holcomb Creek during the early morning hours (34°18’2.484″ N 116°59’0.906″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8075-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I stayed in Running Springs for the night. When I arrived in the late afternoon, there were dark clouds up above and the town was literally in the clouds. The weather forecast for the next day looked much better, so I was optimistic the clouds would be gone by morning. I woke up early since I was still jetlagged from my NYC trip just a day before and when I looked out the window, I could see stars and that was a good sign. I left the hotel at 5:40am and headed to North Shore Campground next to Lake Arrowhead. Unexpectedly, the campground was already closed for the season, so I parked right at the gate where it had room for two cars. I wasn’t too concerned, it would just mean a quarter mile more to hike.

![California Coffeeberry along the trail (34°17’44.55″ N 116°59’41.028″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8078-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Robert, the Tesla driver, arrived on time at 6:30am. He had a blond mohawk and seemed pretty chill. The ride was about one hour. When we turned onto Highway 18 just south of Lake Arrowhead, the scenery was stunning. It was just around sunrise. The sky above was cloudless, but just a few hundred feet below was a thick cloud cover stretching over the Los Angeles metropolitan area for as far as I could see. The highway was right on the rim of the mountains and offered spectacular views south. I could only marvel at this view. While we were driving, I chatted with Robert about Coxey Road and offered that I would walk the rest of the way if he felt uncomfortable driving further. He admitted that he had also been a bit worried and was grateful for the offer. We ended up driving the entire unpaved section to Hanna Flats Campground and stopped about half a mile further when the pavement ended. I could see Robert’s uneasiness about driving further.

I was about 1.5 miles away from the PCT. Coxey Road was still in the shade. A few guys were unloading their motorcycles at Holcomb Creek where I had left the PCT last time. I was excited to be back. The trail paralleled Holcomb Creek for about 1.5 miles before turning west onto Lower Larga Flat. The area had been burned by several fires over the past decades and that was fairly visible through the charred trees, skeletal forest, and thorny brush. After crossing another dirt road, the PCT meandered up and down along boulders. In the distance, I could see the San Gabriel Mountains and the thick low hanging cloud cover I had seen during the drive.

![San Gabriel Mountains in the distance (34°17’7.86″ N 117°0’23.91″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8092-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Holcomb Creek (34°16’41.751″ N 117°3’57.402″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8129-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The PCT descended down to Holcomb Creek again and crossed it. Water was flowing strong even though the creek had been completely dry further upstream. Crossing Crab Flats Road, the trail stayed on the fairly overgrown south side before crossing back to the north side into more open terrain. I passed a few large sandy campsites and then crossed Holcomb Creek a third time. The trail passed Holcomb Crossing Trail Camp, following by Crab Flats Trail which was super rugged and abused by motorcyclists. Another quarter mile later, I took my second break at Bench Trail Camp. It had warmed up quite a bit, but I hadn’t brought sunscreen, so I left my hoodie on and tried to catch the occasional breeze.

![Mormon Metalmark (34°16’42.93″ N 117°4’13.536″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8135-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![South of Holcomb Creek, looking north (34°16’27.57″ N 117°5’45.402″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8154-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

From here, the trail ascended to a somewhat flat but heavily fire impacted area with great views north before descending towards Deep Creek via two long switchbacks. I carefully looked at my map. Now was the time to leave the trail and make it to Crab Flats Road. Except the bushes and tree were not as small and sparse as I had believed. I backtracked a little bit towards a hillside with fewer bushes and started uphill. I had to carefully figure out a path through the thorny bushes up the steep hillside. About 100 feet in, I hit a dead end. The only path was to go back down to the west which I reluctantly did. Fortunately, that led me to the bouldery ravine I had expected. From here, it was a steep climb across boulders, but at least the “path” was fairly obvious. Some of the rocks were pretty loose so I had to tread carefully. But after another 5 minutes of scrambling, I made it to Crab Flats Road.

![Looking back east towards Delamar Mountain (34°16’28.098″ N 117°6’36.534″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8163-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Deep Creek and the Splinters Cabin area (34°16’31.266″ N 117°7’18.072″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8180-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The road was really rugged, definitely not bicycle material. But most certainly motorcycle material. Within just 2 minutes, the first group of motorists passed by. Over the short stretch of about a mile, there were more than a dozen enjoying their Sunday with some dirt road motorcycling. It was dusty and the exhaust was unpleasant. The dirt road crossed Deep Creek and then started uphill again. Fortunately, I soon reached North Shore Recreational Trail and left the dirt road behind. The trail was also used by motorcycles as evident by the deep tracks, but at least not as popular as Crab Flats Road. The trail paralleled Bear Creek which was still flowing. And that meant a few crossings over some logs. The creek wasn’t deep, but it still would have meant wet feet. In some areas it was not just wet, but also very muddy, so I tried to find paths across grass that would leave me dry.

![Looking down Little Bear Creek Canyon (34°15’59.76″ N 117°9’17.808″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8198-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail followed Bear Creek Canyon for a bit before heading up the hillside via several switchbacks. The view back down the canyon was great, but I also had to take a few short breaks on the way up to catch my breath. The trail crossed Rouse Ranch Road and eventually reached North Shore Campground at the very end of the ascent. I was glad to be on level ground. I walked through the closed campground and a quarter mile later reached my car.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Dirt and loose rocks. Lightly to moderately overgrown at times, especially around the crossing of Crab Flats Road 3N16.

**Parking:** There is room for two cars at the campground entrance, right next to Hospital Road.

**Weather:** Sunny with blue skies. Partly cloudy past noon. 50°F / 10°C in the morning at the bottom of Coxey Road. High of 93°F / 34°C in the afternoon with an intermittent mild breeze.

**Land Manager & Permits:** San Bernardino National Forest. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.2

Difficulty: Moderate  
Scenery: Moderate  
Logistics: Easy
