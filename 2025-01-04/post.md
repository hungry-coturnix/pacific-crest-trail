# Montezuma Valley Road to Lost Valley Road

Jan 4, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [33.216865, -116.587919](https://maps.app.goo.gl/EuVDW1UNQRB5Wzcw8) (Eagle Rock Trailhead)  
**End:** [33.325990, -116.636930](https://maps.app.goo.gl/jwo4MRjerg6UDYHf8) (Unnamed trailhead on Lost Valley Road)

**Distance:** 17.5 mi (up to PCT mile 117.9, 4.4% completed)  
**Time:** 7 hours 8 minutes (start 6:50am, end 1:58pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 2,580 ft (low 2,899 ft – high 4,072 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 2,035 ft (start 3,457 ft – end 4,002 ft)

---

I woke up at 4:50am before my alarm even went off, full of excitement for the next hike. I hadn’t intended to go come back so soon, but the mountains kept calling me and I couldn’t resist. Leaving the Julian Gold Rush Hotel (a nice historic hotel that I personally couldn’t appreciate as much as I should have) at 5:14am, I headed towards Warner Springs. Turning onto Lost Valley Road, I had worried a bit during planning that I would have to drive up a windy dirt road in the dark, but it turned out to be paved, yet still very windy. I reached my bike drop-off location, an unnamed trailhead along the road at 5:57am. Contrary to my impression from satellite images, there were no trees around, just somewhat bigger shrubs. So I ended up chaining my bike around one of the bigger shrubs, just a bit off the road so that it wouldn’t be visible.

Heading back down Lost Valley Road and through Warner Springs, I arrived at the starting point of my hike, the Eagle Rock Trailhead at 6:34am. It was cold (39°F) but very dry (17.5% humidity), so it fortunately didn’t feel as cold. Putting my gear on and chewing on a piece of bread, I left the car at 6:50am, right at sunrise, although the sun was still hidden behind the mountains and a bit of residual clouds. Passing through a metal gate, I crossed dry Buena Vista Creek and headed for the first ridge. Out of the corner of my eye, I saw movement and when I turned, several bunnies were hopping through the bushes.

The trail contoured the ridge while cattle at the bottom was greeting me with a loud moo. On the north side of the ridge, the trail dropped down into a westerly flat valley, crossed it and headed up into the grassy rolling hills on the other side. Heading into a narrowing canyon after 3 miles, I saw the very first flowing creek since I had started at the Mexican border. San Ysidro Creek was little more than a trickle, but it was flowing. Following the east bank for a bit, I eventually crossed the stream and zigzagged upcanyon. Views west over Warner Valley and to Lake Henshaw were the reward.

![Warner Valley (33°15’10.6631″ N 116°36’18.3278″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/281A4344-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Eagle Rock (33°15’17.2586″ N 116°36’41.0783″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/281A4347-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail headed west across more grassland and passed by Eagle Rock at 5 miles. Eagle Rock is named after a peculiar rock formation that looks like an eagle spreading its wings. This is a sacred site for the Kuupangaxwichem people who still live on the nearby Pala Indian Reservation. The Kupa people gathered plants and hunted near Eagle Rock when preparing for ceremonies, social gatherings, and death rituals.

I had expected to be alone for the day, but on the next 3 miles, between Eagle Rock and Warner Springs, I ended up passing 49 people, including a large group of boy scouts with backpacks. The trail dropped down into a small canyon with Cañada Verde Creek (Spanish for “green ravine”) at the bottom and paralleled the creek for a while. Along the way, I happened upon a lone wild turkey crossing the trail.

After 8 miles, I reached CA-79 next to the Warner Springs Fire Station. Crossing the highway, the trail continued southwest on the other side. I was greeted again by cattle that was roaming this grassland. The unexpected visitor aside from cows was a coyote, likely on the hunt for some food. The trail gradually angled north and headed towards more shady oak trees, passing an abandoned campground, and headed underneath CA-79, crossing dry Agua Caliente Creek at the same time.

![Hiking along Agua Caliente Creek (33°17’40.3411″ N 116°38’37.7292″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/281A4369-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Looking back towards Warner Valley (33°17’45.1818″ N 116°38’8.7238″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/281A4370-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Following the north bank, the trail stayed somewhat level for a while, affording great views of Hot Springs Mountain straight ahead. Just after a nice camping area with a picnic table, the trail crossed (still dry) Agua Caliente Creek and started heading up the ridge. Soon enough, it was dropping back into the canyon towards the creek which was no longer dry further upstream. Crossing the creek several times, I slipped on one of the last crossings and got my feet wet, but fortunately there were only a few miles left back to the bike.

![Leaving shaded Agua Caliente Creek (33°19’11.868″ N 116°37’22.338″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/281A4379-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Climbing out of the Agua Caliente Canyon (33°19’13.242″ N 116°37’24.84″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/281A4383-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail eventually left Agua Caliente Creek and switchbacked up moderately one of the side canyons, revealing great views of the entire canyon, before contouring west towards Lost Valley Road. Using a short spur trail, I left the PCT and reached by bike.

![The Agua Caliente Canyon (33°19’17.8238″ N 116°37’27.4758″ W](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/281A4389-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt and sand, somewhat overgrown along the upper stretches of Agua Caliente Creek.

**Parking:** There is parking for a few cars in front of a metal gate, just off Montezuma Valley Road.

**Weather:** Mostly sunny throughout the day with a high of 76°F (around 1pm, on the north bank of Agua Caliente Creek) and a low of 39°F (early in the morning, at the start of the hike).

**Land Manager & Permits:** Partly Cleveland National Forest. No permits required.

**Overall Rating:** 2.8

Difficulty: Moderate  
Scenery: Medium  
Logistics: Some Challenges

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 15.0 mi (1 hour 32 min), 892 ft elevation gain, 9.78 mph average speed

The bike ride back was the most strenuous I have had so far, mostly because of the elevation gain. The first 4 miles were downhill on paved Lost Valley Road, steep enough to constantly have me on breaks. Turning left onto CA-79, the road started to ascend steadily into Warner Springs, still manageable at a lower speed. From Warner Springs, it was slightly downhill or at least flat until the junction with San Felipe Road. From there, the last 6 miles were uphill by more than 650 feet without any flat stretches. About two thirds in, I had to take a break to drink some water and get some much needed energy (i.e. sugar). Pushing the bike up two of the steeper hills, I eventually made it back to the car at 3:35pm.
