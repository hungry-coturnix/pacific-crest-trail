# Deer Springs Trail to Snow Creek

May 25, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [33.791243, -116.735530](https://maps.app.goo.gl/JtruVyT9uLu7yhBD9) (Marion Mountain Trailhead)  
**End:** [33.893647, -116.683002](https://maps.app.goo.gl/9rnHnUYSJ1NAe1z97) (Snow Creek Canyon Road)

**Distance:** 25.6 mi (up to PCT mile 207.0, 7.8% completed)  
**Time:** 12 hours 31 minutes (start 6:32am, end 7:04pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 5,110 ft (low 1,225 ft – high 8,963 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 10,194 ft (start 6,319 ft – end 1,235 ft)

---

Four weeks had passed since my last hike and I was eager to continue. Two weekends ago, temperatures on San Gorgonio Pass had been upwards of 100°F / 38°C so hiking was not an option. And then my head surgery kept me cautious the weekend after. Technically, I was not supposed to do any strenuous activities for another 4 weeks, but my mental health is suffering if I don’t get out there. And since everything is healing well, I figured I could still go. After the low photo turnout from last time, I was looking forward to a much more picturesque section – and indeed the trail provided.

Today’s hike was again a hike without a bike ride back. This time I was going to take an Uber to the starting point. I had issued myself a day hiking permit at the San Jacinto Ranger Station in Idyllwild the evening before and reserved a ride for 5am from Snow Creek. As usual, I was awake before the alarm at 4:10am, even though I hadn’t slept very well. My hotel room was about 50 feet from the interstate and the noise was giving me a hard time. I got to Snow Creek well before 5am. It was very breezy as I watched the first light appearing in the east.

Unfortunately my Uber driver, Ray, was 24 minutes late. This was not a good start into what was going to be a long day. Ray was not very talkative and seemingly not an experienced driver. What was supposed to be 38 minute ride to the Marion Mountain Trailhead, took over an hour. Ray was driving significantly below speed limit and was overly cautious taking all the curves up to Idyllwild. On top of that, he missed a turn which forced us to drive through Stone Creek Campground.

![Clouds above the Los Angeles metropolitan area (33°48’35.616″ N 116°41’51.258″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/281A5919-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Almost an hour after sunrise, I was finally able to start my hike at 6:32am. The first few miles were a steep ascent on the western slope of Marion Mountain. Although mostly obscured by trees, I could see glimpses of the Los Angeles basin to the west still covered by a layer of morning clouds that would soon blow off. I had my first break right before the junction with the PCT with 2,300 ft of elevation gain already behind me. The first half mile on the PCT was familiar terrain, except that it had all been covered in snow the last time and now it was completely snow free.

![Cabazon from the northern end of Fuller Ridge (33°50’46.92″ N 116°44’16.698″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A5954-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Panorama across Cabazon and San Gorgonio Pass (33°50’47.826″ N 116°44’16.686″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A5957-Pano-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x391.jpg)

I crossed North Fork San Jacinto River which was flowing well where a thru hiker was just filtering water. Up ahead was Fuller Ridge, a spine in the north of the San Jacinto Mountains with plenty of micro switchbacks which made me go down and up repeatedly. Views to the west were now much more open revealing how the morning clouds were steadily retreating. After passing a parking circle at Fuller Ridge Trailhead, the trail crossed Black Mountain Road 4S01 and turned north. Sweeping vistas opened up to the northwest. San Gorgonio Peak was looming in the north. Mount San Antonio was visible in the distance. And more than 6,000 ft below me was San Gorgonio Pass.

![Coachella Valley in the distance (33°51’0.714″ N 116°44’4.182″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A5974-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Sagebrush Lizard (33°51’20.322″ N 116°44’0.732″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A5978-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail switchbacked multiple times, but I knew this was just the start of a long series of switchbacks as part of a very long descent. I had barely reached the halfway point of the hike and the rest of the way was just downhill. At one point I could see the road where I had parked and it felt surreal that I still had 13 miles to go. The landscape started changing and pine trees disappeared. During my lunch break, I watched a hummingbird pass by me.

![Coachella Valley and Snow Creek Canyon (33°51’44.5294″ N 116°42’50.4671″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6034-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![San Gorgonio Mountain in the distance (33°52’2.8456″ N 116°42’52.9495″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6039-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

As my surroundings grew more open, I could hear a loud buzzing from all the pollinator insects feasting on the wildflowers and blooming bushes along the trail. To the southeast was the imposing northern flank of San Jacinto Peak which made for a great scenic background. After rounding another corner shortly before PCT mile marker 194, trees disappeared entirely. Before me was a shrubbery and bouldery landscape exposed to the desert heat. I could see almost all the way down to San Gorgonio Pass. The wind farms north of the interstate were clearly visible in the distance.

![San Jacinto Peak (33°52’0.9241″ N 116°42’48.0584″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6043-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The north face of San Jacinto Peak (33°51’59.544″ N 116°41’39.876″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6070-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The descent was relentless. Miles and miles of downhill, albeit mostly at a fairly gentle gradient. The pass seemed so close and yet was so far. Switchback after switchback grew my desire for this to be over. I encountered my first snake (possibly a rattlesnake) on trail but it slithered away quickly as I got close. San Jacinto Peak as the backdrop became even more imposing as I descended. I reached the 200 mile marker, or rather a set of small rocks someone had laid out to form the digits right next to the trail. This was an important milestone, but somehow my mind was more focused on finishing today’s hike. I could feel two blisters that had started forming on my feet.

![The 200 mile marker (33°52’0.5863″ N 116°41’39.4849″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6071-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Two miles further I met a thru hiker taking a break on a rock next to the trail and sipping a beer. We chatted for a bit. His trail name was Piano Man and he was from Minnesota. He told me how he had slept in the shelter on San Jacinto Peak the night before so that he could witness the sunrise from the peak. I continued on and as I rounded the next corner, now almost in direct line of sight of Snow Creek, the wind hit me with full force. The gusts were very strong and I had to firmly hold on to my hat while also holding up the brim so that I could see at least a few feet ahead on the trail. It was clear why people had built wind farms on San Gorgonio Pass.

![Snow Creek (33°52’55.908″ N 116°41’34.188″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6081-Pano-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x524.jpg)

![Sunset over San Gorgonio Pass (33°52’44.13″ N 116°40’43.614″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6087-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The last major switchback seemed to take forever. I could see my car but it was still more than 4 miles to go. Walking turned into an almost mechanical activity. I just wanted it to be over now. The trail finally dropped to the bottom of the creek and joined a closed paved road. The sun was already low providing me with welcome shade after such a long stretch with full sun exposure. The view was gorgeous as the sun had started setting, yet I couldn’t appreciate it as much as I wanted. I made it back to my car shortly after 7pm.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt and rocks. Some downed trees along Fuller Ridge. Slightly overgrown at times during the descent to Snow Creek.

**Parking:** There is plenty of space along Snow Creek Canyon Road.

**Weather:** Sunny with blue skies. Breezy for the last 10 miles towards Snow Creek with very strong gusts. Low temperature of 54°F / 12°C in the morning on Marion Mountain Trail shortly before the junction with the PCT. High temperature of 93°F / 34°C at 4:20pm during the descent to Snow Creek.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Santa Rosa and San Jacinto Mountains National Monument. San Bernardino National Forest. Day use permit required for San Jacinto Wilderness (self issued at San Jacinto Ranger Station in Idyllwild).

**Overall Rating:** 3.6

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Superb  
Logistics: Easy
