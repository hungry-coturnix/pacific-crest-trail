# Cleghorn Road to Swarthout Canyon Road

Oct 25, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.289072, -117.366064](https://maps.app.goo.gl/QvmsanjFdMHWAq9g8) (Cleghorn Road, north of group campground)  
**End:** [34.301374, -117.511362](https://maps.app.goo.gl/5Hxmuq43FMW2AzHw5) (Swarthout Canyon Rd & Lone Pine Canyon Rd)

**Distance:** 18.4 mi (up to PCT mile 347.3, 13.1% completed)  
**Time:** 7 hours 49 minutes (start 7:16am, end 3:05pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 7,284 ft (low 2,936 ft – high 4,194 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 7,134 ft (start 3,524 ft – end 3,674 ft)

---

I had expected today’s section to be a bit boring – crossing an interstate and some railroad tracks didn’t seem too exciting – but I was wrong. The views today across Cajon Canyon were spectacular.

![Watching San Jacinto Peak while waiting for my Uber (34°18’4.2039″ N 117°30’39.6027″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8449-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The area still had Uber coverage, so I had parked at the end of today’s section, just off Lone Pine Canyon Road, about half a mile from the trail. It was 20 minutes before sunrise and I could see San Jacinto Peak in the distance with an orange aura. This is where I had been 6 months ago. My driver Adrian was on time and we were back at Silverwood Lake in a mere 15 minutes. He dropped me off right at the intersection of Cleghorn Road with the PCT. I had expected it to be cooler in the morning, but it was already 62°F / 17°C. The sun was slowly coming up in the east, casting shadows across the valley.

![Silverwood Lake from Cleghorn Ridge (34°17’41.088″ N 117°22’41.028″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8473-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Little Horsethief Canyon (34°18’48.408″ N 117°24’26.04″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8499-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Approaching Cajon Canyon (34°19’2.8957″ N 117°27’6.5529″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8568-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail made its way up to Cleghorn Ridge, moving into every gully there is, before crossing the ridge road and descending on the other side towards Little Horsethief Canyon. The last view towards Silverwood Lake was great, but the sun was so low that I couldn’t take any good photo. After crossing several small streams with a trickle of water, the trail dropped into the dry bed of the upper canyon, crossed it, and ascended again on the other side. The stream bed was lined with amazing fall foliage. For the next few miles, the trail kept on ascending gently west up another draw, then crossed Cleghorn Road 3N22 and a giant power transmission line. A quarter mile further, I suddenly reached a steep cliff and was rewarded with a spectacular view of the badlands like landscape across Cajon Canyon. Beneath me was the wide canyon, intersected by Interstate 15 and several railroad tracks (only the third since I left the Mexican border). Beyond were Ralston Peak and the Upper Lytle Creek Ridge. And even further beyond I could see snow covered Mount San Antonio (aka Mount Baldy) and Pine Mountain.

![Panorama across Cajon Canyon with Ralston Peak and snow capped Mount Baldy in the distance (34°19’0.9985″ N 117°27’9.118″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8572-Pano-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x396.jpg)

![Crowder Canyon (34°18’28.6355″ N 117°27’45.4545″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8593-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Emerging from Crowder Canyon onto Interstate 15 (34°18’23.1424″ N 117°27’53.9099″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8594-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I enjoyed this breathtaking view for a bit before I continued down to this landscape. The area had been burned during the 2016 Blue Cut Fire and there was no shade and nothing higher than a few low bushes. It wasn’t too hot, but the sun was intense. A mild breeze was ever present making the descent more bearable. Several trains were passing up and down and their air horns resonated across the canyon. Two miles further, the PCT entered Crowder Canyon, a deeply incised canyon that also seemed to act like a wind tunnel. The canyon took several turns left and right before opening up abruptly at Interstate 15. Half a mile up a paved side road is a famous McDonalds that most PCT hikers seem to crave. Having all the food I needed, I continued. This marked the end of the California Section C of the PCT – 132.5 miles across the San Bernardino Mountains from Interstate 10 to Interstate 15. Section D would take me across the San Gabriel Mountains all the way to Agua Dulce, north of Highway 14.

![Crossing railroad tracks west of Interstate 15 (34°17’50.328″ N 117°28’8.268″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8618-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![A train making its way down Cajon Canyon (34°17’47.76″ N 117°28’16.152″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8628-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Ralston Peak (34°17’24.216″ N 117°28’42.75″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8644-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The way across the interstate was through an enormous boxed culvert that was curving underneath. Because it was curved, I couldn’t initially see its end, so it felt like entering a cave. Fortunately, it was mostly dry, otherwise I would have got wet feet. When I emerged on the other side, there was a thicket of dry brush with a small stream in a sandy jeep track. I hopped across a few rocks, then paralleled elevated railroad tracks before crossing underneath a trestle. The PCT winded through the rocks and hills, then crossed another set of railroad tracks through a round culvert. Following two switchbacks, the last railroad crossing was on the surface. I had seen a stopped train in close proximity not long ago and was hoping for it to pass so that I could take a photo, but after waiting in vain for 10 minutes, I continued.

![Lone Pine Canyon (34°17’29.322″ N 117°29’10.29″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8662-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![A dry creek bed in Lone Pine Canyon (34°17’51.972″ N 117°30’13.596″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8681-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![A water cache near Swarthout Canyon Road (34°17’48.852″ N 117°30’25.392″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8686-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The PCT meandered through the sandstone outcrops of Mormon Rocks, a remnant of the Miocene, crossed several dirt roads, and ascended to a ridge south of Ralston Peak. I could see Cajon Canyon from the western angle now. On the other side was Lone Pine Canyon, leading all the way up to Wrightwood. The trail followed the base of Ralston Peak, then dropped into a wide dry creek bed before reaching Swarthout Canyon Road. This is where I left the PCT and turned north for half a mile to get back to my car.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt. The area between Silverwood Lake and Cajon Pass burned during the 2003 Old Fire and has somewhat recovered, but doesn’t offer much shade. West of Cajon Pass, the area burned multiple times over the last two decades, during the 2016 Blue Cut Fire and the 2002 Blue Cut / Louisiana Fire. It also burned again west of Ralston Peak during the 2009 Sheep Fire. Little shade throughout the entire hike.

**Parking:** There is room for several cars 50 yards from Lone Pine Road at a very trashy turnout.

**Crowds:** Fairly solitary. Met 15 people on trail, 1 trail runner near Silverwood Lake and 14 southbound thru hikers throughout the day.

**Weather:** Sunny with blue skies and no clouds. Low of 62°F / 17°C in the morning at the beginning of the hike. High of 81°F / 27°C in the afternoon.

**Land Manager & Permits:** San Bernardino National Forest and Silverwood Lake State Recreation Area. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.8

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Very Easy
