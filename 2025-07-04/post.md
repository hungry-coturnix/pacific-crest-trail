# Mission Creek to Mission Springs Trail Camp

Jul 4, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.000705, -116.612427](https://maps.app.goo.gl/f5x8DXBymwJMFYJB6) (Mission Creek Trailhead)  
**End:** [34.161872, -116.784984](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Sn9WNVZwbZvP7s3x8) (Heart Bar Campground)

**Distance:** 25.6 mi (up to PCT mile 239.9, 9.1% completed)  
**Time:** 13 hours 21 minutes (start 6:55am, end 8:16pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 7,710 ft (low 2,144 ft – high 8,143 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 2,992 ft (start 2,144 ft – end 6,862 ft)

---

Every section of the Pacific Crest Trail comes with its own unique challenges. This time it was logistics and trail conditions. And in hindsight it was a bit of a temperature challenge as well. I knew it was going to be hot in the lower stretches of Mission Creek, but I was somewhat underprepared for the relentlessly shadeless hike up the rocky river bed for hours on end.

But let’s start at the beginning. The area of today’s hike was severely impacted by Hurricane Hillary which in August 2023 dumped a year’s worth of rain within a matter of hours causing widespread flooding and mudslides. As a result, Mission Creek had been a raging torrent wiping out significant portions of the PCT. I had already got a taste of that last time hiking from the PCT to Mission Creek Preserve. And about 20 miles of today’s hike was up Mission Creek. So I had mentally prepared for a rocky hike. But the reality was still a little different than I had anticipated.

The other aftermath of Hurricane Hillary was that the entire area between the PCT and Highway 38 was still closed. When I had initially planned this segment, I had assumed I could take a dirt road all the way up to the PCT and drop off my bike there. That was clearly no longer an option. Based on street view imagery, I figured that the metal gate just 50 yards off the highway would likely be as far as I could drive. Fortunately, there was enough room to park right next to the gate. I just had to be lucky that nobody else would have the same idea on the same day (but maybe that was a stupid thought?).

Parking close to the highway meant that my hike would be a few miles longer than I had originally planned, but it was still within day hiking limits. On the upside, I was going to take Uber for the entire shuttle ride instead of doing a combination of bike and Uber. The road path from finish to start was 67 miles and would not have been bikeable. I had reserved an Uber for 5:30am. My only worry was not having cellphone reception. So I had still brought my bike as a contingency in case the Uber driver would not show up. When he arrived at 5:34am I was relieved.

![Mission Creek Road (34°0’9.2072″ N 116°36’55.7031″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6399-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![One of the short dirt road sections that did not wash out (34°2’2.9928″ N 116°38’26.0448″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6413-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The ride to Mission Creek Preserve took 1 hour and 22 minutes and I was at the gate starting the hike at 6:55am. No cars in the parking lot which I guess was not surprising given the temperatures. Even this early in the day, it was already 83°F / 28°C. The first 2 miles were the familiar dirt road past a historic stone house and a picnic area. Then the dirt road paralleled the river bed for a while before being washed out. I had found a suitable path last time and retraced my exact steps to reach the PCT again at 4.7 miles. I rejoiced and was looking forward to the next section.

I was still hiking a trail for now. I took my first break soon after rejoining the PCT, squeezing into the little shade that one of the willows in the river bed was affording me. Even though I had to cross the rocky river bed a few times, my pace was good. But that was about to change. On the other side of one of the river crossings the trail was just gone. The entire river bend had been washed away. So I had to continue within the river bed which was quite arduous. Over the next miles, there was little trail. Sometimes it was still visible, but now on top of a 20 feet dirt wall that I had no means to get to. The river bed alternated between large boulders and smaller rocks, the latter being a bit easier to navigate.

![The first crossing of Mission Creek (34°2’36.678″ N 116°39’25.086″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6420-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Yet another crossing, almost 5 miles later (34°4’40.074″ N 116°40’13.53″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6444-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

In some places, the river was flowing through a narrow gorge incised in granite gneiss which was impressive to see. Other than that, the scenery did not change much for hours. Bend after bend in the river. Crossing after crossing. Fortunately, there was not that much water flowing anymore, so I never got my feet wet. At about 12 miles, shortly after Forks Springs, I stopped for my lunch break. Given the heat of the day, I had brought a water filter and filtered a fresh gallon (4 liters). I had completely drunk all the water I had taken with me. For food, I took out my little stove and cooked water under a lone birch tree in the middle of the river bed that was providing me with a little bit of shade. I had brought mushroom risotto which took 20 minutes to rehydrate with the boiling water. The taste was amazing!

![Lunch! (34°5’55.794″ N 116°40’47.742″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6449-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Dry Mission Creek (34°7’1.8058″ N 116°41’35.3827″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6454-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I continued on. More river bed with a few trail segments. The lack of any shade was torturing. My Kestrel was indicating that it was still well above 90°F / 32°C. A slight breeze made it more bearable. Mission Creek was dry now. At about 14 miles, I finally reached an actual trail section that would turn out to take me a long way. I was glad to be off the rocky river bed. At 15 miles, the trail moved into a side canyon, then crossed a dry tributary and began ascending the hillside in switchbacks. The lower portion of that had the first real shade in hours. The trail reached a promontory and stayed high up above Mission Creek for the rest of the hike following the contours of the canyon while continuously gaining elevation.

![More trees up the side canyon (34°7’36.906″ N 116°42’43.212″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6465-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

At 19 miles, the trail moved back into the Mission Creek bed. It was much narrower than in its lower stretched with high walls on either side. And water was flowing again, though barely a trickle. The old dirt road on the north side of the creek was completely washed away. I carefully navigated the creek bed for a while before I followed a few cairns that led me up the slope to rejoin the official trail. The area was forested and there were wildflowers along the trail. Some sections were even heavily overgrown with lupine in full bloom. The temperatures had dropped quite a bit which I was very grateful for. My strength was slowly diminishing – not a surprise after more than 7,000 feet uphill over 10 hours.

![A look back down North Fork Mission Creek (34°7’42.948″ N 116°43’56.922″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6482-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I reached Mission Springs Trail Camp, a small flat spot that hadn’t be in use for a while. Shortly after, I left the PCT via Fish Creek Meadows Road, the dirt road I had originally thought to take to drop off my bike. Up here, it was still in fairly good condition. Just half a mile later, I turned right onto Bean Flat Spur, also an old dirt road, but in much worse shape, very rugged and washed out in a few places. On the upside, it contoured the north side of Mission Creek Canyon offering some great views down the long canyon I had just climbed up from. Past a saddle, the dirt road started its long descent. Initially in fairly good conditions, just covered in a lot of pine cones, the road soon became very washed out, turning the hike into a boulder scramble through a dry creek bed. Fortunately, the road turned north half a mile later and was a lot less washed out, making it a somewhat more enjoyable experience.

![A final look down Mission Creek from the high point of the hike (34°7’46.5975″ N 116°45’5.4635″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6492-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Sunset over San Bernardino National Forest (34°8’35.694″ N 116°45’59.178″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/281A6500-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The sun was setting, painting the pine trees in yellow hues and it was getting a bit chilly. I eventually reached the junction with Fish Creek Meadows Road which was also the intersection with the Santa Ana River. What I assume had once been a tame small creek was now a washed out river bed 60 feet wide. Nature’s force had been unstoppable. Soon after, I hit Cook Creek Jumpoff, the wide dirt road connecting all the campgrounds in the area. It soon became paved, passing Heart Bar Campground. Rounding the last corner, I could see half the asphalt broken away by the force of Cienaga Seca Creek. On the other side was the gate with my car waiting.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Very poor. Large sections of the trail have been completely washed out forcing most of the hike into the rocky river bed. Only sections of the trail along Mission Creek remain, but cannot always be reached. Near the headwaters of Mission Creek, the trail is quite overgrown with lupine in full bloom.

**Parking:** There is room for two or three cars right in front of the road gate to Heart Bar Campground.

**Weather:** Sunny with blue skies and no clouds. 83°F / 28°C at the beginning of the hike, with temperatures in the sun exceeding 110°F / 43°C around 10am. Temperatures dropping with elevation to 72°F / 22°C at the high point of the hike and 62°F / 17°C at the end after sunset.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Sand to Snow National Monument, including a significant portion within San Gorgonio Wilderness. San Bernardino National Forest. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 2.0

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Low  
Logistics: Easy
