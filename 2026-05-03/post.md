# CA-58 to South Kelso Valley Road (Day 2)

May 3, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [35.225799, -118.234563](https://maps.app.goo.gl/LmuNzqvvfnvZ5gyE6) (Golden Oaks Spring)  
**End:** [35.387027, -118.301717](https://maps.app.goo.gl/GMQh9k2kVq5jnGxA7) (Robin Bird Spring)

**Distance:** 21.6 mi (up to PCT mile 603.2, 22.8% completed)  
**Time:** 10 hours 45 minutes (start 7:12am, end 5:58pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 3,837 ft (low 4,692 ft – high 6,329 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 2,995 ft (start 5,474 ft – end 6,316 ft)

---

The night had been pretty decent. I had woken up twice to pee, but I felt pretty well rested when I woke up shortly before 6am. Breakfast was [Peak Refuel Mountain Berry Granola](https://peakrefuel.com/products/mountain-berry-granola) and a cup of hot Jasmine tea. It had only cooled down to 56°F / 13°C over night but the hot tea felt good nonetheless. Some of the thru hikers were already gone when I got out of my tent. I took my time to pack up and left at 7:12am. There were only four thru hikers left and we ended up leap frogging each other throughout the day. The weather was great again, mostly blue skies with very few clouds. It was another very dry stretch of the Pacific Crest Trail. The only water source was Robin Bird Spring, 20 miles away and also my destination for the night. I was carrying almost a full gallon / 3.5 liters.

![Shortly after sunrise at Golden Oaks Spring (35°13’33.3878″ N 118°14’2.6511″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2205-1024x683.jpg)

![Wind turbines north of Cache Peak (35°15’42.5015″ N 118°15’7.3333″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2234-1024x683.jpg)

The PCT headed north again, the crossed a service road with access to more wind turbines. To the south was impressive Cache Peak across from a canyon with Indian Creek at the bottom. The trail passed a ridge and descended again with more views of a human reinforced hillside for the service road I had crossed earlier. The vegetation around me changed as I entered the burn scar of the 2012 Jawbone Complex Fire. It was fairly sun exposed but with great views to the west down unnamed canyons. Rounding a few more gullies, the trail crossed over to the north side of the ridge after passing a gate. I could see all of South Kelso Valley now. Lots of wind turbines were far in the east and I was also seeing my destination for tomorrow in the far distance.

![Indian Creek drainage (35°16’5.273″ N 118°15’14.0919″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2241-1024x683.jpg)

![Expansive views of Kelso Valley (35°16’51.7899″ N 118°15’25.3827″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2255-Pano-1024x554.jpg)

Two miles later, I decided it was time for lunch. Only there was not much shade to be found. So I crouched under a California Flannelbush to get at least a tiny bit of shade. The PCT rounded another knob and descended to cross Back Canyon Road, then headed uphill again past a radio tower. A thru hiker had caught up to me and we walked together for a bit. His trail name was Noodle (because he used to cold soak noodles every day during his early thru hiking days, as I learned later). He was from Essex, England, and this was already his third thru hike after hiking in New Zealand and the French Pyrenees. He curiously asked about my PCT adventure and I shared some of my experiences from the last year.

![Western Fence Lizard (35°17’4.9548″ N 118°16’2.0455″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2272-1024x683.jpg)

![Bush Poppy (35°17’31.2311″ N 118°16’14.833″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2281-1024x683.jpg)

The PCT headed more steeply uphill before dropping to Hamp Williams Pass. I was back in pine trees. There was a trail maintenance crew of 6 people at the pass who were debating how to best tackle a large tree that had fallen across the trail. They had a huge saw with them! The trail headed uphill again with several shorter switchbacks, then rounded an unnamed peak and headed west. Hugh Mann Canyon and Hart Canyon were to the left. The air was a bit hazy now and it even smelled like smoke. Maybe another wildfire had started already. Passing Weldon Peak on the right, I reached another milestone: 600 miles on the Pacific Crest Trail. I didn’t stop because I was very tired already. The constant up and down was exhausting and I knew I had one long final ascent ahead of me. All of my water was already gone. But being above 6,000 feet also meant that it was pleasantly cool.

![Approaching Back Canyon Road (35°17’51.9″ N 118°15’53.544″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2293-1024x683.jpg)

![View from the Piute Mountains east across Kelso Valley (35°18’58.5611″ N 118°16’6.6668″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2308-Pano-1024x494.jpg)

I reached a rough dirt road which the PCT followed for a little over a mile. It was very steep at times and really taking the last bit of strength out of me. Turning onto regular trail tread again, the trail leveled a bit more and then officially entered Sequoia National Forest. The last mile required a lot of will power. The tendons above my right ankle were also giving me a hard time. I finally reached the spur trail to Robin Bird Spring and turned left. 100 yards later I reached the spring and camping area. There was a pipe sticking out of the ground with a good water flow. Two reasonably flat tent spots were nearby and still empty. 30 yards further was another larger flat area where the thru hikers had already set up their tents. I wanted a bit of solitude today and stayed at the earlier spot. After putting my hoodie and base layer out to dry, I set up my tent and made dinner. The [Good-to-Go Cuban Rice Bowl](https://goodto-go.com/products/cuban-rice-bowl) with the remaining bacon bits from the prior day was delicious. The temperature had dropped to 54°F / 12°C already and it was a bit windy as well. The thru hikers had been joined by two section hikers who had brought a skillet and were making fresh quesadillas. I was very impressed. They had also brought a ukulele and were playing a bit of music. I retreated to my tent before 8pm and ended up falling asleep early again.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt, occasionally somewhat overgrown. Fairly exposed to the sun with little shade. Lots of wind turbines for the first few miles. Some impact from the 2012 Jawbone Complex Fire.

**Parking:** There is room for 3 cars just off South Kelso Valley Road.

**Crowds:** Mostly solitude. Leap frogged with four thru hikers. Met a trail maintenance crew of six at Hamp Williams Pass.

**Weather:** Sunny throughout the entire hike with a light intermittent breeze. Low of 54°F / 12°C in the morning at the start of the hike. High of 72°F / 22°C around 1pm south of Back Canyon Road.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Some private land. Some land managed by the Bureau of Land Management. A short stretch within Sequoia National Forest. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.3

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Moderate  
Logistics: Moderate
