# Lake Morena County Park to Mount Laguna

Nov 28, 2024 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [32.682581, -116.516672](https://maps.app.goo.gl/BenY4pFoVgkuwQxY6) (just outside of Lake Morena County Park)  
**End:** [32.857227, -116.423589](https://maps.app.goo.gl/f4WuCo9RfesLpSYn9) (Red-Tailed Roost Trailhead)

**Distance:** 21.2 mi (up to PCT mile 40.8, 1.5% completed)  
**Time:** 9 hours 2 minutes (start 6:34am, end 3:37pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 4,625 ft (low 2,914 ft – high 6,006 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 1,777 ft (start 3,074 ft – end 5,922 ft)

---

The days are short in late fall. I knew I had to time things perfectly to make the most of the daylight hours. That meant getting up at 5am and dropping off my bike in the dark. I arrived at Lake Morena County Park a few minutes before sunrise. Things were off to a great start.

I left my car at 6:34am. The campground just west of me was still very quiet, nobody stirring. Just about half a mile into the hike Lake Morena appeared on the horizon, but it was covered by a thick layer of fog that I knew the morning sun would burn off quickly. Nevertheless, it was a beautiful sight. As the trail turned eastward, the sun started peeking over the horizon, painting the cirrus clouds in orange and pink hues. I felt like the king of the world and lucky to be witnessing this magical moment.

![Fog covered Lake Morena with Morena Butte in the distance (32°41’32.91″ N 116°30’8.202″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A3940-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Cottonwood Valley (32°42’45.384″ N 116°29’49.398″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A3961-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail went up a little butte just north of Morena Village. By the time I reached the top, the sun had already begun its work. Morena Butte, the highest point on my last hike, was prominently visible in the southwest. Dropping back down towards Buckman Springs Road, the trail crossed the dry Cottonwood Creek and headed north paralleling the road. At 5 miles, the trail turned east again towards Boulder Oaks. The second crossing of Cottonwood Creek was not so dry, a little pool of water had accumulated from the trickling stream, but a few strategically positioned logs by thoughtful hikers made this no obstacle. A fence to the north kept prominently saying that trespassing was forbidden by law, but the sheer amount of signs was surprising.

The Boulder Oaks campground was almost deserted, maybe not unexpected given that it was Thanksgiving Day after all. The camp host’s trailer was still there, but otherwise there was nobody in sight. Crossing Old Highway 80 my surroundings were covered in beautiful foliage from cottonwood trees. The leaves seemed to be at their prime, all yellow and orange.

![Crossing Old Highway 80 (32°43’55.068″ N 116°28’59.952″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A3965-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Cottonwood foliage on the way to Kitchen Creek (32°43’59.118″ N 116°28’42.372″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A3970-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I passed below Interstate 8 which marked the beginning of a long ascent. As I kept gaining altitude, views of Cottonwood Valley opened up with the Interstate in the foreground. The trail paralleled dry Kitchen Creek and then crossed Kitchen Creek Road. Continuing on the other side, the views towards the southeast were just stunning. You could still see all the way into Mexico.

![Cottonwood Valley across Interstate 8 (32°43’55.35″ N 116°28’14.178″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A3972-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![View south after passing Kitchen Creek Road (32°44’47.25″ N 116°26’58.356″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A3987-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail passed a saddle and headed into Fred Canyon. At 12.5 miles a scary sign just before Fred Canyon Road warned of unexploded military ordnances. Zigzagging up the hill to the north, I got good views of Cibbets Flat Campground and Kitchen Creek Road. At 17 miles, the trail descended into Long Canyon, another oasis of colorful cottonwood trees.

![Unexploded Military Ordinance near Fred Canyon Road (32°46’18.99″ N 116°26’20.358″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A3996-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Cibbets Flat Campground (32°46’48.306″ N 116°26’14.274″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4003-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

About a mile further, the scenery changed dramatically. Up to this point, the mountains had been covered in shrubs and grasses. Reaching the higher elevations of Mount Laguna, everything abruptly turned into Jeffrey pines. And along with it, the temperature dropped by 20°F. Leaving the PCT and following Desert View Road, I reached the Red-Tailed Roost Trailhead at 3:37pm. I had not met a single person throughout the entire day.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt, with some sandy stretches and some section with lots of loose rocks.

**Parking:** There is plenty of parking space just outside of Lake Morena County Park.

**Weather:** Mostly sunny, with a high of 80°F (during the ascent north of Fred Canyon Road) and a low of 44°F (early in the morning, close to Lake Morena).

**Land Manager & Permits:** Mostly Cleveland National Forest. No permits required.

**Overall rating:** 3.0

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Medium  
Logistics: Easy

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 20.1 mi (1 hour 7 min), 378 ft elevation gain, 17.78 mph average

The bike ride back was initially easier than I had expected. It was a long way down the Sunrise Highway back to Interstate 8 and then further down on Old Highway 80. Only the last stretch back to Morena Village would be uphill. What I had not adequately prepared for though, was the temperature drop. It was already 45°F when I left Red-Tailed Roost with the sun just dipping below the horizon. I had not brought my jacket, so I was only wearing my (sweaty) hiking hoodie. Zipping down the highway at speeds of more than 30 mph made it materially worse. As I felt the numbing cold seep through, I thought to myself that an uphill stretch would be nice to get me moving a bit.

Fortunately there was almost no traffic anymore and with my downhill speed, the miles kept passing quickly. The last 2 miles were most of the uphill, initially somewhat gentle, but turning off Buckman Springs Road towards Morena Village, I had to shift into the lowest gear to make it up that last steep hill. It took everything out of me. The last stretch was downhill again and I made it make to my car at 4:52pm, just 13 minutes past sunset. I was incredibly glad to be back and just being stationary immediately warmed me up.
