# Lake Hughes Road to Liebre Sawmill Road

Feb 14, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.659683, -118.460448](https://maps.app.goo.gl/s5KXZc5AHBFqzkKG9) (Lake Hughes Road)  
**End:** [34.703127, -118.527747](https://maps.app.goo.gl/nJ1gs1T8hQF6reAi9) (Pine Canyon Road at Bushnell Summit)

**Distance:** 16.2 mi (up to PCT mile 496.2, 18.7% completed)  
**Time:** 6 hours 10 minutes (start 7:03am, end 1:14pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 4,620 ft (low 3,058 ft – high 5,309 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 3,583 ft (start 3,058 ft – end 4,095 ft)

---

Originally, I had planned to hike from Lake Hughes Road all the way to Liebre Mountain Trailhead – a long 24.3 miles. However, upon revisiting the elevation profile of the bike ride – 15 miles with more than 1,000 feet of elevation gain – I figured it would be better to split this section. Leaving the PCT at Liebre Sawmill Road and descending to Bushnell Summit on Pine Canyon Road was the best option. And it would give me a bike ride back that was almost entirely downhill.

![Sunrise over the switchbacks on the eastern side of Sawmill Mountain (34°39’38.322″ N 118°27’51.258″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0208-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The Tehachapi Mountains in the distance (34°40’12.42″ N 118°28’17.826″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0214-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I stayed in Frazier Park near Interstate 5, the closest I had ever been to home. Leaving the hotel at 6:06am, it was only half an hour to Bushnell Summit. I had scouted the location on Google Maps Street View and I knew there would be two signs I could chain the bike to. When I reached the PCT intersection with Lake Hughes Road, it was 6:53am and the sun had already come up and was painting the top of the hills golden. It was very cold. The car thermometer showed 31°F / -1°C. And indeed the ground on the trail was frozen. The first 3 miles were only uphill and a steady pace kept me warm. The PCT switchbacked and passed the mouth of an old graphite mine tunnel that was barely large enough for a person to squeeze through. Grass Mountain was in the distance to the east. When I finally reached the top of the ridge, just below Maxwell Road, the views south along Deer Canyon were stunning.

![The San Andreas Rift Zone with Lake Hughes and Elizabeth Lake in the distance (34°40’26.868″ N 118°28’55.128″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0227-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Seemingly endless ridges to the south of Sawmill Mountain (34°40’40.392″ N 118°29’32.928″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0243-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Passing Boy Scout Trail Camp, the PCT crossed Maxwell Road and headed to the northern side of Sawmill Mountain. On a flat spot nearby, two backpackers were just packing up their tent. The views to the north were familiar now, I could see Portal Ridge across Pine Canyon and the San Andreas Rift Zone. In the distance were the Tehachapi Mountains and the Mojave Desert. The area from here to the end of the hike had burned during the 2020 Lake Fire. As a result, there were numerous ghostly trees and plenty of pungent poodle dog bush. I carefully avoided it. A breeze had picked up and made me feel a little chilly. I sat down for a break in a wind covered ravine. The two backpackers passed me. Two miles further, I passed through what used to be Maxwell Trail Camp and had now turned into a forest of poodle dog bush, six feet tall and dense.

![Eerie Upper Shake Campground (34°41’28.56″ N 118°31’47.826″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0261-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The burn scars from the 2020 Lake Fire (34°41’13.146″ N 118°32’41.988″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0267-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

A little further was the intersection with a trail leading down to Upper Shake Campground. Since today’s hike was shorter, I decided to take the detour and explore the campground. It had been closed in 2002 after the Copper Fire to allow for the landscape to recover. Now it was feeling abandoned and eerie. The picnic tables and some of the information boards were still standing. But nature had started reclaiming the area, poodle dog bush was growing through the asphalt. I had another break before I made my way back up to the PCT and continued west. The next mile was spared by fires with plenty of oak trees still shading the hillside. Just before reaching Upper Shake Trail, the burn scar continued. The upside was a number of great views north.

![Burnt Peak from a ridgetop junction (34°41’29.106″ N 118°33’16.998″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0274-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Pine Canyon (34°42’12.216″ N 118°32’2.712″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0284-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I reached a large flat ridgetop junction with 5 dirt roads. This was the high point and also the end of today’s PCT stretch. I took Liebre Sawmill Road to my right, a well graded and maintained dirt road. The remaining 3 miles were steadily downhill with good views north and down Pine Canyon. I passed the two backpackers from earlier again and this time we chatted for a bit. The woman was from San Diego and was also day hiking the PCT. She had started in 2020 though and was splitting the trail into much shorter sections, with more backpacking required. She also had significant help from her husband and friends with company and shuttle rides. We reached Bushnell Summit together where my bike was waiting for me.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt, fairly well maintained. Slightly overgrown at times. A few blowdowns. Lots of Poodle Dog Bush. Most of the area was impacted significantly by the 2020 Lake Fire.

**Parking:** There is room for cars along both sides of Lake Hughes Road.

**Crowds:** Almost complete solitude. Only met a couple that was camping near Boy Scout Trail Camp.

**Weather:** Mostly sunny throughout the entire day with a thin layer of cirrus clouds. Moderate to strong breeze. Low of 31°F / -1°C in the morning at the start of the hike. High of 60°F / 16°C during the descent to Bushnell Summit.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Angeles National Forest. Annual Interagency Permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.6

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Moderate  
Logistics: Very Easy

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 6.2 mi (19 min), 17 ft elevation gain, 19.6 mph average speed, 31.1 mph maximum speed

The bike ride back was very easy. It was paved all the way and except for a small dip completely downhill. The only thing I had not accounted for was the strong wind. It was blowing from the southeast and slowing my momentum to a point where the wind gusts made me somewhat unsteady. It also made me pretty cold despite another jacket and bike gloves. I reached Lake Hughes and turned onto Lake Hughes Road. From here it was another 2 miles back to my car.
