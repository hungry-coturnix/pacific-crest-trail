# Indian Canyon Trailhead to Bouquet Canyon Road

Jan 25, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.436850, -118.273192](https://maps.app.goo.gl/p2Krm6AD5M1yVA9y5) (Indian Canyon Trailhead)  
**End:** [34.583647, -118.321356](https://maps.app.goo.gl/2VGCCL4ZBf6sJmV27) (Bouquet Canyon Road)

**Distance:** 21.6 mi (up to PCT mile 465.5, 17.6% completed)  
**Time:** 8 hours 34 minutes (start 7:12am, end 3:46pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 4,532 ft (low 2,216 ft – high 4,611 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 3,476 ft (start 2,278 ft – end 3,334 ft)

---

Most of the United States was battling arctic temperatures and snow storms, but Southern California was dry and warm giving me the opportunity to hike another section of the PCT. The logistics were still easy. With Palmdale close by and no road closures in the way, there was good Uber coverage. I had left the hotel at 6:02am and arrived at a large pullout along Bouquet Canyon Road at 6:24am. The thermometer in the car said it was 23°F / -5°C, but the air was very dry, so it didn’t feel that cold. My Uber driver Alex was a bit early picking me up in his Tesla. He was going to school in Berkeley and pretty chatty. We talked about my hiking adventures, how bad drivers were in Southern California vs. Northern California, and the illusion of the American Dream. The ride took 35 minutes. When he dropped me off at Indian Canyon Trailhead, the sun had started peeking over the hilltops.

![Railway tracks in Soledad Canyon (34°26’24.2161″ N 118°16’14.9991″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A9925-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![PCT Completion Marker (34°26’24.5943″ N 118°16’15.0135″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A9928-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I left the familiar trailhead, crossed Soledad Canyon Road, and descended down towards the Santa Clara River bed. Passing the KOA Campground, the “river” had very much reduced to a small creek, but it was still about 8 feet wide. A number of logs across the water made it seem like crossing would be straightforward. But alas the logs were unstable and within seconds I found myself knee deep in the cold and swiftly flowing water. Not the best start into the hike! My feet soaking wet, I crossed another rivulet and scrambled up the washed out river bank to reach a Metrolink railroad track. This was only the fourth set of railroad tracks since I had left the Mexican border. A stone memorial on the other side indicated the completion of this section in 1993. From here, it was moderately uphill to a prominent ridge ahead. The sun was still low and painting the hills yellow.

![California Bluebell (34°27’16.494″ N 118°16’8.052″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A9941-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The rolling hills north of Soledad Canyon (34°27’45.498″ N 118°16’55.032″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A9946-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The next few miles were slow. The trail followed the contour lines of the hills and moved into every nook and cranny while steadily gaining elevation. Crossing a minor ridge, the PCT headed west, crossed a seasonal stream in Bobcat Canyon and headed up to the next ridge, north of Three Sisters Rock. Past the ridge, the next two miles were downhill. Thin cirrus clouds dimmed the sunlight. In the distance I could see and hear highway 14. The trail entered Escondido Canyon and turned west again, following the seasonal stream at the bottom of the canyon, and eventually reached a 10-foot-high tunnel crossing underneath the highway. The tunnel was maybe 150 yards long and quite dark in the middle. Fortunately, there was not much water flowing. The trail continued downcanyon on the other side and entered Vasquez Rocks Natural Area. The Vasquez Formation is a conglomerate of igneous and metamorphic rocks set in pink siltstone. The canyon narrowed with impressive multi-layer overhangs on both sides. After crossing the stream a few more times, the PCT turned sharply northeast and headed for the north rim of Escondido Canyon.

![The tunnel underneath highway 14 (34°28’31.296″ N 118°18’16.584″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A9955-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The famous Vasquez Rocks (34°28’58.992″ N 118°18’33.522″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A9964-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Up here it was flat and to the north were the prominent Vasquez Rocks, the hideout of the infamous bandit Tiburcio Vásquez in the 1850s. These rocks have been the scenic backdrop of dozens of movies and TV shows, from Star Trek and Bonanza to MacGyver and The Twilight Zone. The area was busy with lots of kids moving around. The PCT headed up the northern cliff band and descended on the other side before reaching the park entrance at Escondido Canyon Road. I turned left and followed the road west for about 700 yards, then turned onto Agua Dulce Canyon Road to reach “downtown” Agua Dulce. I stopped in the middle of town near the Sweetwater Farms Market and had my second break. I was somewhat unimpressed. Agua Dulce did not give me the typical small town vibes. This was also the end of the PCT California Section D which had let me through the San Gabriel Mountains all the way from Cajon Canyon. Section E ahead would lead me across the Sierra Pelona and Liebre Mountain to the desert and back into the Tehachapi Mountains.

![Sheep and goats along Agua Dulce Canyon Road (34°29’50.268″ N 118°19’34.686″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A9977-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Mint Canyon (34°31’43.3346″ N 118°18’58.6185″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A9984-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Shade Spring Canyon (34°33’40.404″ N 118°19’6.906″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0023-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I followed the paved road for another two miles. Fortunately, there was not much traffic. I passed goats and horses along the homes lining the road. Turning left onto Sierra Highway just a bit, I took Mint Canyon Road and then headed northeast up Mint Canyon, lots of power lines above me. To my left was a large private property that resembled more of a junk yard. Ahead was a much greener canyon and the Sierra Pelona range. The trail crossed a dry stream and passed a trail register which I proudly signed, then crossed a dirt road and started its ascent all the way up to the top of the Sierra Pelona. The incline was moderate but steady. I crossed Anthony Road at a saddle and views down Spade Spring Canyon opened up. The weather was better now than in the late morning, the sky light blue. From the top of the ridge the views south towards Agua Dulce and back into the San Gabriel Mountains were stunning. I reached the high point of the hike just north of Martindale Ridge Road.

![Shade Spring Canyon (34°33’36.426″ N 118°19’14.394″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0027-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Descending into Bouquet Canyon (34°34’8.2825″ N 118°19’7.2351″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0050-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Bouquet Reservoir (34°34’35.862″ N 118°19’35.4″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0060-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Bouquet Canyon was now to the left. The northern side of the Sierra Pelona had a lot more trees. The descent was as unrelenting as the ascent had been. I passed Bear Spring with a pipe sticking out of the mountain. Soon enough Bouquet Reservoir came into view at the end of Bouquet Canton. The PCT followed an old fire road for some time before sharply turning northeast for the final stretch to the trailhead. On the other side of the canyon, I could see the PCT ascending to the next ridge. When I reached my car, there was still plenty of daylight left.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt, well maintained. About 3 miles of paved road through Agua Dulce. Some impact north of Soledad Canyon from the 2016 Sand Fire. Towards the top of the Sierra Pelona, some impact from the 2011 Mint Fire.

**Parking:** There is room for about 10 cars on the south side of Bouquet Canyon Road.

**Crowds:** Complete solitude except for the stretch through Vasquez Rocks where I met lots of people, including a group of 30 kids.

**Weather:** Mostly sunny in the morning, then increasing clouds before noon. Partly sunny in the afternoon. Low of 44°F / 7°C in the morning at the start of the hike. High of 61°F / 16°C in lower Mint Canyon. Mild intermittent breeze.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Partly in Angeles National Forest. Annual Interagency Permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.7

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Moderate  
Logistics: Very Easy
