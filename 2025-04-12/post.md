# Fobes Saddle to Saddle Junction

Apr 12, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [33.640830, -116.639603](https://maps.app.goo.gl/JhTgGfM6yN2izPtk8) (Fobes Ranch Road)  
**End:** [33.764630, -116.685920](https://maps.app.goo.gl/PEr15ULFcvyfvmVh6) (Humber Park)

**Distance:** 21.2 mi (up to PCT mile 179.4, 6.8% completed)  
**Time:** 11 hours 36 minutes (start 6:45am, end 6:21pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 7,071 ft (low 4,512 ft – high 8,688 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 5,172 ft (start 4,512 ft – end 6,411 ft)

---

Giddiness had overcome me again and I woke up well ahead of my alarm at 5:35am. I had already issued myself a day use permit the evening before at the San Jacinto Ranger Station in Idyllwild, so I was ready to go. Humber Park, the end of the hike where I was going to drop off my bike, was a mere 7 minutes away from Tahquitz Pines Retreat where I was staying for the night. I had forgotten my head lamp, but fortunately it started to dawn before 6am, so there was enough light to chain my bike to the bike rack at the start of Devils Slide Trail.

The drive to Fobes Ranch Road was uneventful and I was ready to go by 6:45am. It was cold though, temperatures within Garner Valley had dropped to 44°F / 7°C, so I layered up to stay warm. Right above me, the sky was mostly clear, but to the east where the sun was rising some cirrus clouds where obscuring its warming rays and painting the sky in yellow and pink hues. The first few miles I had hiked last time, initially up Fobes Ranch Road and then following Fobes Trail all the way up to Fobes Saddle. While the valley had been absolutely calm, the saddle – already 1,500 ft higher – was quite windy. I was back on the PCT now!

![Fobes Ranch Road (33°38’45.852″ N 116°38’10.92″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/281A5654-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Fobes Canyon (33°41’54.6″ N 116°37’1.152″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/281A5673-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Within minutes, I saw the first thru-hiker. Since the last time I had been here, thru-hiking season had kicked off and was now in full swing. I was going to see a few dozen people every day from now on. I took my first break half a mile past the saddle in a more wind protected spot and was soon joined by a thru-hiker (trail name “Fruit Market”) who decided to take a break with me. It turned out that he was a teacher from Austria who liked hiking and was able to take 6 months off to do the PCT. We chatted in German for a while, he asked me how far away various cities were and I dutifully answered everything that I could.

![Apple Canyon from Apache Peak (33°43’7.302″ N 116°37’36.84″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/281A5703-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![A lizard along the trail (33°43’7.302″ N 116°37’36.84″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/281A5724-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Continuing on, the trail offered great views both east towards Palm Dessert and west down Fobes Canyon. Ahead was Spitler Peak. The trail did not go all the way to the top but passed the peak on the eastern flank before dropping to another saddle with Spitler Peak Trail with gorgeous views west down Apple Canyon. The following ascent was steep but eventually plateaued with Apache Peak straight ahead. This was my first side trip of the day. A faint use trail led all the way up to the peak which offered spectacular views in all directions. I could see Lake Hemet to the west, Palm Springs to the northeast, and the rugged skyline of the higher San Jacinto Mountains to the north. I could clearly make out Tahquitz Peak in the distance, knowing that would be my next side trip in the afternoon.

![Murray Canyon (33°43’36.654″ N 116°37’44.406″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/281A5725-Pano-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x433.jpg)

![A dynamited section of the Pacific Crest Trail (33°44’19.704″ N 116°38’42.48″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/281A5760-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail descended a bit, rounding another peak east of Apache Peak with a little bit of snow still on the side of the trail. A steep drop-off to my right made this a thrilling experience. As I continued further, the trail ascended steadily to the east of Antsell Rock, passing through a dynamited section and offering increasingly spectacular views of Murray Canyon to my right. Passing another saddle with great views west again, the trail switchbacked up South Peak and headed straight for Red Tahquitz. I met a few more thru-hikers and chatted a little with them. The trail had reached 8,000 feet at this point and the view down Murray Canyon was breathtaking. The entire area had been burned in the 2013 Mountain Fire.

![Apache Peak and Antsell Rock alongside Murray Canyon (33°44’29.538″ N 116°38’49.362″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/281A5791-Pano-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x657.jpg)

![San Jacinto and Santa Rosa Mountains (33°45’23.19″ N 116°38’54.312″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/281A5819-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Turning north and then west, the trail was now on the northern slope of Red Tahquitz. For the first time since Fobes Saddle, the trail was entering pine forest and giving me some shade. But at the same time, the trail was increasingly covered in snow. Fortunately the snow was fairly compact, likely because of the higher temperatures days earlier, which made walking on it relatively easy. I had brought microspikes, but did not end up using them. Footprints from previous hikers were mostly easy to follow, although they didn’t always exactly align with where the trail was, so I used my offline maps, visual cues, and best judgement to find a suitable path with less snow and fewer steep sections.

![Snow on the north side of Tahquitz Peak (33°45’44.628″ N 116°39’8.616″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/281A5833-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Descending from Saddle Junction via Devils Slide Trail (33°46’29.208″ N 116°40’34.014″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/281A5843-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Passing Tahquitz Valley Trail, the trail ascended more steeply on the north side of Tahquitz Peak. This was my planned second side trip of the day to a lookout tower right on top of the peak. Sadly though, I had to skip this side trip. The northern slope was still deeply covered in snow which would have made it difficult to safely get to the peak. On top of that, time was running out. I had underestimated how long it would take to get this far, especially with snow on the ground. I still had 3.8 miles to go and my bike ride back. And I was getting worried that there wouldn’t be enough daylight left, especially without my head lamp. So I pressed on, all downhill from here. I reached Saddle Junction where I left the PCT and turned west onto Devils Slide Trail. This trail headed down the valley towards Idyllwild, passing a few springs along the way and offering stunning views towards Idyllwild. Even though my feet were starting to hurt, I continued without a break and reached Humber Park at 6:21pm.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt. A lot of downed trees between Fobes Saddle and Red Tahquitz. Some exposed sections past Apache Peak.

**Parking:** There is plenty of parking space at the junction of Fobes Ranch Road (FS 6S05) and Quinn Flat Road (FR 6S23).

**Weather:** Partly cloudy in the morning, then mostly sunny throughout the day. Low temperature of 44°F / 7°C at the beginning of the hike. High temperature of 88°F / 31°C at 1:26pm during the ascent to South Peak.

**Land Manager & Permits:** San Bernardino National Forest. Santa Rosa and San Jacinto Mountains National Monument. Day use permit required for San Jacinto Wilderness (self issued as San Jacinto Ranger Station).

**Overall Rating:** 3.6

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Superb  
Logistics: Easy

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 14.2 mi (1 hour 1 min), 560 ft elevation gain, 13.84 mph average speed

The bike ride back was easier than expected. The first few miles through Idyllwild to CA-243 were steep but passing by quickly. From Idyllwild to Mountain Center, it was mostly downhill but at a gradient that barely forced me to break, so I could use all the energy to zoom down the mountain. Turning left onto CA-74, I had a longer uphill stretch in front of me. The sun was also setting, painting the hills in yellow hues. With a few short breaks, I was able to get to Keen Camp Summit. From there it was downhill again to Lake Hemet and then fairly flat through upper Garner Valley. I arrived back at my car 13 minutes past sunset and was glad that I had made it without my head lamp.
