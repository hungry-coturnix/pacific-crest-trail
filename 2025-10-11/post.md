# Deep Creek to CA-173

Oct 11, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.262909, -117.146766](https://maps.app.goo.gl/4JJZQWC39pjotNAK6) (Hook Creek Road)  
**End:** [34.336679, -117.245834](https://maps.app.goo.gl/1rJx922SM88Cv4fG7) (Deep Creek Trailhead)

**Distance:** 18.7 mi (up to PCT mile 314.3, 11.9% completed)  
**Time:** 7 hours 43 minutes (start 7:31am, end 3:14pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 5,463 ft (low 2,991 ft – high 4,926 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 7,262 ft (start 4,926 ft – end 3,127 ft)

---

The desert can be hot and dry, even in October. I was back for two more PCT hikes and the weather forecast predicted cloudless skies. I was staying in Hesperia, just north of the San Bernardino Mountains. Today’s hike was from Lake Arrowhead down Deep Creek all the way to the Mojave River Dam. Cycling would have been arduous, so I had opted for an Uber ride again. It was still dark at 6:15am when I arrived at Deep Creek Trailhead, just at the end of paved Highway 173. My driver was Guadalupe and she arrived a few minutes early. She was an older lady driving for a living. On the ride to Lake Arrowhead, she told me about her son and his new girlfriend. I didn’t mind her being chatty. What I did however mind, was her driving. On the windy way up the San Bernardino Mountains, she was struggling quite a bit with the curves, swerving into the center most of the time and accelerating erratically. She missed several turns and almost hit oncoming traffic once. Towards the end, I had to tell her the directions because looking at her phone for that was too much of a distraction. Needless to say that I was very glad once she dropped me off and I did not hesitate to give her an honest review.

![Approaching the confluence of Deep Creek and Holcomb Creek (34°17’12.234″ N 117°7’42.372″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8241-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The 300 mile marker (34°17’21.234″ N 117°7’44.394″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8245-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Unlike last time when I had started at North Shore Campground, I was beginning the hike on Hook Creek Road. This was a shorter way back to the PCT, but there was no parking at the gate hence it wasn’t an option last time. The road had also been closed since February 2019 when a large storm had damaged Splinters Cabin. The road was paved, albeit with numerous cracks now, and lined with trees that had begun turning yellow – a beautiful start into the hike. From Hook Creek Road, I turned onto the familiar Crab Flats Road. This time, there were no motorist yet, it was too early in the day. I reached the ravine I had come up last time and was able to retrace my steps to the PCT without any challenges. A few switchbacks later, I reached a large metal bridge spanning Deep Creek.

![Deep Creek north of Holcomb Creek (34°17’24.018″ N 117°7’44.214″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8248-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Fall colors along Deep Creek (34°18’29.676″ N 117°8’22.146″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8276-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail stayed on the western side of the canyon, high above the creek. It was impressive how the water had carved the canyon over millennia. I could only see glimpses of the creek itself every now and then. A portion of the canyon was still in the shade as the sun was slowing coming up in the east. Holcomb Creek, which I had paralleled for so long last time, was in a much deeper canyon when it joined with Deep Creek. The trail had lots of ups and downs but was generally more downhill. I had taken off my hoodie pretty early and the sun was already burning relentlessly. Fortunately, up here there were still enough trees every now and then to provide some shade. The trail crossed Bacon Flats Road 3N20 and had two small benches on the other side, so I decided to take my first break.

![Deep Creek north of Coxey Creek (34°19’2.994″ N 117°8’29.922″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8292-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The hills are bare west of Lion Canyon (34°19’36.804″ N 117°9’30.216″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8313-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The rainbow bridge across Deep Creek (34°20’40.644″ N 117°11’40.56″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8338-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Soon thereafter, the trail was gaining more elevation again, crossing over another metal footbridge with great views of the deeply incised canyon. Several smaller canyons joined from the east as the trail was following the contours of every small nook and cranny on the west side while mostly maintaining elevation. After another bend westward, the trail passed by Deep Creek Hot Springs. I could hear people from almost half a mile away. This spot is very popular and can be reached somewhat easily from the north. It is also a clothing optional swimming opportunity and indeed there were at least a handful of naked people I could make out from the trail. While I had been completely alone on the trail so far, the hot springs were very crowded with families and tents. I decided not to get close and just continue on the trail which was again gaining plenty of elevation. About 2 miles further, the trail dropped into the canyon and crossed to the northern side over an arched rainbow bridge.

![The first glimpse of the San Gabriel Mountains (34°20’34.068″ N 117°13’3.414″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8368-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Mojave River Dam at the mouth of Deep Creek (34°20’38.8114″ N 117°13’29.8703″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8380-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The mouth of Deep Creek (34°20’47.394″ N 117°13’43.278″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8388-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The northern side of the canyon was barren with pretty much no shade for the next 3 miles. The sun was scorching and I felt like I was baking. I passed a woman who was hiking southbound and chatted with her for a bit. She was doing a shorter PCT section and had started at Islip Saddle in the San Gabriel Mountains – a place I expected to be a few hikes from now. She asked me whether I was a PCT hiker and we talked a bit about the trail. As I continued, the trail gradually gained elevation again, following an old abandoned aqueduct. I was exhausted and the heat was not helping. But on the other hand, the views of the canyon were spectacular. The trail eventually reached the Mojave River Dam. Also known as the Mojave Forks dam, this is a dry dam which is only supposed to hold back excess water during high floods. Great views to the west had opened up across the Mojave River Forks. After switchbacking down the southern side of the dam a few times, the trail crossed the spillway and then headed all the way to the bottom of the dam.

![The Mojave River Dam with the San Gabriel Mountains in the distance (34°20’48.096″ N 117°13’45.138″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8390-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Mojave River Forks, west of the dam (34°20’19.302″ N 117°14’38.94″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/281A8392-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Deep Creek was fairly shallow here, but I had to cross it and this time, there was no footbridge. Since my feet were hot as well, I decided to just walk through the water and enjoy the cool. It was less than a mile to the car and I was okay hiking with wet feet in exchange for a refreshing river ford. On the south side, the trail gently meandered across the grassy shore. Three young adults were smoking weed but I passed them pretty quickly – no need to attract any attention. When I reached Deep Creek Trailhead I was a little dehydrated and eagerly gulped down two bottles of water.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Dirt and loose rocks. Lightly overgrown at times in the upper reaches of Deep Creek.

**Parking:** There is plenty of room for parking at Deep Creek Trailhead.

**Crowds:** Met 9 people on trail, all between Deep Creek Hot Springs and Deep Creek Trailhead. Deep Creek Hot Springs was very popular with several dozen people.

**Weather:** Sunny with blue skies and no clouds. 61°F / 16°C in the morning at the bottom of Hook Creek Road. High of 94°F / 35°C in the afternoon on the exposed northern side of Deep Creek.

**Land Manager & Permits:** San Bernardino National Forest. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.4

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Easy
