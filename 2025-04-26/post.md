# Saddle Junction to Deer Springs Trail

Apr 26, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [33.813179, -116.638600](https://maps.app.goo.gl/QQFDjfaYKufPSXaA7) (Palm Springs Aerial Tramway)  
**End:** [33.813179, -116.638600](https://maps.app.goo.gl/QQFDjfaYKufPSXaA7) (Palm Springs Aerial Tramway)

**Distance:** 25.2 mi (up to PCT mile 185.7, 7.0% completed)  
**Time:** 9 hours 51 minutes (start 8:15am, end 6:06pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 6,530 ft (low 7,805 ft – high 10,557 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 6,530 ft (start 8,535 ft – end 8,535 ft)

---

Today’s hike was very memorable. Not in the sense of stunning vistas and epic ascents though. This particular hike was going to be different from all the previous hikes. But it turned out to be different in a very unexpected way. Have you ever hiked during a snow storm at 10,000 feet? If not, then this story is for you.

Before we get into the core experience of this hike, let me walk you through the logistics. This was the first loop hike since I started on the PCT, so there was no bike ride back to my car. The loop only included 6.3 miles on the PCT itself, but the highlight was going to be 10,804 feet San Jacinto Peak, the highest peak in the San Jacinto Mountains. The other highlight was the Palm Springs Aerial Tramway which was going to get me to the start of the hike via a 10 minute gondola ride from Palm Springs all the way to 8,500 feet. Advanced tickets for the first tram up at 8am were sold out when I had checked two weeks ago, so I had to get a walk-in ticket.

8am was much later than my usual start time so far and I woke up well before 6am in anticipation. The parking lot opened at 7am and I was at the gate at 7am sharp. I was the first to get a walk-in ticket. While I was waiting to board the tram, I saw two state park rangers with lots of gear going up. I would meet them again soon to get my day use permit. More people arrived, including a group of adolescent boys and a family with a teenage girl who was only wearing shorts. While it was in the high 50s in Palm Springs, the temperature at the mountain station of the tramway was going to be barely above freezing. I felt bad for the girl who was probably unaware what the experience was going to be like.

And this brings me to the weather. I had checked the forecasts every day, including prior trail reports, and was moderately hopeful, but also anxious. A marine layer with a lot of precipitation had made its way into the Los Angeles area. I had seen the dark clouds the evening before when I was driving to Palm Springs. The area to the west of the San Jacinto Mountains was expected to get rain or even a bit of snow. But the clouds were not expected to get across the first ridge and San Jacinto Peak would likely be above the clouds. The trail reports showed photos from less than 2 weeks ago where the clouds were well below the peak, offering a spectacular view from above. Temperatures were going to be low though. The high for the peak was just above freezing and below for most of the day. And a strong breeze was expected as well.

As the tramway was going up, the views of Palm Springs got increasingly better. I felt good about the day ahead. The sky above was blue with not a single cloud. I would have a very scenic day with lots of good photos. It was cold as expected but a brisk hike would soon warm me up. From the tramway a paved path led down steeply to a picnic area and the Long Valley Ranger Station. I filled out my day use permit and briefly chatted with one of the rangers I had seen earlier. Turning south, I crossed Long Valley Creek and headed up Willow Creek Trail towards Hidden Lake Divide. The trail was easy to follow, meandering through lots of pine trees.

On the southern side of the divide, the slope had been ravaged by the 2013 Mountain Fire. Charred trees were still dominating the view. But this also opened up amazing views across the Tahquitz Creek valley towards Red Tahquitz and Tahquitz Peak. And as I had anticipated, the clouds were just behind the ridgeline, putting me slightly above them – exactly what I had seen in the trail report.

![Red Tahquitz from Willow Creek Trail (33°47’48.1788″ N 116°38’41.3627″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/281A5871-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail descended towards Willow Creek, the low point of this trail, and then gently ascended again towards Saddle Junction. My pace was good and I took my first break about half a mile before Saddle Junction at 9:50am. As I got closer to Saddle Junction, I started noticing clouds above me. This was not entirely unexpected, some clouds would flow over the saddle. So as soon as I gained more elevation I would likely be above the clouds again.

I was back on the PCT now, immediately passing a thru-hiker. Over the next 2 miles, the trail was switchbacking up the slope again and again. The sun was gone, visibility was decreasing, and it got a little colder and windier. The trail moved to the northern side of the mountain and was covered in snow. A well traveled path made it fairly easy to get ahead though. Past the junction towards Wellman Divide, the PCT turned west and started descending. Visibility reduced to a few hundred feet and the wind was blowing from the side. Without the cloud cover, I likely would have had amazing views towards Idyllwild, but all I could see now was a gray wall. I took my second break just before the Strawberry Junction Camp at about 10.5 miles. While I was sitting on a log and having snacks, it started snowing a bit.

![Crusty old snow north of Saddle Junction (33°47’1.7915″ N 116°40’26.8945″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/281A5890-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Views of Idyllwild are obscured by clouds (33°47’14.4841″ N 116°40’45.8572″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/281A5893-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I didn’t stay long. After putting on my wind jacket and covering my camera with a protector, I hurried on. Part of me was still hopeful that I would soon be out of the snow again and above the clouds. Past Strawberry Junction, the trail started ascending again, so it would just be a matter of time until I would be out of the snow. A mile paste the junction, the snow intensified and stuck to the ground, leaving everything covered in white dust. The trail was still easy to follow though. Past Marion Mountain Trail, pockets of older, very compact and crusty snow started to appear. I had brought my microspikes, but walking was still easy, so I left them in my backpack.

I reached the junction with Deer Springs Trail which would take me all the way up to San Jacinto Peak. The trail was steeper, with lots of switchbacks, but not too difficult to follow. I met a group of three guys coming down and asked them about the conditions at the peak. My hope was crushed when they told me that they had turned around half a mile from the peak because it was covered in clouds and snowing hard. I did not have much of a choice at this point but to continue. I was past the halfway point of the hike, so I accepted my fate and kept going. The snow was blowing sideways and the temperature had dropped further. Route finding became more and more challenging. There were well worn tracks, but they were now under an inch of fresh snow which made them hard to discover. And the footsteps of previous hikers were very quickly blown over by the wind.

![Deer Springs Trail below a few feet of snow (33°48’37.2034″ N 116°41’21.3475″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/281A5897-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Icy pine needles (33°48’45.46″ N 116°40’57.17″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/PXL_20250426_224412405-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x768.jpg)

The ground was almost solidly covered in old snow now. I reached the Little Round Valley Campground and the signs were barely sticking out of the snow. Past the campground a long series of switchbacks was getting me to the junction with the peak trail. Finding the trail was difficult and I had to backtrack several times. The snow was blowing on and off and it was very wet snow, quickly turning into ice. I was still reasonably warm. But after more than 5,000 feet elevation gain, my energy was slowly draining. After what felt like 50 switchbacks, I finally reached the junction with Peak Trail. A couple was just coming down and I asked them whether it was worth going up. They acknowledged that the top was also covered in clouds and hence I decided not to do the 0.3 miles up to the summit. The temperature had dropped below freezing at this point and the summit would have been even colder than that.

On the east side of San Jacinto Peak, the snow was blowing much less and the trail (although not the bushes right next to the trail) was even partially snow free. Invigorated, I moved up the pace down the mountain. Soon enough, the trail was going over a solid old snow blanket again. I reached Wellman Divide and turned left onto Round Valley Trail. The descent was relentless and my left ankle was starting to hurt. But I kept going, not wanting to take another break. Another 3 miles further, I finally reached the Long Valley Ranger Station again and made it up to the tramway station. When I took off my hat, it was covered in about half an inch of snow. And my backpack was almost fully white.

A few parting thoughts. The distance of this hike was much more than I had anticipated. OpenStreetmap routing had put this hike at 18.3 miles. Trail distances from the National Geographic Trails Illustrated Map had put it at 18.6 miles. The guideposts on trail would have put it at 20.6 miles. I realized that the switchbacks are often not accurately mapped, making the trail shorter than it actually is. On top of that, my backtracking and route finding across snow likely added an extra mile or two. Overall, this means that the actual mileage is more than 20% longer.

I have never hiked in such a snow storm before. My optimism had got the better of me and I was still hopeful for far too long. The weather forecast was also just plain wrong. I guess there was a 50-50 chance whether the strong winds would blow the clouds up the mountain or not. Well, they did. What could have been one of my most picturesque hikes ever turned into a snowy disaster. I only took about 35 photos and I am very sad that a unique place like San Jacinto Peak escaped me like that. I might have to do it again some day, next time just the peak and not the loop with the PCT.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt and rocks (when it was not covered in snow). Some downed trees on Willow Creek Trail.

**Parking:** The Aerial Tramway has an abundance of (paid) parking.

**Weather:** Sunny with blue skies in the morning, then snow storm after noon with about 1 inch of snow accumulation. Breezy throughout the day with strong gusts and low visibility during the snow storm. Low temperature of 28°F / -2°C in the afternoon during the descent from San Jacinto Peak. High temperature of 65°F / 18°C at 9:30am near Willow Creek.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Santa Rosa and San Jacinto Mountains National Monument. San Bernardino National Forest. Day use permit required for San Jacinto Wilderness (self issued as Long Valley Ranger Station).

**Overall Rating:** 3.6

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Low (Superb without clouds)  
Logistics: Very Easy
