# Cow Canyon to Canebrake Road

Jun 6, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [35.737697, -118.077667](https://maps.app.goo.gl/1WCgXZ4PhbzDV1tVA) (CA-178 at the mouth of Cow Canyon)  
**End:** [35.835761, -118.044031](https://maps.app.goo.gl/eqjYM9oyE84m6WhB6) (Canebrake Road)

**Distance:** 19.8 mi (up to PCT mile 682.1, 25.7% completed)  
**Time:** 9 hours 15 minutes (start 6:23am, end 3:38pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 5,028 ft (low 3,821 ft – high 7,025 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 3,302 ft (start 3,833 ft – end 5,559 ft)

---

This was the fourth consecutive weekend of hiking another section of the Pacific Crest Trail. I was getting closer to Kennedy Meadows and the heart of the Sierra Nevada. As the crow flies, I was only going to cover 7 miles today but the actual hiking distance was almost 20 miles. I stayed at Chimney Creek Campground for the night. It was 11 miles up dirt Canebrake Road, right between the Owens Peak Wilderness and Chimney Peak Wilderness. The PCT was crossing Canebrake Road just 300 yards south of the campground which was my destination for this section. The dirt road was steep and quite sandy, but overall very manageable for my Subaru. I arrived at the campground a bit after sunset. At least two PCT thru hikers had set up tent right at the entrance of the campground. I continued down the campground road and found a nice and reasonably level campsite where I parked and set up my rooftop tent. For the first time, there were mosquitos! Yet another sign that I was getting further north. The campsite was nestled among pine trees and was very quiet and peaceful.

I got a good amount of sleep when my alarm went off at 5am. It was dawning and I took about 30 minutes to get into hiking clothes and pack up. My first stop was the PCT crossing at Canebrake Road. A PCT thru hiker was already on the road on the way to continue their journey northbound. I chained my bike to a small tree, then continued driving back down to the highway. The sun was coming up and painting the top of the hills golden. I parked in the exact same spot as last time in a pullout west of the highway. It was much warmer than last time, already 62°F / 17°C at 6:30am.

The first 3 miles were the familiar path up Cow Canyon. I crossed the creek near the highway, scrambled up the hillside to the abandoned dirt road which I followed all the way to the PCT. The sun was peeking over the ridge north of Owens Peak. Within just a few minutes back on the PCT, I met the first thru hiker, an older gentleman who was telling me that he was “just getting the lubrication going”. I enjoyed the views down Cow Canyon. The PCT headed up to a saddle, then continued further uphill to yet another saddle a mile north. Views west were spectacular and I could even see Lake Isabella in the distance.

On the other side of the next saddle, a huge canyon opened up. Straight north was Lamont Peak and down at the bottom of the canyon was Spanish Needle Creek. Looking west, I could also see the Canebrake Road switchbacks that I had taken this morning. The PCT descended eastward into the canyon and would contour this canyon for what felt like an eternity. When I reached the first arm of Spanish Needle Creek that was carrying water, there were two more thru hikers filtering water. This was also the beginning of a 4 mile ascent to yet another saddle east of Lamont Peak. I tried to keep a slower but steady pace. The views downcanyon gradually kept getting better and better. I was glad when I finally reached the saddle at 6,760 feet / 2,060 meters and caught a strong breeze on the other side.

The views down the canyon were great. Lamont Peak was to the left. Lamont Meadow was in the distance. The trail turned east, going uphill just a bit more, and reached another saddle with South Fork Sand Canyon to the east. The view was stunning and reminded me of what I had seen last time east of Mount Jenkins. This was the highest point for today and also the windiest point. The PCT turned north following the crest. Lamont Meadow was slowly getting closer. But the trail did not head straight for the meadow. Instead it crossed over yet again to the north at a saddle south of Sawtooth Peak, then switchbacked down into the canyon. Sawtooth Peak was looming dominantly to the north. To the east was Sand Canyon which I was not able to get a good view of.

It was all downhill from now. The northern part of Lamont Meadow came into view. The high peaks of Chimney Peak Wilderness were to the west. I was walking among Jeffrey pines and sugar pines. Chimney Creek just north of Lamont Meadow was still flowing well. 50 yards later, I reached Canebrake Road where my bike was waiting for me.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Dirt singletrack with some patches of loose rock. Slightly overgrown east of Lamont Peak.

**Parking:** There is room for a few cars in a large pullout along Highway 178.

**Crowds:** Lightly traveled. Met 12 northbound thru hikers throughout the day.

**Weather:** Sunny with no clouds. Light to moderate intermittent breeze past noon. Strong winds across the canyon north of Lamont Peak, especially towards the pass with South Fork Sand Canyon. Low of 62°F / 17°C at the beginning of the hike in Cow Canyon. High of 86°F / 30°C in the afternoon north of Lamont Meadow. Humidity between 52% in the morning and 21% in the afternoon.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Owens Peak Wilderness. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.7

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Moderate

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 12.9 mi (1 hour 53 minutes), 1,060 ft elevation gain, 6.88 mph average speed, 17.75 mph maximum speed

The way back took every bit of energy I had still left. The sun was burning relentlessly. The first 10 miles were along Canebrake Road back to the highway. The first two of these miles were downhill, but then a stretch of two miles uphill followed. For the most part, I was pushing my bike. The strong headwinds didn’t help either. When I finally reached Lamont Peak Trailhead at the saddle, I had to take a short break and drink some more water.

The next 7 miles were downhill and passed fairly quickly. It was fairly sandy, but I was able to navigate these stretches without major difficulties. After crossing Canebrake Creek, I was back at the highway. 3 more miles uphill were between me and my car. Fortunately I had the strong winds on my back now. But even then, the incline felt brutal and I ended up pushing the bike on and off. Traffic was light and there was a small gravel strip on the side of the pavement most of the time. These 3 miles took forever and I was incredibly happy to finally see my car.
