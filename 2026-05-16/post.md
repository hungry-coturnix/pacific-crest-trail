# South Kelso Valley Road to Bird Spring Pass

May 16, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [35.461507, -118.211515](https://maps.app.goo.gl/E6iHEd7GA2buV2C68) (Kelso Valley Road & Butterbredt Canyon Road)  
**End:** [35.552949, -118.133441](https://maps.app.goo.gl/okfgnHFBZG7zu3Aq6) (Bird Spring Canyon Road at Bird Spring Pass)

**Distance:** 16.4 mi (up to PCT mile 632.0, 23.8% completed)  
**Time:** 6 hours 42 minutes (start 6:38am, end 1:20pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 3,232 ft (low 4,495 ft – high 6,016 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 2,372 ft (start 4,495 ft – end 5,355 ft)

---

It was the first time putting my four-wheel drive car and my new roof top tent to the test. The plan was to drive to Bird Spring Pass the prior evening, spend the night there, and drop off my bike in the morning. Bird Spring Canyon Road – the only way to get to Bird Spring Pass – was a maintained dirt road requiring a high-clearance vehicle. When I reached the road it was only a few minutes to sunset and I was expecting to make the 5 miles to the pass fairly quickly. But I was wrong. The road started out very sandy, but fairly easy, then turned into a pretty steep and rugged endeavor. There were lots of large rocks sticking out of the ground and I had to navigate the ruts carefully and strategically to make it through. Needless to say that my pace was pretty slow and I even had a handful of moments where I was wondering whether I would make it all the way to the pass. When I got there, it was almost completely dark. There was another car in the middle of the road on the pass (I assume because it was more level) with two people likely wondering what I was doing up here so late.

When I got out of the car, it was very windy. So much so that it was clear I needed to find a different place to camp for the night. A few spots on the way up had looked reasonably flat and much less windy. I changed plans and just chained my bike to a wooden railing, then started my way down the mountain again. Now it was completely dark. Going downhill was surprisingly easier. Or maybe I just felt a bit more familiar with the terrain. After most of the descent was behind me, I found a small spot on the right sheltered by trees and parked. Setting up the tent only took a few minutes. The area was not as flat as I would have liked, but I figured it would be okay. It was still pretty warm and I settled in with the sleeping bag wide open. The occasional wind gust was flapping the rain cover of the tent but I ended up getting a decent albeit low-quality amount of sleep. When I woke up around 5am, it had started dawning and so I slowly got myself ready, changing into hiking clothes. Packing up the tent was equally fast and I left the spot at 5:43am.

After making my way down Bird Spring Canyon Road, I turned south onto South Kelso Valley Road until I reached a small parking area at the junction with Butterbredt Canyon Road. The PCT was still a mile away, but the last stretch had a lot of elevation gain, so I had decided to hike it on foot rather than trying to tackle this on my bicycle at the very end. The sun had come out by now and was painting the top of the hills golden. I had a bite to eat and then set out on my PCT hike for the day. The road walk was steep but it was only a mile long and there was no traffic at all. At the junction with the PCT, I met two hikers who were just getting water from the gigantic water cache that had been placed there by trail angels. One of them I would meet several times throughout the day as we were leap frogging each other.

I turned left and was back on the PCT. The trail headed east offering splendid views of Kelso Valley to the north framed by the mountain range in the Bright Star Wilderness to the left and Mayan Peak to the right. Dropping down to Butterbredt Canyon Road, the trail was at its lowest point for the day. A long but moderate ascent was on the other side. Mayan Peak dominated the view north. Aside from the occasional Western Joshua Tree and plenty of Western Desert Penstemon, the vegetation was pretty bleak. This area was clearly in the rain shadow of the higher mountain ranges to the west and not seeing much precipitation. After cresting a saddle, rocky Pinyon Mountain was prominently ahead of me. The PCT dropped a little into a small canyon, then tackled the western flank of Pinyon Mountain. The wind had picked up again and it was chilly being so exposed. The northern side of Pinyon Mountain had a few more trees and I took it as an opportunity for a break in a more sheltered gully. The view down the canyon northwest towards Kelso Valley was incredible and you could see Kelso Peak in the distance.

The PCT gained some more elevation and then reached a saddle crossing Dove Spring Canyon Road. The views to the east were expansive across Indian Wells Valley, the El Paso Mountains, and Fremont Valley. The trail turned due north contouring a few east-facing slopes, then crossed over to the western side at another dirt road junction which now offered spectacular views to the west. The wind had picked up even more now and I had to hold on to my sun hat. I should have taken the weather forecast more seriously when it had predicted 30 mph winds with wind gusts of 50 mph. Before crossing yet another dirt road, I could see some old and rusty gravity-fed ore chute, a remnant of gold mining in this area around 1870. The PCT moved back to an east-facing slope and gently switchbacked downhill before crossing two more dirt roads in quick succession. Ahead was another long ascent flanked by Joshua trees. I took another break in the wind shadow of a cluster of Joshua trees, the far reaching views were stunning.

To the north was Wyleys Knob now, a rocky outcrop with a large radio tower on top. The PCT moved back to the eastern side, revealing expansive views down Bird Spring Canyon, then descended gently on the north side of Wyleys Knob and rounded the head of Bird Spring Canyon before reaching Bird Spring Pass. It was surprisingly busy here. A bunch of hikers were around, some resting, some making use of the large water cache north of the road. I took another break before hopping on my bike to cycle back.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt, occasionally quite sandy. Very exposed to the sun with little shade.

**Parking:** There is room for several cars at a small parking area for Butterbredt Canyon Road.

**Crowds:** Mostly solitude. Met two hikers at the water cache at Kelso Valley Road and kept leap frogging with one of them throughout the day. Bird Spring Pass had half a dozen hikers resting or stocking up on water.

**Weather:** Sunny with no clouds throughout the entire day. Very strong winds of 30 mph with wind gusts of 50 mph. Low of 59°F / 15°C around 8:40am on the west side of Pinyon Mountain. High of 76°F / 25°C around 1:10pm north of Wyleys Knob.

**Land Manager & Permits:** All land managed by the Bureau of Land Management. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.8

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Moderate

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 12.6 mi (1 hour 47 minutes), 1,090 ft elevation gain, 7.04 mph average speed, 26.63 mph maximum speed

Even though I had already cut down on the elevation gain by doing the last mile on foot, the bike ride back was still very exhausting. The first 5 miles were downhill on Bird Spring Canyon Road. I was not much slower than in my car, but the steepest stretch in the beginning required careful navigation through sand and around rocks and ruts. The straighter second half of the road was easier although still quite sandy. Within 45 minutes, I had made it back to Kelso Valley Road. The remaining 7 miles were paved but also with a lot more uphill. It was also a lot warmer down here in the valley and I was feeling it. I ended up alternating between cycling and pushing my bike up the steeper stretches of the road. The occasional wind was very welcome now. Only two cars passed me during the entire hour I was on the road. When I could finally see my car, I was elated and exhausted.
