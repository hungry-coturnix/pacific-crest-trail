# Trail Pass to Cottonwood Pass (Day 2)

Aug 23, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Central California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/central-california/)

**Start:** [36.438189, -118.312949](https://maps.app.goo.gl/fXqXo1GgLgqj36CK7) (Rocky Basin Lakes)  
**End:** [36.448336, -118.170638](https://maps.app.goo.gl/DmW1mNZnJkUwNtfk9) (Cottonwood Pass Trailhead)

**Distance:** 16.0 mi (up to PCT mile 751.4, 28.4% completed)  
**Time:** 7 hours 38 minutes (start 6:42am, end 2:20pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 2,305 ft (low 9,699 ft – high 11,129 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 2,949 ft (start 10,580 ft – end 9,936 ft)

---

I woke up before my alarm at 5:45am as darkness had already begun to fade. It was a bit chilly, but not cold, and remarkably peaceful and quiet outside of the tent. After packing up and eating a light breakfast, I set off – excited to get back on the Pacific Crest Trail, even if only for a mere 5 miles going from Cottonwood Pass to Trail Pass. I retraced my steps down the switchbacks to Barigan Stringer and reached the junction towards Cottonwood Pass where I turned left. The trail picked up elevation again via switchbacks up to a saddle to the east before descending rapidly towards Big Whitney Meadow. I could only see faint glimpses of the meadow through the trees but I was hoping for a good view once I actually entered the meadow.

I took my first break after 4 miles, then continued to Big Whitney Meadow. Siberian Pass Trail – which I had hiked further south yesterday – finally merged from the right. I walked up a low sandy ridge. Crossing over the crest of the ridge, the pine trees stopped abruptly, unfolding into the most gorgeous meadow I had ever seen. Big Whitney Meadow was truly expansive. To the north, towering Cirque Peak dominated the skyline with a heavily forested base giving way to bare granite at its summit. Straight ahead to the east, I could clearly spot Cottonwood Pass. A few large granite boulders were scattered across the golden meadow floor. I was in awe. Crossing Golden Trout Creek in the middle of the meadow required some careful boulder hopping, but the stream was shallow and crystal clear, allowing me to make it across while keeping my feet dry. I kept marveling at the meadow as I made my way east, successfully boulder-hopping across two more creeks.

The trail traced along the edge of the wide, golden plain, offering a full wide-angle view back across the expanse with the bare granite top of Cirque Peak looming prominently above the treeline. Looking ahead to the east, the trail cut through sandy terrain dotted with light granite rocks. Cottonwood Pass sat visibly on the horizon, a distinct low notch in the ridgeline calling me forward toward the ascent. Before leaving the open valley floor behind, I stopped for one last look north across the golden grasses of Big Whitney Meadow. The contrast between the bright field, the dense forest wall, and the massive, exposed granite crest of Cirque Peak under the cloudless blue sky was incredible. Shortly after, the singletrack re-entered the pine forest and began to climb once again. The initial climb out of Big Whitney Meadow was short, leading over a low pine ridge before descending across open, sandy ground toward a smaller meadow centered around Stokes Stringer. The stream was barely flowing down in the meadow, which worried me a bit since I needed to replenish my water soon. Returning to the shade of the forest, the ascent to Cottonwood Pass began in earnest. The gradient was steep but steady as the trail tracked up countless switchbacks. I was relieved when the trail crossed Stokes Stringer higher up the drainage, where it was flowing quite well. I dropped my backpack and filtered a batch of fresh, cold water.

Resuming the climb, the views behind me expanded dramatically with every step as the switchbacks carved higher up the slope. Looking back down the valley, Big Whitney Meadow appeared as a bright golden oasis far below, framed by vast pine forests and the distant, rugged granite peaks of the Sierra Crest. The last switchbacks were exhausting. When I finally made it to the top of Cottonwood Pass at 11,129 ft / 3,392 m, I was out of breath. But I was also excited because I was back on the Pacific Crest Trail now – and it was mostly downhill from here. The pass was marked by a weathered wooden signpost pointing north toward Rock Creek and south toward Trail Pass. The PCT headed over a saddle, then descended to a small meadow with great views back north towards Cirque Peak. The trail cut through high subalpine terrain, offering direct views towards an unnamed rocky peak towering above the path. At a rocky outcrop on the right of the trail, I got a little adventurous and climbed up some of the big granite boulders. I was rewarded with an incredible view across Golden Trout Wilderness all the way to the sharp, jagged crest of the Great Western Divide on the distant horizon. I could see numerous meadows framed by forested mountains, with Kern Peak prominently in the distance. I soaked in the view for a while before I continued.

Turning east, the PCT headed towards Trail Peak. Through a natural gap in the pine tree on my right, I caught a great view of Mulkey Meadows nestled far below – the very meadow I had walked past just yesterday morning. Moving over a saddle just south of Poison Meadow, the trail shifted to the northern side of Trail Peak. While much of the perspective here was framed by lodgepole pines, framed clearings provided striking vistas across towards the Sierra Crest in the direction of Mount Langley and the bare top of Flat Top. As the trail wrapped further around the ridge toward Trail Pass, an impressive panorama opened up directly below, revealing the sweeping curve of Round Valley and Horseshoe Meadow cradled inside the vast mountain basin. I took my final break shortly before Trail Pass. On the switchbacks down from Trail Pass, I met an older couple who was taking a break next to the trail with their dog. We chatted a bit and inevitably got to my PCT story. They called it “PCT in installments”, which I found to be a very apt description. We said goodbye, and I continued down into Horseshoe Meadow. The singletrack meandered gently across the wide, grassy floor, crossing the quiet stream that reflected the afternoon clouds floating over the surrounding peaks. The last two miles went by very quickly and I was glad when I finally reached my car.

---

**Trail Conditions:** All trails were in good condition and easy to follow. Cottonwood Pass Trail was quite rocky at times.

**Parking:** There is plenty of room for cars at the trailhead.

**Crowds:** Very lightly traveled. Only met 6 people and 1 dog throughout the day. The first backpacker passed me at Stokes Stringer near Cottonwood Pass. Three day hikers were going north on the Pacific Crest Trail. An older couple with a dog was taking a break on the switchbacks north of Trail Pass.

**Weather:** Sunny with a few scattered clouds throughout the entire day. Unfortunately, I lost all my weather recordings due to spotty cellphone service when I tried to upload the data.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Golden Trout Wilderness. Inyo National Forest. Wilderness permit required (no quota).

**Overall Rating:** 4.2

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Very Easy
