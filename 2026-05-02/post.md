# CA-58 to South Kelso Valley Road (Day 1)

May 2, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [35.100060, -118.292786](https://maps.app.goo.gl/K51jGcersQAGd7t49) (CA-58 at Cameron Road Exit)  
**End:** [35.225799, -118.234563](https://maps.app.goo.gl/LmuNzqvvfnvZ5gyE6) (Golden Oaks Spring)

**Distance:** 17.0 mi (up to PCT mile 583.3, 22.0% completed)  
**Time:** 9 hours 5 minutes (start 7:51am, end 4:56pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 4,005 ft (low 3,769 ft – high 6,193 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 2,374 ft (start 3,844 ft – end 5,474 ft)

---

I was very excited. This would be my first 3-day backpacking trip on the Pacific Crest Trail. The area north of Tehachapi was fairly remote and there was no way to slice the hike into a shorter day hike. Two nights was the bare minimum. Logistically, this trip was also much more challenging than any stretch of the PCT before. Cycling was not an option. Even the car ride from the end to the beginning was about 2 hours. So I had enlisted my friends Chris and Lisa to give me a ride. We had dropped off my car at South Kelso Valley Road the evening before. The drive up from Bakersfield through the Kern River gorge had been spectacular and when we arrived at the PCT trailhead at 7:53pm, the sun had just set and the surrounding mountains were painted in pink hues. Both Lisa and Chris really loved the spot and the view. It was incredibly quiet and peaceful. Then we drove to Tehachapi in their car and stayed at a hotel for the night. I could not sleep very well because I was too giddy. In the morning, we had a simple breakfast with bagels and eggs before we headed out.

Chris and Lisa drove me back to the Cameron Road Exit at highway 58. It was bound to be a beautiful sunny day. Lisa was almost more excited than I was. While I was putting on my camera harness and backpack, a gentleman with a dog parked as well and asked whether he could take a photo of us. I guess it’s always a bit sensational to meet a hiker on the Pacific Crest Trail. I said goodbye to Chris and Lisa, they were headed off to Sequoia National Park, and turned east to start my adventure. The trail was paralleling the highway for the first mile, just within 50 feet of the pavement. Morning traffic was light, but the noise was still a distraction from my usually peaceful hiking experience.

![The trail veers away from the highway towards the ridge (35°6’18.1803″ N 118°16’14.9692″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2049-1024x683.jpg)

![Cooper’s Goldflower (Cooper’s Hymenoxys) along the trail (35°6’22.3123″ N 118°16’9.4794″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2050-1024x683.jpg)

![A lone Joshua tree with Tehachapi Pass in the distance (35°6’35.6924″ N 118°15’27.0534″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2057-1024x683.jpg)

I passed the gentleman with the dog and he asked me whether I had started in Campo. I briefly explained my hiking endeavor and he wished me good luck. Both sides of the trail were lined with wildflowers as I crossed Tehachapi Pass. The mountains on either side were dry with only a handful of Joshua trees and a few shrubs. I knew I had a very long set of switchbacks ahead of me to get up this sun exposed south side. A long freight train passed on the other side of the highway. After crossing bone dry La Rose Creek, the PCT veered northeast away from the highway and started its gradual climb. Soon enough, the trail moved onto a dirt road where I met the gentleman with the dog again who had taken the dirt road shortcut. We chatted for a bit more while hiking. He was from nearby Mojave and came here quite often to walk his dog. He also told me about his experience hiking the John Muir Trail (JMT) a few years back. We parted ways again when the PCT left the dirt road and turned north.

What followed felt like an arduous climb. There was no shade and the sun was already intense. A light intermittent breeze made it only somewhat more bearable. With a heavy backpack and 75°F / 24°C at 9am, it was already draining. The section north of me was known to be very dry. The only water source – Golden Oaks Spring – was 17 miles away and also my destination for the night. It also meant that I had to carry all of my water for the day. I had drunk as much water as I could at the hotel to “camel up” and was only carrying 0.75 gallons / 3 liters. I would have to ration it throughout the day. After about 4 miles – still well within the switchbacks – I took my first break in the small shade of a larger juniper bush. I laid down and closed my eyes to recover a bit. The view from this spot was spectacular. The vast Mojave Desert was stretching out in front of me, just behind the ridge on the other side of the highway. I could see the town of Mojave and the Mojave Air & Space Port. And as I was watching, a large airplane was descending towards the runway.

![The Tehachapi Mountains in the distance (35°8’8.1175″ N 118°16’8.5391″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2130-1024x683.jpg)

After half an hour of rest, I continued my ascent, gaining over 2,000 feet as I made my way to the top of the ridge. On the western side was now Waterfall Canyon with Pajuela Peak and Tehachapi further in the distance. The top of the ridge was finally also more forested with larger pine trees. At 8.4 miles, the PCT joined dirt road MK10 – named after Middle Knob, the ridge to the east – and followed it for the next 4.4 miles. I took my second break in the shade of a large Jeffrey pine, sitting on the ground and leaning my back against the tree. It was a lot warmer now, not unbearable, but slowly zapping the energy out of me. MK10 descended quickly to pass at the head of Pine Tree Canyon to the east and another unnamed canyon to the west. It was a lot more remote now, the only sign of civilization the wide dirt road I was walking on. The steep ascent on the other side was taxing, my pace slowed and I took frequent short breaks.

![Passing the head of Pine Tree Canyon looking northwest (35°10’42.8494″ N 118°14’51.5184″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2153-1024x683.jpg)

I passed a small flat meadow and headed towards the wind turbines straight ahead up on Sweet Ridge. Then I heard engine noise in the distance. A few moments later, two big all-terrain vehicles (ATVs) passed me at an impressive speed. The dirt road was pretty rugged with lots of larger rocks, but they managed to drive over that pretty fast. A quarter mile later, I reached the parked ATVs at a dead end, the drivers were drinking sodas and walking around. I passed them quickly unsure how friendly they were. The PCT continued as an actual trail again behind several large boulders. I passed a service road for the wind farm half a mile later. Northeast of me was another large canyon with far reaching views. The PCT stayed somewhat level but followed the outline of Sweet Ridge, moving in and out of every side canyon.

![The next ridge might already be barren desert (35°13’14.3874″ N 118°13’52.2731″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2200-1024x683.jpg)

I was reconsidering how far to hike today. My original plan had been to hike another mile or so past Golden Oaks Spring after getting some water, but I already felt very tired and my feet were hurting. Crossing another dirt road, I reached the spring. There was a large cement trough and a pipe with flowing water. It was nicely shaded with lots of pine trees around. The area was flat offering ample space for tents. I decided that I wouldn’t hike any further and stay here. I may even end up being the only person staying here tonight. First order of business was to filter some water though. I was very thirsty and had drunk all of my remaining water. While I was filling up my water pouch, two thru hikers arrived as well. So much for being by myself. After drinking a lot of water, I set up my tent and started with dinner. It was already 6:30pm now. I had [Stowaway Gourmet Chana Masala](https://www.trailspace.com/gear/stowaway-gourmet/chana-masala/) with plant-based [Y’all Bacon Bits](https://allyallsfoods.com/collections/plant-based-vegan-bacon-bits). It tasted soo good! More thru hikers had arrived now, a chatty atmosphere filled the area around the spring and more tents were set up. The sun was going down and it started to cool as well. A total of 8 thru hikers ended up spending the night at the spring, seven guys and one woman. By 8:30pm, everyone was in their tent and it was quiet. 9pm is considered hiker midnight after all. Exhausted from the day, I also fell asleep before 9pm.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt. A longer stretch of rugged dirt road with lots of rocks. Exposed to the sun with no shade for the majority of the hike. Lots of wind turbines in the beginning and towards the end of the hike.

**Parking:** There is room for 3 cars just off South Kelso Valley Road.

**Crowds:** Absolute solitude. Didn’t meet a single hiker during the hike.

**Weather:** Sunny throughout the entire hike with a light to moderate intermittent breeze. Low of 60°F / 16°C in the morning at the start of the hike. High of 83°F / 28°C around 2pm on the ascent at the head of Pine Tree Canyon.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Mostly private land. Some land managed by the Bureau of Land Management. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.3

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Moderate  
Logistics: Moderate
