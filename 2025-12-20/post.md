# Three Points Trailhead to Mill Creek Summit

Dec 20, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.343440, -117.982985](https://maps.app.goo.gl/MRHDMsFsoFmtrTLL7) (Three Points Trailhead)  
**End:** [34.391734, -118.081226](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Wof98oBFBHZGWe7V7) (Mill Creek Summit)

**Distance:** 18.4 mi (up to PCT mile 418.6, 15.8% completed)  
**Time:** 7 hours 26 minutes (start 7:08am, end 2:34pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 3,145 ft (low 4,910 ft – high 7,124 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 4,163 ft (start 5,928 ft – end 4,910 ft)

---

After spending the night in Palmdale, I arrived at Mill Creek Summit at 6:06am. This was the end of today’s hike and I was back in an area with Uber coverage. My driver was Melvin and he arrived in his Tesla shortly before the planned reservation time at 6:30am. There was no cellphone reception at the summit, so I had to give him directions. We chatted about hiking and wildlife as he was driving down Angeles Forest Highway and Upper Big Tujunga Road. It was bound to be a beautiful sunrise today with hues of orange and red. One thing that Melvin had not accounted for however, was the significant elevation gain. When he had picked me up, his Tesla’s battery was at 21%. He turned off his lights to save power and told me he would have to turn around at 15%. That was still a few miles from the Three Points Trailhead. When we reached the trailhead, he was at 14%. I even told him to drop me off right on the highway rather than going the extra way to the parking lot. I hope he made it back safely.

From Three Points Trailhead, I headed north on a granite-sand hillside then contouring west. I could see the highest point of my hike ahead: Pacifico Mountain. But first, I had to find my way across the small chaparralled ridges in front of me. The PCT kept on descending towards Sulphur Springs Trail Camp before turning west and following a wider canyon uphill. I crossed Little Rock Canyon Road 5N04. The trail now headed up a brushy ravine, then circled northwest to zigzag in and out of numerous smaller ravines. The views kept getting better. To the south, I could see Alder Saddle, to the northwest was Winston Ridge.

I reached Bare Mountain Canyon going straight north. The PCT wound around its southern head and then started ascending the eastern flank of Pacifico Mountain. Views across the canyon towards Bare Mountain were rewarding. This area had burned during the 2009 Station Fire and while parts of the landscape had been spared, other parts were still very bare. The ascent was demanding, I passed seeping Fiddleneck Spring and moved in and out of numerous gullies. Pacifico Mountain’s north ridge was still covered in Jeffrey pines and I stopped for another break with views north towards Santiago Canyon and Palmdale. The air was cold up here and there was a steady breeze which made it feel even colder.

The PCT turned southwest, descending on the bare northern flank of Pacifico Mountain. In very shielded spots, there were still some small patches of snow. Reaching a shady gap, I left the PCT to take a side trip up Pacifico Mountain. I had a choice between a very steep social trail and a more graded, but also longer dirt road. I opted for the dirt road. The views south were spectacular. The Alder Creek drainage was right in front of me and I could see numerous mountain ranges in the distance. I made it to the top of Pacifico Mountain which is a campground with 8 campsites. The views were truly stunning. I could see Palmdale and Lancaster very clearly. The Sierra Pelona range – which I would cross on the PCT – was to the west. I soaked in the panoramas before making my way back down.

![Aliso Canyon (34°23’36.576″ N 118°3’30.906″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9617-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

On the PCT again, the trail stuck to the northern slope in the shade. Fairly cool, but with great views north towards the desert. Rounding southwest, the PCT entered Tie Canyon. I could already see Mill Creek Summit in the distance, but it was still several miles to go. The canyon was bare, impacted by the Station Fire and the trail was moving in and out of every gully. Somewhat exhausted from the last two days of hiking, I finally reached the summit and got back to my car.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Snow free throughout, but with some very small leftover patches on the northern side of Pacifico Mountain. Significant impact from the 2009 Station Fire throughout most of the hike.

**Parking:** There is plenty of parking at Mill Creek Summit.

**Crowds:** Fairly solitary. Met 3 trail runners and two day hikers, plus a large group of Korean seniors on Pacifico Mountain.

**Weather:** Mostly sunny with blue skies with some cirrus clouds. Low of 54°F / 12°C in the morning. High of 80°F / 27°C on the southern side of Pacifico Mountain.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Angeles National Forest. San Gabriel Mountains National Monument. A portion within Pleasant View Ridge Wilderness. Annual Interagency Permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.8

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Easy
