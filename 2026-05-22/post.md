# Walker Pass to Bird Spring Pass

May 22, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [35.662919, -118.026847](https://maps.app.goo.gl/qoXghZhHqLCHAJhQ8) (Walker Pass Trailhead)  
**End:** [35.552949, -118.133441](https://maps.app.goo.gl/okfgnHFBZG7zu3Aq6) (Bird Spring Pass)

This is a reverse hike to reduce elevation gain on the way back.

**Distance:** 22.1 mi (up to PCT mile 653.2, 24.6% completed)  
**Time:** 9 hours 23 minutes (start 7:04am, end 4:28pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 3,372 ft (low 5,099 ft – high 7,023 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 3,292 ft (start 5,274 ft – end 5,355 ft)

---

With Memorial Day weekend coming up and temperatures gradually climbing, I figured I would break my usual 2-week rhythm and hike the next section of PCT earlier. Only the next section was logistically quite involved. The shortest the cycling path would have been 25 miles across dirt roads and CA-178 with an elevation gain of more than 2,000 feet – clearly too much after what was bound to be a 22 mile hike. So I had come up with an interesting logistical mix of transportation modes. The plan was to drop my bike at Bird Spring Pass in the same spot as the previous hike, then drive to Weldon on a mostly downhill course and take public transportation from Weldon to Walker Pass. The only caveat here was that Kern Transit bus 227 was only running on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays, so I had to do this hike on a Friday.

![Walker Pass Memorial (35°39’46.1113″ N 118°1’36.6942″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A2843-1024x683.jpg)

![Pinyon Peak from Walker Pass (35°39’49.7377″ N 118°1’47.6217″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2852-1024x683.jpg)

I made my way up to Bird Spring Pass on Thursday evening. The memory of the dirt road was still fresh, so it was somewhat easier to drive up the rugged road. I got to the pass at 8:24pm and it was almost completely dark already. While I was unloading my bike and chaining it to the wooden railing, I heard a zipper and sure enough there was a tent just 15 feet from me from a thru hiker. He curiously asked what I was doing and after I explained my logistical routine he mentioned that he had done something similar for the Long Trail in Vermont. With complete darkness almost upon me, I quickly got back into my car to make my way down the pass. Unlike last time, there was almost no wind today and that opened up new possibilities for where I could spend the night. Originally, I had considered a wind shielded little nook at the junction of South Kelso Valley Road and Piute Mountain Road, but now I might as well camp along Bird Spring Canyon Road.

![The mountains north of Walker Pass with Mount Jenkins in the distance (35°39’24.744″ N 118°2’34.662″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A2863-1024x683.jpg)

![Jacks Creek Canyon (35°39’18.84″ N 118°3’32.886″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A2872-1024x683.jpg)

A spot right at the junction of Bird Spring Canyon Road and Kelso Valley Road seemed reasonably level and so I parked next to a board with a map. Getting my roof top tent set up felt almost like a routine already. The inside was indeed completely level this time and I had figured out how to get my LED light strip working as well. I opened up both sides of the tent to let in some cooler air and had a dinner sandwich. It was a clear night and I could see lots of stars and a very bright moon. I fell asleep to peaceful quietness. Two roosters woke me up well before my alarm at 5:10am. I couldn’t manage to go back to sleep, so I started packing up slowly. It was 5:30am when I left my camping spot and made my way back to Weldon. I parked next to a fenced solar power plant at the junction of Kelso Creek Road and Highway 178. This was also right next to the Power’s Track bus station. The 227 to Ridgecrest was scheduled for 6:25am.

![Western Salsify, also known as Giant Desert Dandelion (35°38’38.862″ N 118°3’5.1″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A2883-1024x683.jpg)

![Mount Jenkins sticking out of the ridge to the north (35°38’18.618″ N 118°2’55.134″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A2897-1024x683.jpg)

I got into hiking gear and packed two extra bottles of water in anticipation of a hot and long day, then made sure I had the $3 fare handy and waited next to the bus stop sign. The sun had come up half an hour ago, but the mountains were still throwing long shadows. At 6:21am, a Kern Transit bus approached rapidly but didn’t stop. The display had said “Dial-A-Ride”, but how likely would it be that a second bus was going to pass at almost the exact same time? When it turned 6:25am and no bus was in sight, I started to lose hope. I guessed that had been my bus and now I would need to hitchhike when there was almost no traffic yet. Not a great start into the day. At 6:32am, I saw another vehicle coming and lo and behold, it was the 227 bus to Ridgecrest. I had been too impatient. I got on the bus and asked the driver to stop at Walker Pass, then paid my fare. There were only two other riders on the bus. A woman asked me where I was from. The bus ride took about half an hour past Onyx and Canebrake. I was enjoying the scenery and trying to figure out where exactly the PCT was going to take me on one of the next hikes.

![Desert Canterbury Bells (35°38’10.698″ N 118°2’58.548″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A2905-1024x683.jpg)

![Western Fence Lizard (35°38’5.808″ N 118°3’57.498″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A2925-1024x683.jpg)

The bus reached Walker Pass at 6:58am. Two thru hikers were already waiting, likely eager to take a zero day in Ridgecrest. I got off the bus and walked to the Walker Pass Memorial, then set foot on the PCT again. The trail headed gently downhill northwest, then southwest. Owens Peak was looming to my right in the distance and I could get a glimpse of the High Sierra peaks I would be reaching eventually. Just a few hundred yards from the trail was Walker Pass Campground, right behind a hair pin curve of the highway. There was a trail register at the junction with a spur trail leading down to the campground. I signed the usual way with a reference to this blog and browsed a bit through the previous pages. This year, I am following three YouTubers documenting their PCT hike north and I could tell from the entries that exactly a week ago, [DogFoot](https://www.youtube.com/@dogfoottrailing1770) and [Minion](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCaS6ajtCc1nrIRypNYwDCiA) had passed through here. I felt an even deeper connection now.

![McIvers Cabin (35°37’18.738″ N 118°4’23.388″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A2934-1024x683.jpg)

![Horse Canyon (35°34’50.7012″ N 118°7’24.0306″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A2955-1024x683.jpg)

The downhill stretch ended after passing dry Canebrake Creek. Ahead of me was a long ascent now. The PCT headed for a saddle to the west, switchbacking multiple times with great views north towards Owens Peak Wilderness, then continued to climb further along the eastern side of Jacks Creek Canyon. The trail was still in the shade here and offered temperatures around 70°F / 21°C which I appreciated during this ascent. The canyon was nicely covered in pine trees. Cresting another saddle towards Boulder Canyon, the scenery changed dramatically. This area had burned during the 1997 Jacks Fire and was only covered with thick chaparral brush and Ceanothus thickets rather than pine forest. I had my first break in the shadow of a three story granite boulder.

![A canyon northwest of Skinner Peak (35°34’48.984″ N 118°7’53.142″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A2969-1024x683.jpg)

![Kelso Valley in the distance (35°34’31.662″ N 118°7’47.862″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A2976-1024x683.jpg)

Rounding the head of Boulder Canyon with glimpses down to the Mojave Desert, the PCT reached McIvers Road. Just a few hundred yards to my left was the historic McIvers Cabin and McIvers Spring, so I decided to take this little detour before continuing on the PCT. A northbound thru hiker caught up to me halfway there and another thru hiker was already there filtering water. The dilapidated cabin was situated among fairly picturesque granite slabs, built in 1938 as a rustic retreat. I took a few photos but didn’t stay long. Ahead of me was a 2 mile exposed dirt road walk. The road went up and down several times but there were no stunning views at any point. Eventually, the PCT left the dirt road and I was back on regular trail tread.

![Clouds above the Scodie Mountains with Horse Canyon Road ahead (35°34’4.506″ N 118°7’30.93″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A2983-1024x683.jpg)

![Horse Canyon (35°33’53.754″ N 118°7’20.748″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A2995-1024x683.jpg)

For the next few miles, the trail stay fairly level but followed the contour lines in and out of every side canyon. I was back in pine forest which provided welcome shade. Crossing two closed dirt roads turned trail I reached another saddle at the head of Horse Canyon. Ahead of me was a long uphill to Skinner Peak. Fortunately, there were plenty of amazing views along the way. To the east was wide Horse Canyon with Horse Canyon Road zigzagging up the northern slope. To the west was upper Cane Canyon. The last mile uphill was dragging, but I eventually made it. The trail moved to the north side of Skinner Peak with more views down Horse Canyon.

![Switchbacks southeast of Skinner Peak (35°33’40.914″ N 118°7’11.37″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A3012-1024x683.jpg)

![Wyleys Knob and Bird Spring Pass (35°33’40.89″ N 118°7’11.334″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A3014-1024x683.jpg)

Alas, it was time to descend again. Ahead of me were several miles dropping me 1,600 feet to Bird Spring Pass. The PCT followed short switchbacks then long switchbacks. The wind had picked up a bit and it was partly cloudy now. The views were still breathtaking. To the south was Bird Spring Canyon now with Wyleys Knob on the other side. When I reached Bird Spring Pass, there were two thru hikers filling up on water and an older man was sitting on a bench next to a very large water cache of 50+ barrels of water with 5 gallons each. His trail name was Legend and we chatted for a bit. He was waiting for his wife who was doing a 30 mile day on the PCT but he had also walked the entire trail himself before. He was very friendly and asked about my story as well. After half an hour, it was time for me to cycle back and I said goodbye to Legend.

![Wyleys Knob from a canyon just below Skinner Peak (35°33’24.684″ N 118°7’30.732″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A3047-1024x683.jpg)

![Approaching Bird Spring Pass (35°33’14.232″ N 118°7’47.034″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/281A3058-1024x683.jpg)

This hike also marks the end of California Section F of the Pacific Crest Trail. From Highway 58 to Walker Pass, I hiked 86.7 miles through a fairly dry and remote stretch of the PCT. The logistical challenges have certainly increased but I am also incredibly excited to finally be in the Sierra Nevada. Leaving the Scodie Mountains behind me, I am now entering Owens Peak Wilderness, getting a first taste of the higher Sierra mountains.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt, occasionally quite sandy. Long exposed stretches with little shade. The area between Boulder Creek (just north of McIvers Cabin) and Skinner Peak burned during the 1997 Jacks Fire and is now covered with thick chaparral brush and Ceanothus thickets rather than pine forest.

**Parking:** There is room plenty of room to park at the junction of Kelso Creek Road and Highway 178.

**Crowds:** Lightly traveled. Met 52 northbound thru hikers throughout the day and one day hiker / runner near Horse Canyon Road.

**Weather:** Sunny with no clouds in the morning, then partly cloudy in the afternoon. Light to moderate intermittent breeze past noon. Low of 65°F / 19°C around at 8:20am on the shaded eastern side of Jacks Creek Canyon. High of 95°F / 35°C around at 2:40pm during the ascent to Skinner Peak. Humidity between 37% in the morning and 18% in the afternoon.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Sequoia National Forest and Kiavah Wilderness. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.5

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Difficult

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 16.1 mi (1 hour 20 minutes), 17 ft elevation gain, 12.03 mph average speed, 27.28 mph maximum speed

The bike ride back was less exhausting than I had imagined, partly because it was already 5pm and temperatures were a bit more bearable. The first 5 miles was the now very familiar Bird Spring Canyon Road. I could still remember which parts I had to take more carefully. Turning onto paved Kelso Valley Road was a relief but I still had more than 10 miles ahead of me and the wind was blowing towards me. Fortunately, it was mostly downhill and for the very brief uphill stretches, I could use my downhill momentum to get over them. When I finally reached my car, I was tired and exhausted, but also happy and thankful for a great day.
