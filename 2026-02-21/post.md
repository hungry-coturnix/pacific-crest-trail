# CA-138 to Liebre Sawmill Road

Feb 21, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.776588, -118.607031](https://maps.app.goo.gl/HTYMFertGCZN7Aom9) (269th Street West & CA-138)  
**End:** [34.696801, -118.541225](https://maps.app.goo.gl/mYyf23nPafjtaRDQ7) (Liebre Sawmill Road)

This is a reverse hike to reduce elevation gain on the way back.

**Distance:** 23.6 mi (up to PCT mile 517.6, 19.5% completed)  
**Time:** 10 hours 17 minutes (start 6:58am, end 5:15pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 5,573 ft (low 3,040 ft – high 5,742 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 3,965 ft (start 3,040 ft – end 4,648 ft)

---

If I had to summarize the day in one sentence, I would say that it was a beautiful day with an unexpected amount of snow and a maddening surprise at the end. Maddening because my bike was stolen. Well, not exactly stolen, but half stolen, half vandalized. Keep reading for the full story.

![Looking back towards the snow capped Tehachapi Mountains (34°46’2.058″ N 118°36’28.182″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0292-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Blue Lupine growing along the Pacific Crest Trail (34°45’16.446″ N 118°37’52.392″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0325-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I wanted to do one more PCT hike before my trip to South America. Another major winter storm had swept through California, dumping a copious amount of precipitation and snow in the mountains. The weather forecast was sunny but cold. My plan was to drop off my bike right at the junction of the PCT with Liebre Sawmill Road. The dirt road was in very good condition when I had walked it last time, so I was confident I could drive all the way up there with my low clearance car. I had left the hotel in Frazier Park at 5:32am and it was dawning when I left the pavement to drive up Liebre Sawmill Road. The ground was frozen solid, but if anything that was helpful to avoid mud. After about a mile, snow started appearing on the road, not a scary amount, but certainly something that was worrisome. I evaluated my options. The best plan was to drive only 2 miles to a junction with the dirt road leading to Upper Shake Campground and just chain my bike to the gate. Looking ahead, the snow was increasing, so that was a good call.

![The snow covered Tehachapi Mountains beyond the rolling hills of Antelope Valley (34°45’14.808″ N 118°37’58.47″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0326-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![A sag pond just beyond Pine Canyon Road (34°44’10.992″ N 118°38’48.222″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0343-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

After returning to paved Pine Canyon Road, I made my way back to Highway 138 and parked on side of 269th Street West. The sun had come up now and the temperature was above freezing. But what really caught my eye were the snow covered Tehachapi Mountains to the north. The storm had really brought a fair amount of snow and it was incredibly beautiful to see the mountains in white. I left my car shortly before 7am, heading south across the highway. I was back on the PCT now. There were a bunch of tumbleweeds on the trail. The PCT headed to the mountains in an arrow straight line. There was hoarfrost on the grass. I kept looking back to soak in the view of the snow covered mountains. The next 7 miles were across Tejon Ranch property with a private easement for the PCT. The trail was moving up and down and in and out of various ravines.

![The Mojave Desert from Liebre Mountain with the Tehachapi Mountains in the distance (34°43’20.136″ N 118°39’14.94″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0363-Pano-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x455.jpg)

![The fresh snow blanket on Liebre Mountain (34°43’4.518″ N 118°39’11.7″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0370-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Rounding yet another ridge, I spotted five Pronghorn Antelopes on the other side of the canyon. They had apparently also caught wind of me and were darting away. Pronghorn Antelopes were hunted to extinction in the area in the mid-20th century, but then reintroduced in 1987. Unfortunately, they were too far away for any photos. I took a break on a big log near Cow Spring Canyon. The PCT crossed Pine Canyon Road and headed west. I passed a seasonal sag pond and scared a few ducks. Passing Liebre Mountain Trailhead, the switchbacks started. And soon enough they were covered in a blanket of snow. Initially, that didn’t slow me down, but getting higher on Liebre Mountain, there was more snow and eventually it was slow going. I took another break near Horse Camp to catch my breadth from the relentless ascent. Finally at the top of Liebre Mountain, the views were stunning. A thick snow blanket right in front of me with snow covered trees. The barren desert as the backdrop. And the snow covered Tehachapi Mountains in the distance.

![Blue mountain ridges in the distance (34°42’43.758″ N 118°38’4.404″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0383-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Bear Canyon (34°42’35.964″ N 118°37’28.38″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0387-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I was glad to reach the highest point of the hike. The PCT stayed on the northern side of the mountain for some time. That meant a more solid snow blanket to walk on but also fewer views through the oak covered hillside. The area south of the ridge had been heavily impacted by the 2020 Lake Fire, but the northern hillside was unscathed. The PCT crossed Liebre Mountain Road and entered the burn scar. Not surprisingly, there was also a decent amount of Poodle Dog Bush here. The views were more unobstructed though with ridge after ridge in the distance. After a a few miles of descent, the trail was going back up and I was panting a little. Finally at the top, the views east were magnificent. Across from North Fork Fish Canyon were Burnt Peak and Sawmill Mountain. And in the distance were the snow capped San Gabriel Mountains.

![Burnt Peak and Sawmill Mountain with the snow covered San Gabriel Mountains in the distance (34°42’9.1883″ N 118°36’42.0971″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0398-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The Mojave Desert beyond Sawmill Mountain (34°42’17.796″ N 118°36’0.036″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0415-Pano-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x416.jpg)

The trail started descending rapidly and with it the snow blanket became more manageable and disappeared entirely after crossing Atmore Meadow Road. It was a warmer and sheltered pocket at the head of North Fork Fish Canyon. A small sign next to the trail was designating the spot as the 500 mile marker since the Mexican border. I was proud to have come this far. Crossing Liebre Sawmill Road once more, the PCT was back on the oak covered northern side with few views. And there was snow again. I passed a spur trail to Sawmill Campground and two miles later reached the little plateau where I had left the PCT last time. There was an inch of snow on the ground here, so it had been a good idea to not drive further. It was almost 5pm now, so I picked up the pace for the last mile down the mountain so that I would have enough daylight for the bike ride back.

![500 miles on the PCT (34°42’34.2967″ N 118°35’35.8959″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0429-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![A “beautiful” specimen of poisonous poodle dog bush (34°42’31.7884″ N 118°35’17.7594″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0434-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

When I reached my bike, I was in shock. The back wheel was missing. Not just that, someone had tried to open the lock with brute force. It looked like they had attached a strap to the lock and pulled it with a truck. The strap had torn in the process and was now wedged into the lock. So much so, that I couldn’t even get a key back into the lock. I was devastated. Why would someone try to steal a bike in the middle of nowhere?! And when it was clear that the lock was too strong, why would they take the back wheel?! This was really just evil. I was very disappointed by humankind.

![The Tehachapi Mountains from Sawmill Mountain (34°42’24.366″ N 118°35’3.558″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0441-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![What was left of my bike … (34°41’48.1856″ N 118°32’28.0443″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0449-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The sun had set and it was getting dark, so there was not much time to strategize. There was also no cellphone reception here. My only option was to hike back down to Pine Canyon Road and hope that a merciful driver would stop and give me a ride. 2 miles later, I was back on paved Pine Canyon Road. Not a single car passed within 10 minutes. The first passing car didn’t stop despite my waving desperately. It was almost completely dark now, so I took out my head lamp and turned on the blinking LEDs on my bike helmet. Another 10 minutes later, another truck approached and luckily, they stopped. The driver was a bit reluctant at first, but when I told him my bike had been stolen, he agreed to give me a ride back to my car. He asked me to hop in the back where there was a bunch of cut fire wood he was hauling. I found a somewhat comfortable position and he drove off. Then a quarter mile later, he stopped again. He told me he felt bad and asked me to sit in the back of his car. I offered that he could frisk me to be sure I had no weapons on me and he did. Driving back, we warmed up. He was also a software engineer, working for NASA. We chatted about what had happened and my PCT adventures. His name was Rami and he was originally from Palestine. It was a very friendly conversation. When he finally dropped me off at my car, I was incredibly thankful. With no food or water left, this would have been a torturous adventure to get back to my car.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt. Slightly overgrown at times. A few blowdowns. Lots of Poodle Dog Bush between Liebre Mountain and Atmore Meadow Road. Snow starting at 4,000 ft just south of Liebre Mountain Trailhead. About 5 inches of snow on Liebre Mountain. The area south of Liebre Sawmill Road was impacted significantly by the 2020 Lake Fire.

**Parking:** There is room for two cars along 269th St W.

**Crowds:** Almost complete solitude. Only met one backpacker near Atmore Meadow Road who said he hadn’t seen anyone for two days.

**Weather:** Sunny throughout the entire day with a light to moderate intermittent breeze. Low of 38°F / 3°C in the morning at the start of the hike after sunrise. High of 58°F / 15°C in a sheltered corner at the head of North Fork Fish Canyon.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Angeles National Forest. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 2.9

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Moderate  
Logistics: Difficult
