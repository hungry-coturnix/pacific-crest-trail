# Acorn Trail to Inspiration Point

Nov 28, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.372295, -117.709844](https://maps.app.goo.gl/bKJZTcFbcTB64PhW7) (Blue Ridge Trailhead)  
**End:** [34.372295, -117.709844](https://maps.app.goo.gl/bKJZTcFbcTB64PhW7) (Blue Ridge Trailhead)

**Distance:** 11.0 mi (up to PCT mile 369.4, 13.9% completed)  
**Time:** 6 hours 27 minutes (start 6:29am, end 12:57pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 1,881 ft (low 7,363 ft – high 8,476 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 1,881 ft (start 7,364 ft – end 7,364 ft)

---

Today would be the second attempt to hike along Blue Ridge, but this time with snow shoes and as a shorter loop (remember: snow hiking is a lot more exhausting, even with snow shoes). I was going to take Blue Ridge Truck Trail all the way back to where I had made the hard decision last time to descend via Acorn Trail. And from there I would take the PCT back to the car.

![Sunrise over the San Gabriel River drainage (34°22’14.514″ N 117°42’16.734″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8963-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I arrived at the trailhead at 6:21am, about 15 minutes before sunrise. I was the first car at Inspiration Point. And it was surprisingly warm at 44°F / 7°C for this early hour. The initial stretch of Blue Ridge Truck Trail was completely snow free, so I carried the snow shoes over my backpack. I would try to walk without them for as long as I could. Snow covered Mount Baden-Powell was to my right, but the snowline was significantly higher than last Sunday. Snow covered Mount Baldy was up ahead but still in the shade. After the first half mile, small patches of snow appeared and soon became a more solid blanket. The snow was mostly still frozen which made it a lot easier to walk and kept me from sinking in deeply.

![Blue Ridge Express Ski Lift (34°22’8.352″ N 117°41’29.226″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8973-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Mount Baden-Powell (34°21’3.534″ N 117°40’41.448″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A8995-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Soon enough, I crossed the first ski slope. It had been groomed very recently. The road stayed mostly on the northern side of the ridge which meant a lot more snow. But it was still easy to walk in just boots. I crossed two more ski slopes. The top of Mount Baden-Powell was getting a lot of sunshine already. Past Blue Ridge Campground, there was more snow on the road, but I still managed to continue without snow shoes. The road passed a fenced water reservoir and continued uphill on the southern side of the ridge. The views south towards Los Angeles were breathtaking. I could clearly see the path I had taken last Sunday.

![Pine Mountain Ridge and San Antonio Ridge (34°20’34.434″ N 117°39’55.824″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9007-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Snow drifts on Blue Ridge Truck Trail (34°20’30.816″ N 117°39’50.112″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9010-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

At 4.5 miles on the northern side of the ridge at 8,400 feet, it was finally time to put on the snow shoes. There were now snow drifts of two to three feet which meant that I would sink in quite a bit. The next mile passed quickly, mostly downhill, leaving the spur road to Guffy Campground on my left and eventually passing the junction with Forest Service Route 3N39 that I had taken last time. My boot tracks were still clearly visible. But in just 5 days, the snow had become much less, there were even patches without snow! I reached the PCT junction at 5.5 miles. The rest of the way was back on the PCT but mostly on the northern side of the ridge, so I was anticipating more snow drifts.

![Snow and desert within close proximity (34°20’26.97″ N 117°39’12.498″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9024-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Snow covered Mount Baldy looming in the distance (34°21’22.236″ N 117°40’59.13″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9045-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The first stretch was steeply uphill to Guffy Campground. It was easier with snow shoes but still exhausting. You could still make out the numbered campsites and a big water tank. I continued northwest. The trail was relatively easy to follow if you paid attention to the patterns in the snow, the (lack of) vegetation, and cut trees. Views to the desert in the north were a remarkable contrast. The next few miles were slow. The trail was passing through the burn scar of the 2024 Bridge Fire and a lot of black branches had fallen onto the snow. Sometimes the snow was fluffy, sometimes it was covered in branches or shards of ice that had fallen from trees. The section about half a mile from the fenced water reservoir was by far the hardest. It was a shady northern slope and while it was obvious where the trail was, there was no “level” ledge, instead the crusty snow was at a steep sideways angle. Every step had to be very careful, ensuring that I had enough grip with my snow shoes. The muscles in my legs and feet were very tense. A wrong move could be fatal here.

![Upper Chisolm ski slope (34°22’2.25″ N 117°41’44.106″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9055-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I eventually passed this crazy section after what felt like an eternity and was back on Blue Ridge Truck Trail for a very short while. I took off the snow shoes and had some snacks. The PCT went up the ridge again and I had to use my offline map to make sure I was still walking on the “trail”. The next half mile was heavily logged after the Bridge Fire and the usual signs of the trail were absent. I charted a way around felled trees and eventually found the trail again. Across another water reservoir, I could see a busy ski lift. Soon after the trail skirted the ski slope and I watched the skiers and snowboarders for a bit.

![The San Gabriel River drainage (34°22’3.102″ N 117°41’44.772″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9057-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I passed another ski lift that wasn’t in operation and the trail was now alternating between snow free and snow covered. Crossing Blue Ridge Truck Trail once more, the PCT made a big zigzag. There were a lot more boot tracks now. I passed a spot where a number of people were sledding. The last quarter mile was easy and snow free. I was back at Inspiration Point.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Snow free for the first half mile, then covered in solid snow. Significant impact from the 2024 Bridge Fire, some stretches are very bare or scarred with black trees.

**Parking:** There is plenty of parking on Inspiration Point.

**Crowds:** Very solitary. Only met a few people within half a mile of the trailhead.

**Weather:** Sunny with blue skies and no clouds throughout the entire day. Low of 39°F / 4°C in the morning on the northern side of Blue Ridge. High of 57°F / 14°C around noon on the snow free portion of the PCT.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Angeles National Forest. San Gabriel Mountains National Monument. Annual Interagency Permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.2

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Very easy
