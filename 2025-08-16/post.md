# Doble to Coxey Road

Aug 16, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.302287, -116.823104](https://maps.app.goo.gl/ZauHfSUhpzqQLQ8n9) (Holcomb Valley Road)  
**End:** [34.288531, -116.973571](https://maps.app.goo.gl/mQmCtZkYYywXtEqz5) (Hanna Flats Campground)

**Distance:** 23.0 mi (up to PCT mile 285.9, 10.8% completed)  
**Time:** 9 hours 30 minutes (start 6:21am, end 3:52pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 7,873 ft (low 6,531 ft – high 8,208 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 7,586 ft (start 6,877 ft – end 7,164 ft)

---

Just two days after my last hike, a wildfire broke out a mere 3 miles away from the Pacific Crest Trail on the next section. The [webcam](https://ops.alertcalifornia.org/cam-console/2688) on Onyx Peak showed a large smoke dome north of Big Bear Lake. The Gold Fire had started north of Holcomb Valley near John Bull Flat. I anxiously read the daily updates on [InciWeb](https://inciweb.wildfire.gov/incident-information/cabdf-2025-bdf-gold-fire), fearing that I would not be able to continue my PCT hike any time soon – either due to smoke, the fire itself, or a post-fire forest closure. Luckily the firefighter response was swift and effective. Within just a few days, smoke was no longer visible on the webcam and the updates indicated that containment was growing. On the precipice of today’s hike, the fire had been 85% contained. To help with recovery, a [forest closure order](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/sanbernardino/alerts/gold-fire-area-closure-including-john-bull-trail) had been put in place and I was lucky that it did not include any portion of the PCT. It did however include several access roads, but the PCT was accessible on either end of today’s hike.

Logistically, I had opted for a bike shuttle again. I would exit the PCT on Coxey Road and hike up the steep end to Hanna Flat Campground where I was going to drop off the bike. From the campground it was mostly downhill. Coxey Road was unpaved and I was a little bit worried about the road conditions since recreation.gov recommended a high clearance vehicle to get to the campground. But the dirt road was actually in excellent condition and getting to the campground – even in the dark – was easy. I had gotten up at 4:45am and left at 5:07am. I arrived at the campground at 5:32am. Finding a spot to lock my bike proved to be difficult though. All the trees in the area were either too small or had a trunk with too much circumference for my lock. I ended up picking a fence post right next to the road which didn’t look super sturdy but was going to do.

![Quartzite along Gold Mountain (34°17’25.326″ N 116°50’3.258″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6784-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I arrived at the starting point of the hike at 6:07am and parked in the exact same spot as last time. Sunrise was at 6:10am and I was soon able to witness the sun coming over the mountains. It was cold. The car thermometer said 34°F / 1°C and I only had a thin t-shirt, so I was yearning for the sun to warm me – a feeling that did not last too long given the heat of the day. The first half mile was through an area burned by the 2017 Holcomb Fire. Back in unaffected forest, I reached Doble Trail Camp, a small campground for PCT hikers with an open-air outhouse.

![Jacoby Canyon and the Mojave Desert (34°18’1.134″ N 116°50’32.652″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6788-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Continuing on, the trail zigzagged up the eastern side of Gold Mountain with great views of Baldwin Lake. Large amounts of quartzite along the trail were testament to the area’s history of hard-rock mining. Reaching a saddle at 2.6 miles, I was the closest to the fire closure, about a quarter mile. A few views down Jacoby Canyon and into the Mojave Desert were rewarding the first ascent of the day. The trail rounded Gold Mountain before turning west. I took my first break at 5.5 miles at Tanglewood Group Campground. The campground was closed since it was not reachable due to the forest closure and felt a bit eerie. Lying on one of the picnic tables, I stared into the sky and enjoyed the soundscape of this tranquil spot. I had brought my homemade mango fruit leather and it was actually quite yummy.

The trail headed down Van Dusen Canyon and after crossing dry Caribou Creek reached Van Dusen Canyon Road. I saw numerous fire fighting personnel driving up the road to continue their work suppressing the Gold Fire. On the other side, the trail moved closer to Bertha Ridge and reached a saddle on its western end. I left the PCT here to make my way up to Bertha Peak to enjoy the views of Big Bear Lake. I had not met a single person so far, but the dirt road up to the peak had a surprising amount of foot traffic. I guess I should not have been surprised since Bertha Peak is a mere 2.9 miles from Big Bear Lake via the Cougar Crest Trail and hence it is a popular weekend destination.

![Big Bear Lake during the ascent to Bertha Peak (34°16’54.7907″ N 116°54’15.7439″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6820-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Big Bear Lake, San Gorgonio Mountain in the distance (34°16’56.886″ N 116°53’57.084″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6833-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The dirt road up the peak was incredibly steep, but the views from the top were breath taking. To the south was Big Bear Lake (the lake itself and the town) with Sugarlump Mountain right behind and San Gorgonio Mountain in the far distance. To the west was Butler Peak, and to the north was Holcomb Valley. I was able to spot a very small area that still seemed to have faint smoke just below John Bull Flat. After enjoying the view for a little while, I made my way back down to the PCT. The next 0.4 miles of PCT were the first section of the PCT I had ever hiked when I went up to Bertha Peak via Cougar Crest Trail in 2017. The next few miles were somewhat uneventful with few views. The PCT crossed Poligue Canyon Road and then followed the eastern flank of Delamar Mountain. I decided to pass on the trail to the summit given how much distance I still had to cover.

![Big Bear Lake from the top of Bertha Peak (34°16’58.644″ N 116°53’57.948″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6836-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Holcomb Valley (34°16’59.16″ N 116°53’58.464″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6839-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Past Delamar Mountain Road, the trail entered an area burned by the 2007 Butler Fire along Holcomb Creek. Shrubs and grasses had reclaimed the area, but trees were far and few. As the trail contoured the southern side of the valley, I was longing for shade. The temperatures were around 102°F / 39°C in the exposed sun and my stamina started to dwindle. But another creature was loving the sun – a rattlesnake was sunbathing on the trail and quickly slithered away when it saw me approaching. I reached Little Bear Springs Trail Camp next to dry Holcomb Creek and took another break. A quarter mile further, the PCT crossed Coxey Road, my exit for today. But I still had 2 miles of exposed and steep ascent ahead of me to get back to my bike at Hanna Flat Campground. More than a dozen cars passed me on my way up. When I was just half a mile away from my bike, someone stopped and asked whether I wanted a ride. But my pride stood in the way so close to the finish line. Nonetheless, the last stretch was torturing and I could not appreciate the views anymore. When I reached my bike, I was relieved.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly in good condition. Dirt and loose rocks. The portion of the PCT between Delamar Mountain Road and Coxey Road is quite overgrown at times.

**Parking:** There is plenty of parking 50 yards north of the PCT next to the dump site.

**Weather:** Sunny with blue skies and no clouds. 46°F / 7°C in the morning at the beginning of the hike. High of 102°F / 39°C in the afternoon with an intermittent mild breeze.

**Land Manager & Permits:** San Bernardino National Forest. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.2

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Easy

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 13.4 mi (1 hour 11 min), 1,081 ft elevation gain, 11.28 mph average speed, 24.92 mph maximum speed

The bike ride back had more elevation gain than I had anticipated, but fortunately it was spaced out quite a bit so that I never had a long continuous uphill stretch to deal with. From Hanna Flat Campground, the dirt road went up a little before going into a long descent to Fawnskin. My pace was good and within just 15 minutes, I was on Highway 38 in Fawnskin. From here it was along the north shore of Big Bear Lake and Baldwin Lake. The road was going up and down quite a bit and I was able to leverage my kinetic energy from going downhill to make it up the next hill. Turning north along Baldwin Lake, I now had the wind in my face which made it slow going for 2 miles. The last mile was uphill on Holcomb Valley Road and it truly took my last strength to get back to the car.
