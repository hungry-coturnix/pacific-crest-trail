# CA-173 to Cleghorn Road

Oct 12, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.336679, -117.245834](https://maps.app.goo.gl/1rJx922SM88Cv4fG7) (Deep Creek Trailhead)  
**End:** [34.287168, -117.369121](https://maps.app.goo.gl/3BGjaH5fe6zVp7ac9) (Cleghorn Road, near the group campground)

**Distance:** 16.3 mi (up to PCT mile 329.5, 12.4% completed)  
**Time:** 6 hours 43 minutes (start 7:17am, end 2:00pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 6,326 ft (low 3,110 ft – high 3,774 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 5,935 ft (start 3,127 ft – end 3,518 ft)

---

As long as there was Uber coverage, I decided to use the luxury of a shuttle ride instead of cycling back. Today’s shuttle could have been a bike ride, but a car ride was a lot more convenient. Initially, my assigned Uber driver had been the same as the day before, but I was glad to see she had canceled and instead I had the pleasure of riding with Mark. He arrived shortly before 7am at my pickup spot, the end of the pavement on Cleghorn Road just past the Rio Baranca Valle Group Campground in Silverwood Lake State Recreation Area. Mark was a freelance video editor and told me about how people had lost touch with the real world and nature. People were only looking at things through their camera or on their phone instead of being present in the moment.

The ride only took 21 minutes. Mark dropped me off at Deep Creek Trailhead where I had left off yesterday. The long steep ridge on the northern side of Summit Valley was already seeing the morning sun while the southern ridge was still in the shade. As I left the trailhead I heard what I presumed to be a gun shot. And soon enough I saw a man with a dog not too far from the trail chase and shoot quail. I wouldn’t say I was afraid, but I was certainly uncomfortable being so close to someone shooting with a gun. I picked up the pace and headed for the ridge ahead which the trail was rapidly ascending. The reward was an amazing view across Summit Valley. It was a lot more obvious now that the ridge to the north had once been the edge of an early Pleistocene alluvial fan with sediments from the San Gabriel Mountains deposited on the sides.

The trail followed the outline of the mountains and headed into every little side canyon while still going up and down considerably. Crossing dry Grass Valley Creek, the trail gained elevation again and afforded me with more views north and south. Highway 173 was fairly close but way below. The next few miles continued to be meandering. At about 8 miles, my camera ran out of battery. I had thought about charging it the evening before, but given the spare battery in my backpack, I didn’t think it was necessary. The spare battery however was also empty. I was mad at myself for not checking that. It also meant that I had to take any remaining photos for today’s hike with my phone.

After crossing paved Forest Road 2N33, the trail headed steeply downhill and reached the bottom of Cedar Springs Dam on the northern side of Silverwood Lake. From here it was about half a mile along the highway passing the concrete spillway flume of the dam. Past the Mojave Siphon Power Plant, the trail resumed, headed up the brushy hillside, and entered Silverwood Lake State Recreation Area. At a saddle I was greeted by an amazing vista over Silverwood Lake. There were several boats out on the lake. The trail winded through chaparral on the western side of the lake. A portion of the park had burned a few months ago in June when the Lake Fire had scorched 489 acres in the area. The PCT crossed this burned area just west of the Chamise Boat-In Picnic Area. It felt like a surreal ghost forest. Only the charred stumps of bushes were still standing. Everything else was gone. No grass. No leaves. Just dark sand and black stumps.

Luckily, the picnic area was just outside of the burn zone. It offered several picnic tables in the shade. And there was nobody there. I took a longer break and soaked in the views across the lake. From here, the PCT headed uphill to offer even better views of the lake. Soon enough, the southern portion of Silverwood Lake with its marina came into view. The area around the lake was very pleasantly green. Getting closer to Cleghorn Picnic Area, the PCT entered the burn zone again. And it was a larger section than before. Well beyond the picnic area, the trail briefly became a bike path and headed underneath Highway 138. On the other side, trail tread resumed north of the park’s group camp. It eventually skirted Cleghorn Road very briefly. This is where I left the trail and walked the remaining quarter mile back to my car on the paved road.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt. The area between Deep Creek Trailhead and Silverwood Lake burned during the 2016 Pilot Fire and is fairly exposed to the sun. Two more sections around Silverwood Lake burned during the 2025 Lake Fire and have not yet begun recovering.

**Parking:** There is room for several cars off the pavement just past the group campground.

**Crowds:** Solitude. Didn’t meet a single person on trail and only saw a few people on boats from a distance on Silverwood Lake.

**Weather:** Sunny with blue skies and no clouds. Low of 46°F / 8°C in the morning at the beginning of the hike. High of 91°F / 33°C in the afternoon along Silverwood Lake.

**Land Manager & Permits:** San Bernardino National Forest and Silverwood Lake State Recreation Area. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.8

Difficulty: Moderate  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Very Easy
