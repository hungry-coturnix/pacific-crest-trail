# CA-94 to Lake Morena County Park

Oct 19, 2024 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [32.605475, -116.483161](https://maps.app.goo.gl/fmJE8ATA33TZVuEp9) (CA-94, just west of Campo, CA)  
**End:** [32.682581, -116.516672](https://maps.app.goo.gl/BenY4pFoVgkuwQxY6) (just outside of Lake Morena County Park)

**Distance:** 18.4 mi (up to PCT mile 20.0, 0.8% completed)  
**Time:** 8 hours 31 minutes (start 7:30am, end 4:01pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 3,355 ft (low 2,366 ft – high 3,591 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 2,752 ft (start 2,471 ft – end 3,074 ft)

---

I was giddy. This was the very first full day hiking on the PCT and also the first time I would have to bike back to my car. I was very excited to get started.

It was about half an hour past sunrise when I finally hit the trail. The first two miles were gentle ups and downs through the lower hills and crossing a railroad line. Campo Creek was dry as expected with a bit of poison oak nearby.

Past a nice campsite in a dry meadow, the trail started ascending Hauser Mountain on its eastern escarpment. Soon enough, the Hacienda del Florasol came into view, a beautiful meadow with a few oak trees.

![Railroad Crossing (32°36’25.9193″ N 116°29’20.6684″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/281A3762.jpg)

![Hacienda del Florasol (32°37’30.288″ N 116°30’27.402″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/281A3791.jpg)

At 6 miles, after a few switchbacks, I reached the mostly flat top of Hauser Mountain. The trail stayed fairly level for the next 1.5 miles, following the contour lines of Hauser Mountain, before starting to drop into Hauser Canyon and offering good views of Morena Butte on the other side.

The trail kept on descending to the bottom of Hauser Canyon through a long switchback on a little traveled dirt road. Hauser Creek was dry as well. After crossing Hauser Creek Road, the trail went up the southern side of Morena Butte. The incline was manageable and as I ascended, I was able to see the end of Barrett Lake, the reservoir further down in Hauser Canyon.

![Morena Butte across from Hauser Creek (32°39’5.976″ N 116°31’49.188″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/281A3803.jpg)

![View from Morena Butte (32°40’8.268″ N 116°32’11.322″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/281A3817.jpg)

I reached a saddle just southeast of Morena Butte and decided to attempt reaching the top of the butte. The faint trail was not too difficult to recognize but very heavily overgrown and steep which made it hard to keep going. After a quarter mile, which got me to a small rocky stretch, I decided to turn around without making it to the very top.

Back at the saddle, I turned north and continued the more gentle ascent to the last ridge for the day. I reached the highest point of this hike and was glad to have another downhill stretch ahead. Lake Morena soon greeted me with excellent views. As I descended, I could hear the noise from the campground in Lake Morena County Park – it was a prime weekend for camping. I reached the trailhead at 4pm to bike back to Campo.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Dirt and rock. The trail is a little overgrown in the first half which made it feel more like bushwhacking.

**Parking:** The dirt roads leaving CA-94 to the south are all private property. However, the shoulder is wide enough for a few cars to park right where the PCT crosses the highway.

**Weather:** Sunny, no clouds, with a high of 77°F (during the ascent to Morena Butte) and a low of 48°F (early in the morning, at the beginning of the hike). Breezy after 9am for most of the day.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Mostly Cleveland National Forest (including a stretch of Hauser Wilderness). No permits required.

**Overall rating:** 3.2

Difficulty: Moderate  
Scenery: Medium  
Logistics: Very Easy

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 7.0 mi (28 min), 238 ft elevation gain, 14.77 mph average

This was my first hike with a bike shuttle back to the car. Dropping off the bike at the end of the hike was straightforward. A number of wooden posts were at the trailhead to lock the bike. I reached the trailhead at 6:54am which was also the exact time of sunrise.

Biking back was easier than I had thought although still exhausting. A few uphill stretches were testing my stamina. But given the moderate elevation gain, it was not too bad. I was also lucky that the entire route back was on paved roads.
