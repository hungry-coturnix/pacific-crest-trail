# Olancha Pass Trail to Trail Pass (Day 2)

Aug 2, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Central California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/central-california/)

**Start:** [36.275525, -118.168028](https://maps.app.goo.gl/XE7tv2cJCfpdCY8h7) (Brown Cow Camp)  
**End:** [36.332301, -118.133937](https://maps.app.goo.gl/TALQ4XboT169h76QA) (Campsite north of Death Canyon)

**Distance:** 18.8 mi (up to PCT mile 733.3, 27.7% completed)  
**Time:** 12 hours 2 minutes (start 6:13am, end 6:16pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 4,009 ft (low 7,970 ft – high 10,555 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 3,245 ft (start 8,742 ft – end 9,506 ft)

---

I had woken up a few times during the night being thirsty, but I remained intent on rationing my water. With a few miles to hike in the morning before reaching the next reliable water source, conserving every drop was critical. Inside the tent, the ambient temperature hadn’t dropped below 51°F / 11°C, though it was a crisper 45°F / 7°C outside. Thankfully, the mosquitoes from the previous evening had vanished. To save water, I skipped hot tea and deferred a bigger breakfast to later, having only a cereal bar and some trail mix. Brown Meadow lay completely dark, quiet, and still – not a single leaf stirred. My hiking shoes had dried overnight, though their stench was horrible (something to deal with back home). The second day would transition me from the gentle, sub-alpine basins of the Kern Plateau up onto the high, exposed spine of the southern Sierra Nevada. With over 4,000 feet of climbing, this very strenuous leg would pass below the iconic bulk of Olancha Peak, navigate sparse late-summer water sources, and end at a dramatic campsite high above Death Canyon. Packing up quickly, I set out by 6:13am to make as much progress as possible in the cool morning shade before the sun took over. With my water bottle completely empty, reaching a water source was my top priority. Hessian Meadow lay two miles ahead, though whether its creek was carrying water was anyone’s guess.

![The USFS administrative site north of Monache Meadow (36°13’43.374″ N 118°9’43.164″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4587-1024x683.jpg)

The trail climbed immediately out of Brown Cow Camp toward a saddle directly east of Brown Mountain. As the sun crept up, the mosquitoes returned quickly, prompting me to pull up my sun hood for neck protection. From the saddle, the path dropped steeply into Hessian Meadow. Unfortunately, my fears were confirmed: the creek bed was bone dry. The next hope for water was Monache Creek, another two miles ahead near the northern edge of Monache Meadow. Though the sun was higher now, the trail remained pleasantly shaded and cool. Shortly before Monache Creek, I reached a USFS administrative site – the Monache Guard Station – tucked into the timber along the northern rim of Monache Meadow, the largest meadow in the Sierra Nevada. As a backcountry outpost, the site features a rustic, field-use cabin, allowing rangers and wilderness guards to monitor the heavy 4WD and OHV recreational use, stock grazing, and wilderness boundaries around the South Fork Kern River. A well-graded dirt road led right up to the cabin. I hadn’t realized until now that this area was actually not designated wilderness but wedged between Golden Trout Wilderness (which I had left at the saddle earlier) and South Sierra Wilderness. So it was possible to get here with a vehicle. Not far from the cabin was Monache Creek and it was flowing! I was relieved to find water again and immediately started filtering a full bag of cold, delicious water.

![Views of Monache Meadow from Olancha Pass Trail (36°13’25.98″ N 118°8’36.462″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4590-1024x683.jpg)

Sitting on a large log, I also had to take care of something else: a blister on the heel of my left foot. I put a blister bandaid on it and that helped. On the other side of Monache Creek, I suddenly spotted a car. Someone was car camping there. This area was somewhat of a trailhead, there was even a large information board. I didn’t stay and pressed on. This was Olancha Pass Trail leading me back to the Pacific Crest Trail in less than 2 miles. But it was also sandy and steep, gaining 1,000 feet of elevation quickly. Glimpses of Monache Meadow flickered through the pine canopy as I climbed, eventually opening up to reveal the massive golden valley floor sweeping across the distant basin. And then I finally rejoined the Pacific Crest Trail. I was elated to be back. The next half mile was familiar before I turned left where I had previously gone straight towards Summit Meadow and Olancha Pass. The ascent was unforgiving, I was tackling another steep climb as the trees thinned to reveal broad panoramas of Monache Meadow far below.

![Brush Meadow views towards Monache Meadow (36°14’38.484″ N 118°7’13.614″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4614-1024x683.jpg)

![The barren High Sierra crest around Mount Langley in the distance (36°14’53.43″ N 118°7’31.608″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4625-1024x683.jpg)

The next thing I was worried about again was water. The water sources along the trail were not as plentiful as I would have liked, to I had to be strategic about where to filter water. I stopped again soon at another small stream flowing across the trail and grabbed another bag of water, then sat beneath a nearby pine tree to filter and have [Peak Refuel Mountain Berry Granola](https://peakrefuel.com/products/mountain-berry-granola). I didn’t bother boiling water though, it was quite hot already, so a cold breakfast was just the thing I needed. While enjoying the shade, I strategized about the water sources for the rest of the day. I wanted to carry as little water as possible while ensuring I wouldn’t go thirsty. I settled on the next water in 6 miles south of Gomez Meadow and then again 2.5 miles later at Death Canyon which would have to supply me overnight. Back on trail, I continued uphill and met a backpacker going southbound. After cresting another saddle, I reached Brush Meadow where dramatic, far-reaching views opened up across Monache Meadow and further into the Sierra Nevada. From this high perspective, the massive scale of the Kern Plateau basin really hit home.

![Crossing the sandy slopes below Olancha Peak (36°15’49.836″ N 118°7’50.958″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4635-1024x683.jpg)

![Kern Peak and Templeton Mountain in the distance (36°16’11.3912″ N 118°8’4.7814″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4636-1024x683.jpg)

Just below Olancha Peak, I reached the highest point of today’s hike at 10,555 feet / 3,217 meters. Olancha Peak is widely considered the southernmost major peak of the High Sierra before the range transitions into lower, more arid terrain. Peering through the subalpine pine canopy along the crest, the jagged, pale granite wall of the High Sierra – including Mount Langley in the far distance – stretched sharply across the northern horizon. Traversing the high shoulders directly below Olancha Peak, the PCT cut a clear singletrack tread through dry, sandy soil littered with fallen pinecones. The shade of the surrounding foxtail and lodgepole pines offered a welcome respite from the high-altitude sun as I began winding toward the long descent. As the trail curved along the open, brushy slopes, sweeping views opened up across the entire Kern Plateau basin to the west. Far across the forested expanse, the rounded volcanic dome of Templeton Mountain and the prominent granitic peak of Kern Peak stood out against the horizon – a satisfying look back at the terrain I had covered during the previous day.

From the high point below Olancha Peak, it was about 5 miles of downhill. Far in the distance across the pine forest, the broad volcanic dome of Templeton Mountain dominated the western horizon, framed nicely above the subalpine meadows below. It was getting hotter by the minute as I was losing elevation and eventually reached 92°F / 33°C. I took another break at Gomez Meadow next to a boardwalk across the meadow. I had read on FarOut (the app for thru hikers) that there was a habituated bear in the vicinity that showed no fear of humans, but the meadow remained quiet and no bear in sight. The wide, golden meadow stretched quietly beneath blue skies, bordered by towering pines and white granite outcroppings along the ridge. The PCT rounded a smaller hill and then passed along Big Dry Meadow to the left before reaching Dry Creek within Death Canyon. The latest FarOut comment described the water in the creek as “beyond vile” and it was indeed incredibly gross. I dropped my backpack and grabbed my water bag. There was supposed to be a clean spring a quarter mile upstream. I followed an obvious path with footprints and found a small side stream fed by a spring coming out of the mountains. The water wasn’t “gushing” as the comments had described, but it was clean and flowing well. I grabbed a completely full bag of water.

![Templeton Mountain (36°17’2.226″ N 118°8’28.8454″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4650-1024x683.jpg)

![The boardwalk across Gomez Meadow (36°18’10.386″ N 118°7’28.122″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4652-1024x683.jpg)

I had two options now. Camping directly at the creek or making it two miles further to a prettier campsite but with a lot of uphill to get there. It would definitely be dry camping again but I wanted to reduce the elevation gain for the next day, so I decided to keep going. The ascent was demanding, especially carry extra water weight so late in the day. But as the trail climbed higher north of Death Canyon, sweeping views of Olancha Peak emerged through the trees, making every drop of effort completely worth it. When I finally reached the campsite I had targeted, I was utterly exhausted. But the views were spectacular. You could see all the way across Golden Trout Wilderness to the highest peaks of the Sierra Nevada in the northwest. There were several great flat spots for my tent, so I picked one and set up camp. Dinner was [Farm to Summit Garden Mac & Cheese](https://farmtosummit.com/products/garden-mac). I started boiling water, but about a minute later, my fuel canister spluttered and died. I thought I had half the canister left at the beginning of this trip, but obviously not. Now I knew for sure that every canister had only 10 boils. The water was hot but not boiling, so I poured it into the pouch and hoped for the best.

![Gomez Meadow (36°18’10.7051″ N 118°7’28.2958″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4654-1024x683.jpg)

![Olancha Peak from the ascent north of Death Canyon (36°19’39.252″ N 118°8’3.48″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4663-1024x683.jpg)

The pasta ended up being a little crunchy but it was still edible. And given how hungry I was, I didn’t care much. I had a few Haribo gummy bears for desert and was happy. My socks and shoes were giving off a horrible stench now. I had to survive one more day. There were almost no mosquitoes here, but I still settled into my tent early, tired from more than 12 hours of hiking. After the sun had set, I had another unique experience: Coyotes started howling on the plains below me. And then other coyotes answered from the distance. This continued for a few minutes. Coyotes are extremely vocal in the high country, especially at dusk and at night. Their howls and group pack choruses can sound remarkably deep, haunting, and far-reaching, easily echoing across canyons and wide plains. It was hard to tell how far away they actually were. As darkness settled, I fell asleep quickly, a more restful sleep than the night before.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Good trail tread throughout the entire day. The trail towards Monache Meadows had a lot of loose pine cones on it. From Monache Meadows to the Pacific Crest Trail, it was quite sandy at times. The Pacific Crest Trail itself was in excellent condition.

**Crowds:** Very lightly traveled. Only met one car camper at Monache Meadows and one southbound backpacker near Olancha Peak.

**Weather:** Sunny with no clouds throughout the entire day. A light intermittent breeze in the afternoon. Low of 45°F / 7°C in the morning at the beginning of the hike. High of 92°F / 33°C during the descent from Olancha Peak. Humidity between 43% in the morning and 11% in the afternoon.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Golden Trout Wilderness. South Sierra Wilderness. Inyo National Forest. Wilderness permit required (no quota).

**Overall Rating:** 4.1

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Very Easy
