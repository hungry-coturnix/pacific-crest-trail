# Bouquet Canyon Road to Lake Hughes Road

Feb 8, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.583647, -118.321356](https://maps.app.goo.gl/2VGCCL4ZBf6sJmV27) (Bouquet Canyon Road)  
**End:** [34.659683, -118.460448](https://maps.app.goo.gl/s5KXZc5AHBFqzkKG9) (Lake Hughes Road)

**Distance:** 21.2 mi (up to PCT mile 485.7, 18.3% completed)  
**Time:** 8 hours 36 minutes (start 6:41am, end 3:17pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 4,201 ft (low 3,015 ft – high 4,460 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 4,477 ft (start 3,334 ft – end 3,058 ft)

---

It was Super Bowl Sunday, the perfect day for another hike in solitude. Southern California was experiencing an unusual February heat wave with forecasts above 70°F / 21°C while the East Coast was still being firmly gripped by bitter cold and snow. I had felt like I was getting sick on Friday, probably from a particularly stressful week with three days in the office. But on Saturday afternoon, I felt okay enough and too restless to not go. So here I was back in Palmdale, likely for the last time. I got up at 5:10am and left the hotel at 5:26am. This would be the first bike shuttle since Wrightwood last November. The plan was to drop the bike at the intersection of the PCT with Lake Hughes Road. The LA County website said that Lake Hughes Road was still closed from the aftermath of the holiday storms. But last time I had driven past it on my way back home, it didn’t seem closed. So I was hopeful to be able to get to the PCT all the way. Worst case, I would have to drop off my bike a bit earlier which would at most add 2 miles to the hike – I was comfortable with that.

![Dowd Canyon (34°37’24.726″ N 118°22’43.296″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0141-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

When I turned onto Lake Hughes Road, there was a large sign saying the road was closed 5 miles ahead. My hopes went up. A quarter mile later, there was a sign in the middle of the road saying the road was closed, but it was clearly positioned in a way that traffic could still pass on both sides. So I kept going. I reached the PCT crossing and chained my bike to a fire prevention board right next to the trail, then drove back to Bouquet Canyon Road. At 6:31am, I was back at the spot where I had ended my last hike. Sunrise was still 15 minutes away. I had some baguette and another bottle of water, then set out on the PCT across the road. Just around the next big tree, there was a hiker paradise – a big cabinet filled with water bottles, two camping chairs and a large cooler. I am sure thru hikers would have been very appreciative.

![Green Valley (34°38’4.668″ N 118°23’46.23″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0158-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Elizabeth Lake and the San Andreas Rift Zone with the Mojave Desert in the distance (34°38’28.896″ N 118°24’16.512″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0166-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The PCT gained a bit of elevation, opening up views of Bouquet Reservoir in the distance. The sun had come up now and was painting the top of the hills in golden colors. The trail meandered in and out of several gullies, then turned due north to tackle the top of the Leona Divide. Big power lines were hanging across the trail and one of the towers was rattling in an odd way. The PCT crossed a firebreak, then descended again on the northern side into Spunky Canyon. It was distinctly greener here. There was fresh green grass on the ground and the chaparral and oak trees were denser and bigger. The trail made it all the way to the bottom of the canyon, then descended further to cross Spunky Edison Road 6N09. A mile later was a memorial bench underneath a very large oak tree where I took an extended break. The PCT gained elevation again winding in and out of every small ravine, then topped a ridge on the south side of Dowd Canyon. I could see the trail on the other side of the canyon. It seemed so close, but knowing how the trail was winding, it was still miles to get to the other side. The bottom of Dowd Canyon was pleasantly green and in the distance I could see some residences of Green Valley.

![The head of South Portal and Munz Canyons (34°38’49.056″ N 118°24’47.976″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0172-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![South Portal Canyon (34°38’52.59″ N 118°25’9.666″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0179-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

After another ascent to a ridgetop separating Dowd Canyon and San Francisquito Canyon, I was back on a steep downhill stretch towards San Francisquito Road. Green Valley was to the south and on the other side of the canyon was Grass Mountain. I took another break after crossing the road on a tree stump in the shade. It was pretty warm now and over the next few miles I would be ascending on a fairly bare and exposed hill side, so I decided to take my hoodie off and hike in short sleeves. The ascent was not as bad as I thought, but I ended up counting the steps to focus on a steady pace. The vegetation around me reminded me of the early days near the Mexican border. There were lots of broad leave yuccas right next to the trail. One of them poked straight into one of my right hand’s arteries, immediately drawing a big drop of blood.

![Hiatt Canyon (34°38’56.82″ N 118°26’25.74″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0187-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Sawmill Mountain (34°39’0.738″ N 118°26’39.144″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/281A0189-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Reaching the high point just east of Grass Mountain, panoramas unfolded north towards Elizabeth Lake right on top of the San Andreas Fault and to the distant Tehachapi Mountains which I would cross in a few hikes from now. The PCT contoured the northern slope of Grass Mountain, then crossed a saddle at the head of South Portal Canyon and Munz Canyon. Turning south, the trail stayed above South Portal Canyon, then crossed another saddle at Tule Canyon Road 7N01 and started its final descent towards Lake Hughes Road. The views were great, to the north was Hiatt Canyon with Portal Ridge in the distance. Past another saddle, I could see Elizabeth Lake Canyon with Lake Hughes Road. The trail wound in and out of numerous ravines again, stretching what seemed like a short distance into two more miles. Eventually, I reached the bottom of Elizabeth Lake Canyon, crossed the wide wash, hopping across the strong stream, and made my way back up to the road. I stopped at a shady bench just across the road to hydrate and snack again before cycling back.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt, well maintained. Slightly overgrown on the north side of the Leona Divide entering Spunky Canyon. Very exposed to the sun with little shade throughout the entire hike. Some impact north of Grass Mountain from the 2013 Powerhouse Fire.

**Parking:** There is room for about 10 cars on the south side of Bouquet Canyon Road.

**Crowds:** Complete solitude. Only met a single hiker with a dog on South Portal Canyon.

**Weather:** Sunny throughout the entire day with no clouds and no breeze. Low of 55°F / 13°C in the morning at the start of the hike. High of 69°F / 21°C on the south side of Grass Mountain.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Angeles National Forest. Annual Interagency Permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.5

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Moderate  
Logistics: Easy

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 17.7 mi (1 hour 45 min), 1,030 ft elevation gain, 10.1 mph average speed, 30.6 mph maximum speed

The bike ride back was tough. It was immediately uphill and demanding. The first two miles to Lake Hughes were slow going. Fortunately there were no cars since the road was closed behind me. When I turned onto Lake Elizabeth Road, the incline was more manageable and I was able to pick up the pace a bit, alternating between rolling and pushing ahead. I passed Elizabeth Lake and turned right towards Andrade Corner. Traffic was minimal and drivers were giving me a wide berth. Around Leona Valley, the road was going downhill, a welcome reprieve which came to an abrupt end when I turned onto Bouquet Canyon Road. Now it was another 3 miles uphill. My energy reservoir was depleted and I ended up pushing my bike through the steepest stretches all the way to the summit. The last mile was downhill again. When I reached my car, it was just past sunset.
