# CA-58 to South Kelso Valley Road (Day 3)

May 4, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [35.387027, -118.301717](https://maps.app.goo.gl/GMQh9k2kVq5jnGxA7) (Robin Bird Spring)  
**End:** [35.450729, -118.224156](https://maps.app.goo.gl/kHM3rnLUmVwBxjZP6) (South Kelso Valley Road)

**Distance:** 15.2 mi (up to PCT mile 617.1, 23.3% completed)  
**Time:** 6 hours 38 minutes (start 7:33am, end 2:11pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 1,425 ft (low 4,954 ft – high 6,793 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 2,787 ft (start 6,316 ft – end 4,954 ft)

---

It had cooled down to 47°F / 8°C inside the tent over night. I woke up shortly before 6am and it was already light outside. But when I opened my tent, it wasn’t blue sky that was greeting me. We were in the clouds and they were floating through the campsite. I was hoping that the clouds would burn off quickly with the sun out so that I would be able to take more photos. The weather forecast had predicted partly cloudy skies, but that was 3 days ago. For breakfast, I had [Peak Refuel Strawberry Granola](https://peakrefuel.com/products/strawberry-granola-meal) and a cup of hot Jasmine tea. I stayed in the tent so that I could still enjoy the warmth of my sleeping bag. Finally, I had to get out of the tent and start packing up. Unfortunately my clothes were still not dry, it had been too humid over night. So I had to put on two dry jackets instead. Before I got back on trail, I had to take care of one other thing though. I searched for a quiet spot away from the campsite and used my [DirtSaw Deuce](https://thetentlab.com/Deuce/DeuceofSpadespage.html) trowel to dig a 6 inch cathole for my personal business.

![The sun is slowly burning through the low hanging clouds (35°23’50.3921″ N 118°18’2.4495″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2354-1024x683.jpg)

![Walking through pine forest along Landers Creek (35°26’5.8692″ N 118°18’35.965″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2362-1024x683.jpg)

Back on trail, it was cold. Without the protection of the campsite, the temperature had dropped down to 36°F / 2°C and I was walking through the low hanging clouds. After half a mile of uphill, I crossed dirt Jawbone Canyon Road, then descended again towards Cottonwood Creek. The cloud cover was getting lighter and I could even see a few blue patches of sky now. But as I headed deeper into the Piute Mountains, the clouds became denser again. The next uphill was very manageable, but the occasional wind was quite cold. I crossed another rugged dirt road and had one final ascent before I crossed over the ridge. The two section hikers – brothers with trail names Mayor and Kyte – were taking a break. We ended up walking together for a bit. They were from San Jose not far from me and hiking from Tehachapi to Kennedy Meadows. Kyte was carrying the ukulele that I had heard the evening before.

![Looking back at the Piute Mountains (35°27’15.552″ N 118°18’6.63″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2381-1024x683.jpg)

![The first view towards Brightstar Wilderness and Kelso Valley (35°27’22.8526″ N 118°16’10.7736″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2428-Pano-1024x529.jpg)

On the other side of the ridge, the clouds opened up and I could see down the valley with Landers Creek at its bottom. Another mile later, I stopped to filter some more water from the creek. It had warmed up a little bit now. Noodle, the thru hiker from the previous day also caught up to me and got some water. We chatted a bit more. After plenty of rest, it was time for the last 9 miles. The PCT paralleled Landers Creek, surrounded by lots of pine trees. I passed Landers Meadow which was acquired by the Pacific Crest Trail Association to preserve the area and crossed Piute Mountain Road. There was a trail register on the other side which I signed as usual. The trail soon turned east crossing another dirt road to Landers Camp and gained some more elevation. The area was also scarred by previous wildfires. I looked back frequently to see the clouds still hanging in the Piute Mountains.

![Blue Lupine along the trail with Gold Peak in the distance (35°27’21.1875″ N 118°15’2.8831″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2445-1024x683.jpg)

![Reaching the eastern end of St. John Ridge with views of Mayan Peak (35°27’26.14″ N 118°14’2.5358″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/281A2476-Pano-1024x438.jpg)

The final ascent dragged on for a while, crossing a field of large boulders. Then the trail crossed Piute Mountain Road again and started its descent in earnest along the northern side of St. John Ridge. The views north were incredible. Lots of peaks were visible in the distance, framed by a few clouds. As I descended, the landscape gradually changed back to a desert ecosystem. I was back among shrubs and much more exposed terrain. The PCT moved in and out multiple gullies then turned southeast along St. John Ridge until I finally reached South Kelso Valley Road. Noodle and three other thru hikers were taking a break next to a gigantic water cache of more than 60 bottles of water with 3 gallons each. This spot was known to be attended to by a trail angel from Kelso Valley. I was very glad to be back at my car. The trip had been spectacular, but I was also looking forward to a shower and a comfortable bed.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt, occasionally somewhat overgrown. St. John Ridge is fairly exposed to the sun with little shade.

**Parking:** There is room for 3 cars just off South Kelso Valley Road.

**Crowds:** Mostly solitude. Leap frogging with a few hikers.

**Weather:** Dense low hanging clouds in the morning, then partly cloudy. Very light intermittent breeze. Low of 36°F / 2°C in the morning at the start of the hike. High of 62°F / 17°C around noon on St. John Ridge.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Sequoia National Forest and land managed by the Bureau of Land Management. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.8

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Moderate
