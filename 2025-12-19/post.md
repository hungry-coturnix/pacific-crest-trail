# Burkhart Trail to Three Points Trailhead

Dec 19, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.343440, -117.982985](https://maps.app.goo.gl/MRHDMsFsoFmtrTLL7) (Three Points Trailhead)  
**End:** [34.343440, -117.982985](https://maps.app.goo.gl/MRHDMsFsoFmtrTLL7) (Three Points Trailhead)

**Distance:** 21.8 mi (up to PCT mile 403.1, 15.2% completed)  
**Time:** 8 hours 54 minutes (start 6:49am, end 3:44pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 4,430 ft (low 5,629 ft – high 7,685 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 4,430 ft (start 5,928 ft – end 5,928 ft)

---

The weather forecast for the following week was predicting torrential rain. At the higher elevations, most of that would undoubtedly fall as snow. I wanted to get past the highest mountains before that so I could hopefully continue hiking the PCT throughout the winter. I decided to do two more hikes to get through the heart of the San Gabriel Mountains and closer to Acton, just south of Palmdale.

![San Gabriel Wilderness with Monrovia Peak in the distance (34°20’20.17″ N 117°58’46.1022″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9332-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

You might wonder why this section of the PCT does not continue where I left off last time, at Eagles Roost Picnic Area. That’s because a 3.6 mile stretch of the PCT has been closed since 2005 to protect the Southern California mountain yellow-legged frog and its designated critical habitat in Little Rock Creek. This area is one of the last remaining strongholds for the frog and the closure was intended to curtail human activity to help the frog recover. My plan was to do a loop hike starting at Three Points, hiking the Mount Waterman Trail to Buckhorn Campground, and then to rejoin the PCT right at the end of the closure area in Cooper Canyon and hike back on the PCT.

![Looking across Devils Canyon (34°19’54.6928″ N 117°56’31.22″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9368-Pano-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x482.jpg)

I had stayed at a hotel in Burbank and the drive up the Angeles Crest Highway to Three Points took about an hour. I arrived at 6:36am, about 17 minutes before sunrise. I was the only car in the parking lot. It was going to be another beautiful sunny day. The area around Three Points had burned during the 2009 Station Fire and only shrubs and bushes had recovered so far. From the parking lot, I headed south, crossed the highway and turned onto Mount Waterman Trail which was contouring the southern hillside of Mount Waterman. The views across Devils Canyon were expansive. I could see the Los Angeles metropolitan area in the distance, still covered in fog. On the other side of Devils Canyon was Stony Ridge, and behind that I could make out the Mount Wilson Observatory.

![Mount Baldy looming in the distance (34°20’5.3197″ N 117°55’53.8202″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9384-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Bear Creek drainage with Twin Peaks to the right (34°20’6.6586″ N 117°55’50.005″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9391-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The sun had come up and was painting the eastern slopes in golden light. Two miles into the hike, I entered the burn scar of the 2020 Bobcat Fire which would accompany me for most of today’s hike. The charred trees were still standing watch. There was plenty of poodle dog bush and I could often smell it before I actually saw it. The trail kept on climbing steadily until I reached a junction with a spur trail to Twin Peaks saddle to the south. I turned north towards Mount Waterman up some serious switchbacks. Views into the Bear Creek drainage opened up and I could make out closed highway 39 on the other side. After passing another junction that could have led me all the way to the top of Mount Waterman, the trail switchbacked down to a ridge and then descended rapidly to the Angeles Crest Highway. There were still a few small patches of snow that the sun had been unable to reach, but nothing that posed an obstacle. I could hear loud chainsaw noise from the Mount Waterman ski area.

![Pallet Mountain down Cooper Canyon (34°21’32.832″ N 117°54’21.378″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9419-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Crossing the highway, I passed through the Buckhorn Day Use Area, then made my way to Buckhorn Campground. The campground had closed before Thanksgiving and it felt a bit surreal to walk past the empty campsites. After a creek flowing across the concrete campground road, I settled for my second break on the picnic table for site 26. The campground had been spared from the Bobcat Fire and I was enjoying the sound of the wind blowing through the pine trees around me. I continued to the end of the road to Burkhart Trail. It was paralleling a well flowing creek as it descended to Cooper Canyon. Through the burned trees, I could see down the canyon towards Pallet Mountain.

![Pleasant View Ridge (34°21’52.1786″ N 117°55’22.1822″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9435-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I finally reached the junction with the PCT and continued 0.2 miles further to the end of the closed area. I was expecting a sign, but there was nothing. It wasn’t even obvious that the PCT was continuing east here. I guess that’s what happens when an area is closed for 20 years. I turned around, mentally preparing myself for several miles of ascent. But before that, I stopped at Cooper Canyon Falls. Or rather, I scrambled down to the falls since it was very steep. The falls had a surprising amount of water given that the last rainfall had been 4 weeks ago. With everything in the shade, it was difficult to take a good photo.

![Snow covered Throop Peak in the distance (34°21’45.3161″ N 117°55’48.1148″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9456-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The PCT crossed the creek feeding the falls and followed an old jeep road up Cooper Canyon. The trail was on the northern side and where the sun was able to reach the ground, it was actually very warm. A little over a mile later, I reached Copper Canyon Trail Camp and took another break. There was actually a tent on the ground, but nobody was around. Continuing on, the trail made its way up to the ridge, offering spectacular views of Pleasant View Ridge on the other side of Little Rock Creek Canyon. Turning south towards Winston Peak, the PCT moved back into the shade. I crossed a dirt road and tackled the last ascent for the day to Cloudburst Summit. No clouds were bursting here, but there was certainly a strong burst of wind.

![Pacifico Mountain, my next destination, to the west (34°21’10.5845″ N 117°56’52.3894″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9472-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The PCT paralleled the Angeles Crest Highway for about a mile before crossing it towards Cloudburst Canyon. After a switchback, it then continued to descend and parallel the highway, now in the shade of Mount Waterman. After passing the 400 mile marker, I reached the Camp Glenwood Boy Scout Camp which was closed for the season, then crossed the highway again at the 6,000 Foot Day Use Area. The PCT now followed an old dirt road with a very gentle gradient, not far from the highway. Camp Valcrest, a historical camp for youth to experience the wilderness was north of the highway. I finally crossed the highway one last time and made it back to the Three Points Trailhead where I had started.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Snow free throughout, but with some visible snow patches along the trail east of Mount Waterman and in Cooper Canyon. Lots of downed trees on Mount Waterman Trail. Significant impact from the 2020 Bobcat Fire throughout most of the hike.

**Parking:** There is plenty of parking at Three Points Trailhead.

**Crowds:** Fairly solitary. Only met 7 people throughout the day, all day hikers along Cooper Canyon.

**Weather:** Mostly sunny with blue skies with a few cirrus clouds. Low of 54°F / 12°C in the morning. High of 75°F / 24°C in the afternoon in upper Cooper Canyon.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Angeles National Forest. San Gabriel Mountains National Monument. Portions within San Gabriel Wilderness and Pleasant View Ridge Wilderness. Annual Interagency Permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 4.2

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Very Easy
