# Oriflamme Mountain to Mount Laguna

Nov 30, 2024 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [32.978302, -116.524703](https://maps.app.goo.gl/MzSeoXLvWLGtse9m7) (Sunrise Trailhead)  
**End:** [32.857227, -116.423589](https://maps.app.goo.gl/f4WuCo9RfesLpSYn9) (Red-Tailed Roost Trailhead)

This is a reverse hike to reduce elevation gain on the way back.

**Distance:** 21.4 mi (up to PCT mile 59.5, 2.2% completed)  
**Time:** 9 hours 22 minutes (start 6:32am, end 3:54pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 3,553 ft (low 4,825 ft – high 6,047 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 2,634 ft (start 5,003 ft – end 5,922 ft)

---

Another day, another 5am alarm. The weather forecast did not disappoint and I was ready for another vista filled section of the PCT. This time I would be hiking southbound so that the elevation gain on the bike ride back would be more manageable.

Passing through Mount Laguna, I left Cleveland National Forest, entered Anza-Borrego Desert State Park, and parked at Sunrise Trailhead. The sun was just coming up in the east and painting a few clouds and the hills in a broad spectrum of warm orange and red. It was 6:32am when I left the car.

![The first light over Oriflamme Mountain (32°58’41.6677″ N 116°31’28.5957″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4011-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Sunrise over Anza-Borrego Desert State Park (32°58’48.474″ N 116°31’14.106″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4015-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The views were quite different from the last hike. Seemingly bare mountain ranges filled the horizon with Oriflamme Mountain straight to the east and the Vallecito Mountains north of that. The trail was following the contour lines of the Laguna Mountains and alternating between moderate uphill and downhill stretches.

As I continued south, towards Garnet Mountain, spectacular views to the east towards Mason Valley opened up, framed by the Sawtooth Range to the south.

![Oriflamme Mountain with Mason Valley in the distance (32°57’37.836″ N 116°30’15.882″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4030-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Mason Valley (32°56’48.684″ N 116°29’19.104″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4043-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Past Kwaaymii Point, I was surprised to find a lot of plaques and photos set into the rock. This is apparently a place to commemorate loved ones. Patriotically, a large American flag was just to the side. Continuing on, I passed Pioneer Mail Trailhead and headed towards Garnet Peak. I decided to take the short side trip and head all the way up to the peak. The panoramic views were simply breathtaking. I stayed for a few minutes to soak in the spectacular scenery before I headed back down and south on the PCT.

![Commemorative plaques near Kwaaymii Point (32°56’1.05″ N 116°28’55.362″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4058-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![View from Garnet Peak (32°55’32.394″ N 116°27’31.332″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4073-Pano-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x520.jpg)

The trail reached Penny Pines Trailhead and moved along the upper reaches of Storm Canyon. A few zigzags led me to Foster Point which offered great views down Storm Canyon. Further south, the golf ball radar domes of Stephenson Peak came into view, part of the abandoned Mount Laguna U.S. Air Force Western Air Defense Network Station and now a Federal Aviation Administration navigation control site.

South of Stephenson Peak, I stopped at the Desert View Picnic Area to enjoy the spectacular views of Anza-Borrego Desert State Park to the east. Vallecito Valley, its floor more than 4,000 feet below in the rain shadow of the Laguna Mountains, looked dry but beautiful in its own way. Continuing on, I passed the Burnt Rancheria Campground, originally a seasonal ranch owned by cattlemen in the late 1800s which was burned down by American Indians to stop their advancement.

![Abundant views across Anza-Borrego Desert State Park (32°53’49.188″ N 116°25’43.842″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4111-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![View from Desert View Picnic Area (32°52’0.09″ N 116°24’45.102″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4115-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The last half mile was familiar terrain leading back to Red-Tailed Roost Trailhead, the route I had taken the last time.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt, with lots of loose rocks.

**Parking:** There is ample parking available at Sunrise Trailhead.

**Weather:** Mostly sunny, with a high of 74°F (somewhere around Garnet Peak) and a low of 50°F (early in the morning, at the start of the hike). Partly cloudy after 2pm.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Anza-Borrego Desert State Park and Cleveland National Forest. No permits required if you park at Sunrise Trailhead.

**Overall Rating:** 3.8

Difficulty: Moderate  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Very Easy

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 11.6 mi (47 min), 539 ft elevation gain, 14.78 mph average

I was better prepared for the bike ride back this time. Even though it was warmer, I had brought a jacket to protect me from the wind. The route was straightforward, just following Sunrise Highway north back to the car. I was expecting more uphill stretches than during the last rides, but I was hopeful that they wouldn’t be too difficult. And for the most part that turned out to be true. The first difficult uphill stretch was just north of Pioneer Mail Trailhead, but I made it in first gear without stopping. Though shortly thereafter, another steep uphill stretch south of Kwaaymii Point forced me to get off the bike and push it for a bit. A young couple was just getting back into their car and was cheering me on. The rest of the way was manageable and I got back to the car at 4:49pm, just 10 minutes past sunset.
