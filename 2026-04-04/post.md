# Broken Arrow Road to Tehachapi Willow Springs Road

Apr 4, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.899877, -118.455083](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Xq7YFx9D9msao42KA) (Aqueduct Road & Broken Arrow Road)  
**End:** [35.052179, -118.358599](https://maps.app.goo.gl/eYjVq657vSk77Aat5) (Tehachapi Willow Springs Road)

**Distance:** 23.7 mi (up to PCT mile 558.5, 21.1% completed)  
**Time:** 9 hours 42 minutes (start 6:49am, end 4:31pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 4,808 ft (low 3,105 ft – high 6,310 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 3,774 ft (start 3,105 ft – end 4,139 ft)

---

The Tehachapi Mountains were calling. I needed to get out of the hot and dry Antelope Valley while the temperatures were still bearable. So I was back for another weekend of PCT hiking. For the very first time, I was staying in Tehachapi, a sign of how far I had already come. I left the hotel at 5:43am and made my way to where the PCT was crossing Tehachapi Willow Springs Road. It was still dark, but the first light was visible to the east. I had looked at Google Street View ahead of time to figure out where to chain my bike and it was indeed easy to attach it to one of the wooden utility poles. Getting the bike off the rack was so easy now, I really enjoyed my new car. From here it was a 30 minute drive down into Antelope Valley and to where I had stopped last time. I parked my car on the side of Aqueduct Road as the sun was starting to rise.

Today was going to be a long day, but the shortest option to slice the PCT into a manageable day hike. The first 5 miles would be through the Manzana Wind Farm. The wind turbines were quietly humming. There was very little wind so far, but I knew it would pick up. There was a reason after all why they had built a wind farm here. It was still chilly in the shade. After a quarter mile on a dirt road, I was finally back on regular trail tread. The moon was hanging low to the west, framed by wind turbines. The PCT was well marked with frequent big and small trail markers, paralleling a barbed wire fence going north in a straight line. I crossed various dirt roads used to access the individual wind turbines and slowly gained elevation. The ground was covered in grass and weed, unusually dry for early April. While gaining elevation, I could see the entire Antelope Valley unfolding behind me. Ahead of me were the Tehachapi Mountains and Tylerhorse Canyon.

After about 5 miles, I had left the wind farm behind and entered a small canyon going north. The trail became steeper, climbing to a ridge west of Tylerhorse Canyon before crossing the ridge and dropping to the canyon bottom. A small creek was flowing and I used the opportunity to take a break and filter some water. The wind had already picked up, but down here it was calm and peaceful. After drinking as much water as I could, I climbed back out of Tylerhorse Canyon. The PCT moved in and out of several canyons, maintaining roughly the same elevation. The views south across the wind farm and the larger Antelope Valley were stunning. Cresting a ridge where I signed a trail register, the PCT quickly descended several hundred feet into Gamble Spring Canyon. Both sides of the canyon had deep ruts from motorcyclists. And right at the bottom of the canyon, five guys on their motorcycles were waiting. I passed them and started the ascent on the other side. It was a long uphill climb from here. The hillside was covered in wildflowers, yellow and blue, which was incredibly pretty.

The views across Antelope Valley kept getting better and better. Eventually, I reached the top of the ridge with views north into the Tehachapi Mountains across Oak Creek Canyon. Covington Mountain was prominently to the west. A number of scattered vacation homes were within Oak Creek Canyon, but the canyon itself was private land. I passed a spot with several good campsites and then a little hiker oasis. Right next to a dirt road, a trail angel had built an entire trail camp. There were a bunch of chairs and cabinets with recently stocked gallons of water. There were rusty tea pots and other things hanging from the bushes. It had character! Shortly thereafter, I reached the highest point of today’s hike. From here it was mostly downhill. The area was still visibly scarred from the 2011 Canyon Fire, even after so many years. But I also noticed that some of the brush next to the trail looked fairly freshly cut. And then just around the next corner, I ran into a trail maintenance crew. There were 8 people total, busy with different tasks, from cutting encroaching branches to removing blowdowns and smoothing the ground. Everyone was very friendly and I thanked all of them for their work.

Rounding the head of Pitney Canyon, I could now see the Tehachapi wind farms in the distance. Soon enough, the steady hum of the wind turbines was audible as well. There were so many of them! The next few miles passed relatively quickly. It was a steady downhill, following the outline of every little side canyon. I could see Tehachapi Willow Springs Road in the distance, but it was still 2.5 miles to go. The trail was just far from a straight line. I reached Oak Creek, passed another trail register which I signed again, and crossed over a small wooden bridge. A picnic table on the other side was inviting me to another break. There were lots of information sheets stapled to the table, about accommodation options and trail angels. After some more rest, it was only a quarter mile to my bike and I still had some water left to spare.

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt, occasionally somewhat overgrown with plenty of weed. Completely exposed to the sun with no shade. Lots of wind turbines for the first and last 5 miles. Some impact from the 2011 Canyon Fire on the ridge south of Oak Creek Canyon.

**Parking:** There is plenty of room to park along Aqueduct Road.

**Crowds:** Absolute solitude. Only met a backpacker going south early in the morning and a trail maintenance crew in the afternoon.

**Weather:** Sunny throughout the entire day with a firm breeze and strong occasional wind gusts. Low of 53°F / 12°C in the morning at the start of the hike after sunrise. High of 86°F / 30°C in the early afternoon.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Mostly private land. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.5

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Moderate  
Logistics: Very Easy

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 16.6 mi (1 hour 7 min), 136 ft elevation gain, 14.78 mph average speed, 29.05 mph maximum speed

The bike ride back ended up being easier than I thought. The first half mile was somewhat uphill and gentle enough that I could do it in a lower gear. I was already pretty exhausted after the long hike, but it was manageable. Then it was downhill for a while. The gradient was perfect, I didn’t have to brake. And the road down into Antelope Valley was such a straight line. Then I turned onto Aqueduct Road. It was the familiar half dirt half concrete road from last time. And it was so incredibly flat. I made great progress at a steady pace. The concrete occasionally vanished for a bit and only left me with the dirt road, but it did not slow me down that much. The wind turbines appeared again ahead and soon enough I was back at my car, a long exhausting day behind me. It had taken me 11 hours roundtrip.
