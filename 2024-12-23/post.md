# CA-78 to Montezuma Valley Road

Dec 23, 2024 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [33.098417, -116.471580](https://maps.app.goo.gl/5wF9xeQ39ovpuybi8) (CA-78, at the junction with San Felipe Road)  
**End:** [33.216865, -116.587919](https://maps.app.goo.gl/EuVDW1UNQRB5Wzcw8) (Eagle Rock Trailhead)

**Distance:** 25.0 mi (up to PCT mile 101.2, 3.8% completed)  
**Time:** 10 hours 29 minutes (start 6:45am, end 5:14pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 3,248 ft (low 2,240 ft – high 4,420 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 2,051 ft (start 2,263 ft – end 3,460 ft)

---

Today was exhausting – because of the distance, the heat, and the unusual elevation profile. But let me not get ahead of myself and start from the beginning. I had parked in the exact same spot as the day before, but today’s hike was north towards the San Felipe Hills. This stretch has a reputation for being both blistering hot and frustratingly gentle. As it turned out, it very much deserved this reputation.

I started 2 minutes before sunrise at 6:45am. The trail paralleled CA-78 for about 100 yards before crossing the highway and heading straight for the base of Grapevine Mountain. The flora along the trail was very similar to the day before, with teddy bear cholla and agave, now mixed with red barrel cactus. The sun soon came up and painted the hills in a bright orange.

![Teddy bear cholla (33°6’16.512″ N 116°28’22.338″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4244-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Barrel cactus (33°6’11.934″ N 116°28’32.43″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4241-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail itself had only a very small incline, but as a result of that it was zigzagging up Grapevine Mountain very slowly and following the outline of every nook and cranny. Progress seemed almost futile. Even an hour into the hike, I could still very clearly see my parked car. As I inched my way north, views of San Felipe Valley and the Volcan Mountains to the west got increasingly better. The contrast was impressive. The Volcan Mountains were pretty green whereas Grapevine Mountain (in the rain shadow of the Volcan Mountains) felt very dry and like a true desert. Campsites along the trail weren’t scarce, but water was nowhere in sight.

![Volcan Mountains (33°7’39.798″ N 116°29’23.436″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4258-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![San Felipe Hills (33°8’56.088″ N 116°30’46.764″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4273-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Crossing through a metal gate in a barbed wire fence at 9.3 miles, the scenery changed slightly and the hills were dotted with more shrubs. The trail descended into a small valley before zigzagging up the other side. The sun was about to reach its highest point for the day and was relentlessly and intensely heating the landscape. I didn’t want to imagine what this section of the PCT would be like during summer, but I suspect temperatures around 120°F wouldn’t be uncommon.

![Looking back down one of the canyons in San Felipe Hills (33°8’57.834″ N 116°31’0.954″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4274-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![View to the west of the San Felipe Hills (33°10’38.916″ N 116°32’56.04″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4298-Pano-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x578.jpg)

Passing through two more metal gates, I reached a junction with a sign for “H2O” at 14.8 miles. I knew from reading through the guide books that there was a water cache set up by a trail angel just half a mile down the spur trail to the right. Having experienced the exposed trail myself, I understood how relieving that would be for hikers in spring.

![Looking back to San Felipe Valley and Granite Mountain (33°10’45.216″ N 116°33’18.936″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4311-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The first glimpse of Warner Springs (33°11’52.092″ N 116°33’45.534″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4322-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Now in the San Felipe Hills, the trail continued to climb in a very long zigzag on the eastern side before switching back to the western side for the final ascent. I reached the highest point of the hike at 3:10pm and was rewarded with views of Montezuma Valley to the east. After taking my last break, the last 5 miles were ahead of me, a frustratingly gentle descent visiting every nook and cranny on the northern side of the San Felipe Hills. The sun set at 4:45pm, but I still had some way to go. Walking at my fastest pace I finally reached Barrel Spring with the last light. This would have been a very nice campsite had I been backpacking. Turning north and passing through another metal gate, I reached Montezuma Valley Road and my bike chained to the nearby fence.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt, with lots of loose rocks.

**Parking:** There is ample parking available right at the junction.

**Weather:** Mostly sunny throughout the day with a high of 87°F (around 2pm, 2 miles north of the junction to the water cache) and a low of 54°F (early in the morning, at the start of the hike).

**Land Manager & Permits:** Anza-Borrego Desert State Park. No permits required.

**Overall Rating:** 2.8

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Medium  
Logistics: Very Easy

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 13.0 mi (51 min), 338 ft elevation gain, 15.33 mph average speed, 36.67 mph maximum speed

The bike ride back was obviously in full darkness. I had put on my head lamp and turned on the blinking LED lights on my helmet. The light was great, but I quickly realized that it wouldn’t allow me to see very far and gauge the conditions of the road ahead of me (in particular any holes I would have to evade). The first stretch west on Montezuma Valley Road was downhill, but after turning onto San Felipe Road, there was a fairly long stretch of moderate uphill ahead of me. Fortunately, San Felipe Road had a dedicated bike lane and so it didn’t matter that I was going very slowly. After I had reached the top, the rest of the way was mostly downhill again with just a few short uphill stretches. I finally reached my car at 6:15pm.
