# Snow Creek to Mission Creek

Jun 22, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [33.893647, -116.683002](https://maps.app.goo.gl/9rnHnUYSJ1NAe1z97) (Snow Creek Canyon Road)  
**End:** [34.000705, -116.612427](https://maps.app.goo.gl/f5x8DXBymwJMFYJB6) (Mission Creek Trailhead)

**Distance:** 23.9 mi (up to PCT mile 225.6, 8.5% completed)  
**Time:** 9 hours 59 minutes (start 5:56am, end 3:55pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 5,857 ft (low 1,192 ft – high 3,733 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 4,948 ft (start 1,235 ft – end 2,144 ft)

---

I had anxiously watched the weather forecast for Whitewater, CA since the last hike. Every day had been more than 100°F / 38°C with some days even peaking at 109°F / 43°C. This stretch is known for being blisteringly hot and I had worried that I might not get a chance to hike it until September or October which would have put a four month pause on my PCT adventure. Fortunately, this weekend was somewhat cooler with only a high of 88°F / 31°C. This was my lucky opportunity. The hike afterwards would take me back to 8,000 feet, so high temperatures would be less of an issue.

Still being very close to a metropolitan area, the shuttle logistics were conveniently easy again allowing me to get an Uber. I had stayed in Desert Hot Springs, a mere 20 minutes from the end of the hike at Mission Creek Preserve within Sand to Snow National Monument. I got up at 4:30am after a night of very light sleep due to the noise of the air conditioning and left at 4:45am. The last 2 miles to the parking area were unpaved and I had worried a bit whether that would be okay for an Uber ride. But the dirt road was in very good condition. I had scheduled the ride for 5:30am and unlike last time, the driver had not changed multiple times. With sunrise at 5:36am, there was also enough light to easily navigate the road. My driver, Juan, was going to be on time, but he was taking the dirt road at an excruciatingly slow speed.

Juan was very chatty but friendly. He was from the area and we talked about the Pacific Crest Trail and what kind of animals I had encountered while hiking. We arrived in Snow Creek at 5:55am which gave me an early start. As I opened the car door, I was blown away – literally, not figuratively. The wind was very strong. I knew the area was prone to wind, just like I had experienced last time. But today, it was exceptionally strong with gusts of more than 50 mph / 80 kmh. No point in putting my wide brimmed hat on, so I just attached it to the straps of my backpack. The sun was still low and hence it was okay to walk without the hat for a while.

![Sunrise over Coachella Valley (33°53’39.8085″ N 116°41’0.5791″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6245-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![San Jacinto Peak up Snow Creek Canyon (33°53’57.852″ N 116°41’5.064″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6249-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

It was hard to walk in a straight line though. The wind was blowing from my left with mighty force. And the sandy trail tread wasn’t making it any easier. This was prime desert again, with low shrubs and sand. San Jacinto Peak was looming behind me. In front of me I could see San Gorgonio Mountain and Interstate 10 right in the middle of San Gorgonio Pass. About a mile into the hike, I reached the lowest point on the Pacific Crest Trail south of the Columbia River Gorge at the border of Oregon and Washington. It was an unmarked spot at 1,192 feet just before a powerline. As I crossed into the dry San Gorgonio River bed, the trail was no longer marked and I just followed the wash. A long freight train was passing close by. I reached the underpass for the railroad tracks and the interstate after about 2.5 miles.

![Passing under Interstate 10 (33°55’27.3052″ N 116°41’40.5651″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6257-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Even though this was not the most memorable place, it marked another milestone, the end of the California Section B of the Pacific Crest Trail and the beginning of Section C. Right under the interstate was a well known spot for trail magic. Now that thru hikers had all passed, there was no one there anymore, but cardboard on the side was signed by numerous hikers and 5 large bottles of potable water were still available for anyone making this journey in the heat. I took a break in one of the make shift chairs and had a snack. There was no wind down here.

![View across San Gorgonio Pass with San Jacinto Peak dominating the scene (33°56’14.19″ N 116°42’6.126″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6260-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Crossing Desert View Road (33°56’50.58″ N 116°41’32.43″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6265-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The wind was considerably less on the other side. Still a strong breeze, but I was able to put my hat back on. Crossing Tamarack Road, the trail followed the outskirts of Whitewater before entering Sand to Snow National Monument. The hills to the east were covered in wind turbines that unlike me were appreciating the strong winds. Soon after entering the monument, two guys on bicycles passed me. I was not pleased. I called them out for riding in the monument and on the PCT where bikes were not allowed anywhere and their only reaction was “Oh, really??!”.

The trail turned east into Gold Canyon. The wind was in my back now, but I still had to hold on to my hat every now and then through the gusts. Passing through a metal gate and a coral, the trail paralleled the dirt road up the canyon. At 6.3 miles, I reached the heart of the Mesa Wind Farm. This place is known to be hiker friendly and I stopped for a while under what used to be a large wooden tent to get some shade. The temperatures were rising and it was only 8:30am.

![Gold Canyon and Mesa Wind Farm (33°58’13.344″ N 116°40’25.092″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6278-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Teutang Canyon (33°58’16.4359″ N 116°40’25.585″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6286-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Well rested, I continued up the canyon to a narrow pass between Gold Canyon and Teutang Canyon. The views to both sides were spectacular and rewarding. To the south you could see the wind farm with San Jacinto Peak looming in the distance. To the north was the San Bernardino Mountains. After the pass, the trail zigzagged down quite a bit, eventually revealing a glimpse of the Whitewater River. Or rather the almost dry but exceptionally wide bed of the Whitewater River. It was mind boggling to imagine nature’s force as it had carved this gigantic stream bed and pushed all these grey rocks downstream.

![The Whitewater River in the distance (33°58’54.342″ N 116°40’6.396″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6312-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The wide bed of Whitewater River (33°59’22.302″ N 116°39’55.506″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6326-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

After another break, I tackled the last switchbacks down to the river bed. Whitewater Preserve – a popular resting place for thru hikers – was half a mile across the river bed to the south. I continued north on the edge of the river bed. I could really feel the heat now. The rocks and sand were radiating. The trail eventually crossed the river bed. Route finding was a bit more difficult as any kind of marker would be seasonally swept away, but I eventually saw a large human made pile of rocks on the other side. The river was still flowing, but it was a very shallow stream just a few feet wide. I was able to cross it with caution without getting my feet wet.

![Crossing the Whitewater River bed (34°0’49.47″ N 116°40’8.934″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6336-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Looking back to Whitewater River and San Jacinto Peak (34°1’8.37″ N 116°39’54.06″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6338-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

On the other side, the trail ascended again to another pass. The surrounding canyon almost reminded me of Death Valley National Park, lots of different colors of rock and increasingly less vegetation. The view from the pass was worth it. Zigzagging down again, the trail soon met the West Fork Trail to Mission Creek Preserve. This would have been a shorter way back to my car. But I had decided to continue on for another 4 miles before taking the way back. Ahead of me was another long ascent of almost 1,000 feet. The trail was arcing across the north side of a valley and then ascending to the ridge.

![San Gorgonio Mountain in the distance (34°1’41.208″ N 116°39’6.024″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6351-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![San Jacinto Peak still dominates the scene even this far away (34°2’7.712″ N 116°39’30.4275″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6378-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The views from the ridgeline were truly stunning. The valley I had just climbed out of was impressive on its own, but the sheer imposing mountain range beyond was even more remarkable. To the south, San Jacinto Peak was still looming. I could now see part of the Mission Creek bed, almost as wide as the Whitewater River’s bed. The trail followed the ridgeline for a while before steeply zigzagging down to the Mission Creek bed.

![The first view of Mission Creek (34°2’19.258″ N 116°39’21.5677″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6381-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Descending to Mission Creek (34°2’20.1319″ N 116°39’13.7428″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6388-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I left the PCT here and turned downstream onto an old and somewhat overgrown dirt road. Mission Creek had seen severe flooding in 2023 which had completely reshaped the creek bed. The dirt road was completely washed away in a number of places and you could still see large piles of debris which gave me an indication as to how high and forceful the water must have been. I reached Mission Creek Preserve. The sun was burning now. The last few miles were on a dirt road which made for easy walking. The temperature in the shade was indeed 88°F / 31°C but there had been almost no shade along the entire trail. As I reached my car, I met the first humans since the two cyclists in the early morning. It was very clear that thru hikers were much further north now.

![Mission Creek (34°1’42.312″ N 116°38’7.89″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/281A6392-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt. Several very sandy stretches north of Snow Creek. No defined trail across the San Gorgonio River. The track along Mission Creek is completely washed out in several places.

**Parking:** There is plenty of parking space at the Mission Creek Preserve.

**Weather:** Sunny with blue skies and no clouds. Extremely windy north of Snow Creek with gusts of more than 50 mph / 80 kmh. Windy throughout the rest of the day except in more sheltered areas. Low temperature of 66°F / 19°C in the morning north of Snow Creel. High temperatures of well above 100°F / 38°C along Mission Creek in wind sheltered and exposed areas.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Santa Rosa and San Jacinto Mountains National Monument. Sand to Snow National Monument, including a significant portion within San Gorgonio Wilderness. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.4

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Very Easy
