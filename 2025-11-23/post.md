# Acorn Trail to Vincent Gap

Nov 23, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.373013, -117.752163](https://maps.app.goo.gl/krS27CPfo9HVgrgk8) (Vincent Gap)  
**End:** [34.348968, -117.645573](https://maps.app.goo.gl/iyRpyaofrZFJWMyt6) (Acorn Drive)

**Distance:** 17.5 mi (up to PCT mile 364.1, 13.7% completed)  
**Time:** 10 hours 57 minutes (start 6:39am, end 5:36pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 9,810 ft (low 4,425 ft – high 8,572 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 9,034 ft (start 6,478 ft – end 7,254 ft)

---

Today’s hike was a complete failure. I am lucky that I didn’t have to call Search and Rescue. My plan had been to cover about 10 miles of the PCT as part of a larger 22 mile loop. But I had significantly underestimated how much off-trail hiking and two feet of fresh snow would slow me down. So I ended up not really hiking any part of the PCT at all. But let me start at the beginning.

I was staying at a spacious loft in Wrightwood for the entire week, hoping to do four hikes over the Thanksgiving week. The two weeks before had seen the first few major winter storms of the season. Initially mostly rain, the last 3 days had brought considerable snowfall to the higher elevations. I kept on monitoring the [live webcam](https://ops.alertcalifornia.org/cam-console/2201) on top of Blue Ridge to gauge what conditions would be like on the PCT. There was a solid snow cover now, but it looked like it was just a few inches. In my naivete, I thought this would still be easy to hike through. Little did I know that it was more than just a few inches and that hiking uphill through a prolonged period of time is extremely exhausting (well, I should have known that).

![View towards Blue Ridge and Pine Mountain (34°22’18.426″ N 117°45’0.156″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8876-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Still full of enthusiasm, I got up at 5:30am, left at 6:05am, and arrived at Vincent Gap at 6:23am. Temperatures were just around freezing and on the drive across Blue Ridge, I could see a decent amount of snow next to the road. The parking lot at Vincent Gap had been partially cleared of snow, so it was easy to park. A film of ice on the asphalt made it a very slippery endeavor though. The sun was about to rise and I could see the top of Mount Baden-Powell to the immediate west rising 3,000 feet above me with ice and snow covered trees. It was beautiful in its own way. Three other parties had also arrived and it was clear they were going to summit Mount Baden-Powell – my plan for a few days later. I put on my winter boots and gaiters and set out for the adventure.

![Grizzly bear tracks! (34°22’9.198″ N 117°44’45.714″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8883-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

For the first few minutes, I descended on Big Horn Mine Road, a wider dirt road covered in a very thin layer of frozen snow. Lots of boot tracks made it evident that this road was fairly well traveled. Soon enough I turned onto Vincent Gulch Trail – and it was still covered in pristine snow without any sign of human boot tracks. Instead it had somewhat unexpected tracks from a grizzly bear! The paw pattern was very distinctive. About a mile later I moved below the snow line and was back on solid dirt. The trail switchbacked down the eastern side of Mount Baden-Powell, moving in and out of the burn scar from the 2024 Bridge Fire. The sun was up now but it wasn’t really warming. Vincent Gulch – unsurprisingly – had a fair amount of water, but crossing the stream was still fairly easy.

![Snow covered Mount Baden-Powell from Vincent Gulch (34°20’40.686″ N 117°43’29.742″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8893-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail had been fairly visible up to now, but close to the wider stream bed it fizzled out turning this into an off-trail experience. The stream bed was littered with rocks of all sizes and it felt more like an exercise to find the path of least scrambling. I finally reached the confluence of Vincent Gulch and Prairie Fork San Gabriel River and turned left to follow Prairie Fork upstream. There was a broken wooden sign on the ground pointing in the right direction, but not really a trail either. The banks along Prairie Fork were a lot more overgrown and route finding was much trickier. I had to cross the creek multiple times but kept my feet dry. Poodle dog bush was thriving as well and I carefully avoided coming anywhere close. A very long mile later, I reached Cabin Flat Campground – or rather what little was left of it. I could barely recognize that this had been a campground before had it not been for the small concrete plateau with two toilet seats. Everything else had burned to the ground.

![The burned pit toilets at Cabin Flat Campground (34°20’38.718″ N 117°42’1.752″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8900-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I took a break nearby. It had taken me 4 hours to get to this point and this was barely a quarter of the way. I was getting worried that I would run out of daylight for the rest of the hike. I pushed that thought aside and kept going. I was now on Forest Service Route 3N39, a well graded dirt road that I would follow all the way to the top of Blue Ridge a little under 3,000 vertical feet above me. As I looked back, the view was spectacular. Snow covered Mount Baden-Powell was dominating the horizon. The contrast with the green valley below was stunning. Ahead was snow covered Pine Mountain. About 2 miles later, the first patches of snow appeared on the ground and slowly developed into a consistent blanket. I took another break at Lupine Campground and evaluated my options. Being the optimistic person that I am, I encouraged myself to keep going.

![Leaving Cabin Flat Campground with snow covered Pine Mountain up ahead (34°20’42.45″ N 117°42’1.218″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8904-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The road was now solidly covered in a few inches of snow. Walking had already become harder, every step breaking through the crusty snow. I crossed Prairie Fork one last time and was at the beginning of a much steeper and windy ascent for the last 1,200 feet of elevation gain. The snow got thicker and thicker, now about a foot. Sometimes it was very soft, letting me sink in immediately, sometimes it gave me the illusion of firmness before letting me sink in. I started counting the steps and taking short breaks every few hundred steps. The views of Mount Baldy were amazing, but I couldn’t appreciate them as much as I wanted. I felt like the ascent would never end. Every step felt harder and harder. My gaiters were doing a good job keeping the snow out of my boots. The snow was almost 2 feet thick when I finally reached the top of the ridge.

![Looking back at Mount Baden-Powell from Forest Service Route 3N39 (34°20’36.792″ N 117°41’49.716″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8907-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I turned right on Blue Ridge Truck Trail and reached the PCT junction about 50 yards later. The PCT was about to climb a little more to Guffy Campground. I paused. It was a little after 3pm. I had a little over an hour of daylight left. Conditions on the PCT would be similar to what I had just experienced, lots of snow and very slow going. It was 10 miles on the PCT back to the car. Even if I exited earlier at Inspiration Point and walked the highway the rest of the way, it would still be 6 miles to go and then another 4 miles of road walking. That would be hours into the darkness, even if I was able to physically make it. I could turn around and hike the 13 miles back. But even under the best circumstances that would have taken 5 hours – and circumstances were far from best here. The last alternative was to go east for half a mile on Blue Ridge and then descend to Wrightwood on Acorn Trail, basically the same route I had taken last time. Acorn Trail was on the northern side of the ridge, so certainly also covered in deep snow, likely for a while. That was about 3 miles, but except for the first half mile it was downhill and hopefully there would be less snow the further I descended.

![The beginning of the steeper switchbacks (34°19’58.692″ N 117°39’15.798″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8920-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I weighed the options for a bit. Acorn Trail was the shortest, but it also meant I had no way of getting back to my car. After considering everything carefully, it was the best available option. So I started making my way east on Blue Ridge Truck Trail. It was uphill again. Some snow drifts were deeper than just two feet. I was so exhausted that I could only take 50 steps at a time before I had to take a break. Every step required effort. A quarter mile further, I had very painful cramps in my left thigh muscle. Now I was seriously getting worried. If the pain persisted, I was not sure how I would make my way off this mountain. That would have been a Search and Rescue situation. But eventually the pain subsided and I kept going. I reached the junction with Acorn Trail. Now it was “just” downhill.

![The Prairie Fork valley I had come up from (34°19’52.608″ N 117°39’8.172″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8926-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

But downhill through snow drifts of two feet or more isn’t that much easier. I still had to take frequent short breaks. I could guess where the trail was, partly because I remembered the route from last time, partly because it was not too difficult to “read” the terrain. Half a mile downhill, I saw a hiker on snow shoes coming up. We only chatted very briefly. I didn’t ask why he was still going up when it was almost dark. Soon after, the first boot tracks appeared. Apparently someone had made it up this far. Despite the tracks, it wasn’t that much easier to keep going. The tracks were very deep and the stride length was shorter than mine. The sun had set now and it was rapidly getting colder. Still above freezing, but definitely colder. I kept on walking without a light for as long as I could. The snow cover eventually reduced enough for me to pick a faster pace. I took out my phone to use as a flashlight. I finally reached the end of the trail at Acorn Drive.

![Pine Mountain (34°20’11.97″ N 117°39’26.988″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8938-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Now the question was how to get back to my car. The first option was to try to get an Uber. After 5 minutes of waiting without an assigned driver, I gave up. I walked down Acorn Drive towards Highway 2. A guy was sitting in his car with his lights on half a mile further, I explained my situation but he wasn’t able to give me a ride. I reached Highway 2 and tried to hitchhike – the very first time I have every tried that. Even though there was a considerable number of cars, nobody stopped. In fairness, it was dark and I might not have looked like the most trustworthy person. I walked all the way to the loft where I was staying and dropped my backpack and camera there. I assumed the owner was living on the first floor, so I rang the door bell but they didn’t answer. I reached out to the owner over text message. They responded immediately and let me know that they weren’t actually living in Wrightwood all year round. But they were going to reach out to someone to see if they could help. After 15 minutes they still hadn’t responded. With my keys and wallet in hand I walked back to Highway 2 to a small gas station and market.

![The last sunlight atop Blue Ridge at the junction of Acorn Trail and the PCT (34°20’11.952″ N 117°38’30.558″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8955-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I asked about 10 people until I got to a young couple with a jeep that had just pulled into the gas station. I explained my situation and luckily they were going in the same direction. The guy was a little worried though that I might stab him in the back. So I let him frisk me and even pulled up my shirt so that he could see that I was not wearing any weapons. They agreed to give me a ride for free. When I told them about my day they were somewhat shocked. The girl had apparently never seen snow before in her entire life. It only took 15 minutes to get to Vincent Gap. I hopped in my car and drove back to Wrightwood, very grateful that everything had turned out well in the end.

I am not sure when I will be able to continue hiking this section of the PCT. If the sunny and warmer weather persists, the snow may become more manageable and I might be able to attempt this stretch again later during the week. If not, then I will likely have to wait until spring.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Snow covered for the first mile and past Cabin Flat Campground. Vincent Gulch Trail is in good shape but ends about half a mile from Prairie Creek. Getting from Vincent Gulch to Cabin Flat Campground is a serious off-trail route finding exercise. Lots of rocky terrain with overgrown underbrush and plenty of poodle dog bush. Significant impact from the 2024 Bridge Fire, some stretches are very bare or scarred with black trees.

**Parking:** There is plenty of parking on Vincent Gap.

**Crowds:** Very solitary. Only met one hiker coming up Acorn Trail, no people throughout the rest of the day.

**Weather:** Sunny with blue skies and no clouds throughout the entire day. Low of 36°F / 2°C in the evening after sunset. High of 53°F / 12°C around noon during the ascent to Blue Ridge.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Angeles National Forest. San Gabriel Mountains National Monument. A few miles within Sheep Mountain Wilderness. Annual Interagency Permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 2.2

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Very Difficult
