# CA-74 to Fobes Saddle

Mar 1, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [33.563808, -116.576832](https://maps.app.goo.gl/6snxKfzvPBHznNJr6) (Pacific Crest Trail at CA-74)  
**End:** [33.640830, -116.639603](https://maps.app.goo.gl/JhTgGfM6yN2izPtk8) (Fobes Ranch Road)

**Distance:** 20.9 mi (up to PCT mile 166.5, 6.3% completed)  
**Time:** 8 hours 36 minutes (start 6:25am, end 3:01pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 3,627 ft (low 4,512 ft – high 7,183 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 4,016 ft (start 4,901 ft – end 4,512 ft)

---

The weather forecast had made me somewhat apprehensive. When I had checked again on Thursday, it predicted fog and a chance of showers before 10am and then partly sunny conditions around noon. I had considered postponing this hike, but the urge to continue prevailed. Cloudy weather is often a double edge sword. It likely keeps the temperature down and makes the hike more enjoyable, but it can also deprive me of good photo opportunities. Today, it ended up creating some unique and particularly stunning shots.

![A carefully stacked cairn along the way (33°34’48.966″ N 116°34’19.3001″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/281A4603-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I left the hotel at my usual time of 5:15am. The bike drop off was at the end of unpaved Fobes Ranch Road and I was expecting it to take longer to get there. The first half mile was just sandy, but straightforward to navigate. But once the dirt road started gaining elevation, it became rough. I cautiously avoided deeper holes and large rocks, but about half a mile further it was clear that this was a little too much for my car. I admitted defeat and turned around – not easy on a fairly narrow dirt road going uphill. I stopped at the bottom of the road and chained my bike to a fence on the northern side.

![The clouds over Penrod Canyon are slowly dissipating (33°36’39.732″ N 116°33’53.484″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/281A4625-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Heading to the start of the hike, darkness was fading. And indeed there was a solid layer of fog keeping any sunlight away. The air was cool and moist. But I was not going to lose hope now, maybe in an hour or so the fog would dissipate. I started hiking at 6:25am. The trail kept meandering along shrubs and small trees which wouldn’t have afforded me any views even in good weather. About an hour into the hike, it started drizzling a little bit. The fog cover seemed to lift though and here and there I was able to spot a hole of blue sky.

![Lake Hemet in the distance from a saddle (33°38’33.822″ N 116°34’13.944″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/281A4645-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Looking back at the zigzags up the ridge (33°38’39.3″ N 116°34’2.874″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/281A4649-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

As I gained more elevation, some views of Penrod Canyon and Pine Meadow opened up. Both still had a layer of fog hanging above them. I passed through a gate and reached a saddle with a side trail to Live Oak Spring. Continuing north, I could see vast mountain expanses to the east, albeit shrouded in clouds and fog. As I reached another saddle just south of Lion Peak, I was finally rewarded with the first picturesque views west and some blue sky. San Jacinto Peak was still engulfed in fog and further west the clouds were hanging low. In fact I was pretty much above the clouds at this point. I could even see Lake Hemet in the distance.

![View west down the canyon (33°39’49.8164″ N 116°34’19.6807″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/281A4671-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The San Jacinto Mountains (33°40’42.3077″ N 116°35’45.3966″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/281A4687-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The clouds are stuck just below the ridge (33°40’42.3077″ N 116°35’45.3966″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/281A4690-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Encouraged by the increasingly better weather, I continued north, past the junction with Cedar Springs Trail. The ascent was steep at times. And as I was about to reach the highest point of this hike, just south of Palm View Peak at 7,183 feet, I was surprised by a little pile of snow. Not much really, but snow nonetheless.

![A large cloud trying to move over Fobes Saddle (33°40’48.156″ N 116°35’57.714″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/281A4694-Pano-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x615.jpg)

![Trees burned during the 2013 Mountain Fire (33°40’58.5361″ N 116°36’4.3522″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/281A4707-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Fobes Canyon (33°40’59.8754″ N 116°36’8.344″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/281A4713-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

From here it was all downhill. Ahead was Fobes Saddle and a long series of switchbacks through an area burned by the 2013 Mountain Fire. To the east, Palm Springs was very recognizable. But the view was stunning. San Jacinto Peak in the north was now very clear. And a large cloud was moving over Fobes Saddle providing me with a unique spectacle. I couldn’t help but marvel at this weather phenomenon. On Fobes Saddle, I left the PCT and headed southwest down to Fobes Canyon. I slowly moved below the clouds that kept on moving up the canyon. And then within a few minutes, things changed. The clouds started piling up and getting darker and darker making it a surreal and intimidating experience.

![Spitler Peak (33°41’1.98″ N 116°36’12.486″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/281A4724-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![View down Fobes Canyon (33°41’14.9738″ N 116°36’43.8284″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/281A4735-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

When I reached Fobes Ranch Road, I knew I had another 3 miles to go to get back to my bike. The road was easy walking though. I reached my bike at 3pm and hadn’t met a single person for the entire day.

![Clouds above Spitler Peak (33°41’6.372″ N 116°36’39.84″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/281A4740-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt with some rocky and sandy stretches, somewhat overgrown at times.

**Parking:** With a 4WD you could drive all the way to the start of Fobes Trail where there is plenty of room to park. However, Fobes Ranch Road is pretty rough and not advisable for a 2WD, so I recommend parking at the junction of Fobes Ranch Road (FS 6S05) and Quinn Flat Road (FR 6S23).

**Weather:** Low hanging fog in the morning with a slight drizzle. Partly sunny after 10am. Increasing clouds past 1pm, eventually becoming a thick, dark cloud cover. Low temperature of 48°F at 7am, two miles north of CA-74. High temperature of 62°F on Fobes Trail at 1:40pm descending from the PCT.

**Land Manager & Permits:** San Bernardino National Forest. No permits required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.4

Difficulty: Moderate  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Easy

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 7.1 mi (40 min), 526 ft elevation gain, 10.47 mph average speed

The bike ride back was short and uneventful, albeit mostly uphill. As I approached the last mile back to the car, the fog was back on the ground and visibility limited to 100 yards. Humidity reached more than 80%.
