# Haiwee Pass Trail to Olancha Pass Trail (Day 2)

Jul 19, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Central California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/central-california/)

**Start:** [36.139569, -118.129383](https://maps.app.goo.gl/hrFrby2bkp7kNnv8A) (Meadow along Haiwee Pass Trail)  
**End:** [36.225605, -118.061429](https://maps.app.goo.gl/H5We7V8h4uiEUFuB6) (Olancha Pass Trailhead)

**Distance:** 16.5 mi (up to PCT mile 721.8, 27.2% completed)  
**Time:** 8 hours 35 minutes (start 6:30am, end 3:06pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 2,122 ft (low 5,841 ft – high 9,227 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 4,878 ft (start 8,597 ft – end 5,841 ft)

---

My alarm went off at 5:30am. It was dawning outside and I had slept pretty well. The wilderness was still, peaceful, and quiet. I boiled some water for breakfast, TrailFork Peanut Butter Banana Oats. It didn’t take long to remember two things: first, I really don’t care for peanut butter; second, the packet’s “best by” date was back in 2019. It was a chore to force down, but I knew I would need every calorie for the miles ahead. I drank the last of my water, but still felt a bit dehydrated. Finding a reliable water source along the PCT would be my immediate priority. Packing up went quickly and I headed west by 6:30am. The remaining stretch of Haiwee Pass Trail was as grueling as expected given the experience during the last hike. Although I had a better sense of the terrain this time, the dense brush required constant checks on my offline map. Bushwhacking through heavy scrub is tough on any day, but doing it with a full pack was a real workout. When I finally reached the PCT, I was relieved. Instantly, the path transformed into well-engineered, clear tread.

The PCT descended gently towards Beck Meadow, the southeast finger of Monache Meadow which is considered the largest meadow complex in the Sierra Nevada. The view was captivating. In the distance was Mount Langley. The sprawling meadow looked gentle and inviting. There was cattle grazing in the middle of the meadow. My first priority was still water though. Based on my map, there was a cattle trough on the eastern side of the meadow not far from the trail. I left the PCT and headed towards the trough but I could already tell from the distance that it was bone dry. Luckily though, there was a spring a mere 20 yards above the trough that was still bubbling out of the ground. It wasn’t easy to scoop up water out of the shallow trickling pool, but I did manage to collect 2 liters / 0.5 gallons and waited for it to filter. I sat on a rock in the grass and enjoyed the view of Beck Meadow in the meantime.

Back on the PCT, the trail climbed up the northwest flank of Deer Mountain, crossed a saddle, then switchbacked to a sagebrush flat. The eastern expanse of Monache Meadow was in front of me and gorgeous to look at. The South Fork Kern River was winding through the meadow with a few cows grazing at a river bend. The reflective ribbon of the river carved a lazy, sweeping loop through the golden-green flats, framed by pine-covered hills and distant high country peaks. A steel footbridge led across the now gentle river. Standing on the bridge and gazing west, the river turned into a smooth, dark mirror reflecting the vibrant blue sky, winding past quiet sandbars toward the heart of Monache Meadow. It really sank in: This is the High Sierra! Beyond the river, the trail continued north, drifting gently uphill toward Cow Canyon. The open sagebrush meadow slowly narrowed until pine trees reappeared to offer welcome pockets of shade. I took a short break under a large pine, preparing for the next big effort: Ahead of me was a 1,000 feet ascent up Cow Canyon along Cow Creek. It was still nicely flowing. The gradient was steep, so I locked into a slow, rhythmic pace, letting the steady beat of my boots carry me upward.

As the canyon narrowed, tall pines closed in to offer cool shade, and puffy cumulus clouds began building overhead, a welcome shield against the midday sun. Reaching Upper Cow Canyon, the Olancha Pass Trail merged in from the west, sharing a brief stretch with the PCT. I had run out of water again and was thirsty, so I stopped at the last crossing of Cow Creek and filtered more water. A few hundred yards ahead, I left the PCT again to head towards Olancha Pass. The trail paralleled Summit Meadow which was covered in beautiful wildflowers. I also passed a large cattle enclosure, a nostalgic relic from before this area was protected as wilderness. There were a few more clouds now which I appreciated on the way back to the high desert floor. I anticipated scorching temperatures on the way down. Crossing the familiar Olancha Pass, I had the final 5 miles of downhill ahead of me. Fortunately, the clouds kept me in the shade for most of the time. I could appreciate the far reaching views east across the desert. I took one final brief break to rest my knees, but with the car in sight far below, I pushed through the last hot mile in the full sun. Reaching the trailhead brought immense relief, wrapping up an incredible, challenging 33-mile High Sierra loop.

**Trail Conditions:** Haiwee Pass Trail east of the Pacific Crest Trail was significantly impacted by the 2008 Clover Fire. The trail is very overgrown and hard to follow. Bushwhacking required. The Pacific Crest Trail is in great shape, albeit a bit sandy at times. Olancha Pass Trail is in good condition as well.

**Parking:** There is plenty of room for cars at the trailhead.

**Crowds:** Very lightly traveled. Only met 3 southbound backpackers throughout the entire day.

**Weather:** Sunny with no clouds in the morning. Partly cloudy with a light intermittent breeze in the afternoon. Low of 48°F / 9°C in the morning along Beck Meadow. High of 90°F / 32°C in the afternoon near Olancha Pass Trailhead. Humidity between 67% in the morning and 28% in the afternoon.

**Land Manager & Permits:** South Sierra Wilderness. Sequoia National Forest and Inyo National Forest. Wilderness permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.8

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Very Easy
