# Mill Creek Summit to Indian Canyon Trailhead (Day 2)

Jan 11, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.379903, -118.189440](https://maps.app.goo.gl/oPtAEa7pUE2kmAcR7) (Messenger Flats Campground)  
**End:** [34.436850, -118.273192](https://maps.app.goo.gl/p2Krm6AD5M1yVA9y5) (Indian Canyon Trailhead)

**Distance:** 15.2 mi (up to PCT mile 444.3, 16.8% completed)  
**Time:** 7 hours 17 minutes (start 7:01am, end 2:18pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 1,765 ft (low 2,278 ft – high 5,883 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 5,342 ft (start 5,855 ft – end 2,278 ft)

---

I woke up at 11pm. The wind was violently shaking the tent. Apparently the wind direction had shifted and the restroom was no longer giving me the protection that I had hoped for. Gusts of 50 mph were making me wonder whether the tent would keep standing and whether I had tethered it to the ground enough. I could feel the cold air blowing in from below the tent flaps. The sleeping bag was only helping so much. I put on my second hoodie and a second pair of pants and put my feet into the arms of my jacket to retain as much body heat as I could. This was all I had in terms of insulation. Fortunately, it was enough and I felt reasonably warm – except maybe in my face.

The wind was loud and the shaking tent was not helping either. I kept drifting in and out of sleep but didn’t get more than 3 hours total. By 6am, a very fine layer of dirt was coating pretty much everything inside the tent. The wind had not lessened and I was strategizing in my head how to best get up and get going. I needed to minimize the time between leaving the tent and disassembling it. My main worry was that the tent would just blow away while I was at it. I packed everything while I was still inside the tent, then got out and put my backpack and camera at the more protected restroom entrance. As soon as I had pulled out two of the tent stakes, the wind was blowing the tent in the opposite direction. I quickly pulled out the rest and dragged the tent inside the restroom where I would have an easier time taking it apart and packing it back up.

![Mill Canyon (34°23’8.5538″ N 118°14’33.0624″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9830-Pano-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x647.jpg)

![North Fork Saddle (34°23’13.1165″ N 118°14’45.8726″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9840-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Since I had run out of propane, I couldn’t have a hot breakfast and tea – which is what I had planned for – so I just had two cereal bars and a bottle of seemingly ice cold water. Looking at my Kestrel’s temperature readings, it had dropped to 38 degrees during the night. At least it wasn’t freezing. I had put my water filter inside the sleeping bag though, just in case. The sun would take a bit longer to come across the hills, but I left to get a head start. The first 1.5 miles were the same as the day before, moving west and rounding Messenger Peak. But the lighting was very different now. When I reached the spring to stock up on water, it was also time to take off some clothing. Taking off two pairs of pants and putting one back on is surprisingly difficult when you have no place to sit or stand without shoes and the ground isn’t even level.

![The head of Mattox Canyon, looking towards Three Sisters Rock (34°24’19.0906″ N 118°14’40.1266″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9875-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Crossing Moody Truck Trail, I entered another difficult sections of blowdowns. All of them were eventually manageable, but some took time. And with some I had to break off a bunch of branches to make it easier to pass. The PCT joined Mount Gleason Road for about 100 yards before rounding an unnamed peak on its southern side. Views of Pacoima Canyon were pleasant. The trail switchbacked a number of times and turned west again, now following the head of Mill Canyon. I could see all the way to Soledad Canyon in the distance and what would likely be the PCT on the other side of the canyon. 2 miles later, I reached North Fork Saddle. It had a nice picnic area where I took a break and inspected the closed ranger station. Back on the PCT, a bearded ranger shouted at me from afar whether I needed water. I politely declined and moved on.

![Soledad Canyon with Parker Mountain in the background (34°26’11.9346″ N 118°16’36.4382″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/281A9919-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail was much more exposed now and therefore also much warmer. I shed another layer. I could see BPL Road on the other side of the canyon winding its way up and down. The PCT finally wound north and started a steep descent into Mattox Canyon along the top of a relatively narrow ridgeline. In the distance to the north, I could already see Vasquez Rocks, a peculiar rock formation that stood out. Even further in the distance was the bald Sierra Pelona range. The PCT dropped into Mattox Canyon which had a strongly flowing seasonal stream with a number of great camp sites next to it. I had another break and ate my last food. The trail headed uphill again to reach another ridgeline with Indian Canyon to the west, then descended again following the outline of Fryer Canyon. After another steep uphill stretch, the PCT crossed Indian Canyon Road and gently rounded the ridge before finally reaching Indian Canyon Trailhead. I was glad to be back at my car. The scenery had been great, but the short night and the wind had taken a toll on me.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Lots and lots of blowdowns between Messenger Flats Campground and North Fork Saddle Ranger Station, some very difficult to pass given dense vegetation and steep terrain. Significant impact from the 2009 Station Fire and 2016 Sand Fire throughout most of the hike.

**Parking:** There is plenty of parking at Indian Canyon Trailhead.

**Crowds:** Complete solitude. I only met a ranger at North Fork Saddle Ranger Station who was asking me whether I needed water.

**Weather:** Mostly sunny with blue skies with some cirrus clouds. Low of 42°F / 6°C in the morning at the start of the hike. High of 63°F / 17°C on the exposed ridge between Mill Canyon and Mattox Canyon. Very strong winds of 20 to 30 mph with gusts of 50 mph.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Angeles National Forest. San Gabriel Mountains National Monument. Annual Interagency Permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.2

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Moderate  
Logistics: Easy
