# Vincent Gap to Eagles Roost

Dec 13, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.373060, -117.752208](https://maps.app.goo.gl/dwdW8GhvLNRZ5wRq7) (Vincent Gap)  
**End:** [34.354453, -117.877818](https://maps.app.goo.gl/jpYMZ3fRnKVorFLu7) (Eagles Roost Picnic Area)

**Distance:** 16.8 mi (up to PCT mile 390.2, 14.7% completed)  
**Time:** 10 hours 30 minutes (start 6:28am, end 4:58pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 5,326 ft (low 6,582 ft – high 9,407 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 5,254 ft (start 6,582 ft – end 6,654 ft)

---

I finally summitted Mount Baden-Powell! The peak had teased me for the last four hikes, first without snow, then with too much snow. But today was finally the day I conquered this mountain! Although I can’t say that everything went according to plan, but more about that later.

![Half way up the switchbacks to Mount Baden-Powell (34°22’2.592″ N 117°45’36.714″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9142-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The final push to the summit of Mount Baden-Powell (34°21’39.708″ N 117°45’45.912″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9151-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

My car was repaired after a large rock had torn off some of the metal plating on the bottom during the last trip to Wrightwood. I was hoping to have better luck this time getting past 9,400 foot Mount Baden-Powell which was gating any subsequent hikes throughout the winter. I was staying in Hesperia again, so the drive to the trailhead was a bit longer this time. I left the hotel at 4:44am and arrived at Eagles Roost at 5:43am. It was still completely dark and I chained my bike to one of the large signs before making my way back to Vincent Gap. Three cars were already parked there. Mount Baden-Powell was a popular destination.

![Mount Baden-Powell from just below the summit, with the Santa Ana Mountains in the distance (34°21’38.58″ N 117°45’46.866″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9155-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![View towards Blue Ridge and the Mojave Desert from just below the summit of Mount Baden-Powell (34°21’38.58″ N 117°45’46.866″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9158-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Pine Mountain Ridge with snow covered Pine Mountain and Mount Baldy in the distance (34°21’34.59″ N 117°45’49.332″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9161-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Sunrise was at 6:49am, but I already left Vincent Gap at 6:28am. Ahead of me were almost 3,000 feet of elevation gain over 4 miles via more than 40 switchbacks. The first few were already completely snow free, but soon enough patches of icy snow appeared and I had to put on my microspikes to avoid slipping. Snow free and snow covered stretches alternated for a while as the sun was slowly coming up in the east. The switchbacks were on the northern side of the mountain and snow had lingered here for much longer. The snow cover became a solid blanket with well worn boot tracks making it pretty easy to follow the seemingly endless switchbacks uphill. I could follow all but the last 4 switchbacks, it was straight uphill from there.

![The summit plaque on Mount Baden-Powell (34°21’30.642″ N 117°45’52.71″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9167-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Descending from Mount Baden-Powell to Mount Burnham (34°21’31.59″ N 117°45’54.21″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9185-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Palmdale and Lancaster in the distance (34°21’40.662″ N 117°46’23.61″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9191-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

When I finally reached the top of the ridge, the views were spectacular. The south facing side was almost completely snow free, but the north facing slope still had several feet of snow. After a final push, I reached the summit. To the east were Blue Ridge and Mount Baldy. To the south was the deeply incised valley of Iron Fork San Gabriel River. To the north was the desert and the southern Sierra. And to the west I could see the next few peaks on my route today, namely Mount Burnham and Throop Peak. There was a large American flag on the summit as well as a concrete monument to Lord Baden-Powell whom the peak was named after. I had a break next to the monument and put on my gaiters. I wasn’t expecting any boot tracks from now on and the snow would likely still be deep in places.

![Mount Burnham and Throop Peak ahead (34°21’40.662″ N 117°46’23.61″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9192-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Mount Burnham (34°21’33.1543″ N 117°46’52.62″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9203-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Iron Fork San Gabriel River drainage (34°21’27.906″ N 117°47’9.294″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9206-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The PCT stayed close to the top of the ridge, but was often slightly below on the northern side that was still deeply covered in snow. My plan was to stick to the top of the ridge as much as possible even if it meant deviating slightly from the PCT. First it was downhill. You would think downhill should be significantly faster, but without an obvious path and snow drifts it wasn’t. My microspikes did a good job keeping me on solid footing even on steep slopes. Approaching the next smaller unnamed peak, I made the mistake of veering too much to the northern side of the ridge. Deep snow drifts and fluffy snow made me regret that very quickly.

![Snow covered Mount Baldy in the distance (34°20’42.714″ N 117°48’12.534″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9226-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Mount Hawkins (34°20’28.314″ N 117°48’20.112″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9228-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

For the next peak – Mount Burnham – I opted going straight up to the peak so that there would be less snow. And that was much easier. A colorful sign marked the summit. The next peak from here was Throop Peak, but first it was 500 feet downhill and back uphill again. I took another break on the top of the ridge with stunning views of the Iron Fork San Gabriel River before tackling the uphill portion. I passed Dawson Saddle Trail which had a few boot tracks and turned left. The PCT was not going all the way to the summit but stayed somewhat level on the southern side. The pleasant side effect of that was less snow and even some snow free stretches.

![View to the west from Mount Hawkins (34°20’28.314″ N 117°48’20.112″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9230-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The northern side of Mount Hawkins with Mount Williamson in the distance (34°20’27.678″ N 117°48’22.536″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9232-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Looking back up the ridge to Mount Hawkins (34°20’29.19″ N 117°48’34.956″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9235-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The last peak ahead was Mount Hawkins. The PCT took a path on the snow covered northern side, but I took the direct path to the summit to avoid the snow. Two hikers were at the top taking pictures of themselves with yet another colorful peak sign. The views were still very rewarding. Mount Islip was to the west, the Crystal Lake Recreation Area just below. From here it was downhill for quite a while. I passed Windy Gap where the trail moved to the northern side. Being below 8,000 feet now, the snow was manageable though. Soon after, I reached Little Jimmy Trail Camp and there were a handful of people setting up their tents.

![South Fork Iron Fork San Gabriel River drainage (34°20’29.358″ N 117°48’39.42″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9238-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![View towards the Crystal Lake area (34°20’23.886″ N 117°49’2.994″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9243-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Mount Williamson from the snow covered northern side of the ridge east of Windy Gap (34°20’28.266″ N 117°49’15.954″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9250-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The PCT paralleled a dirt road to the campground and kept descending, eventually emerging on an open grassy patch and then switchbacking down to Islip Saddle to cross Highway 2. I took off my microspikes, the remaining route looked snow free and took another break at a picnic table on Islip Saddle. It was already past 3pm and there wouldn’t be much daylight left. The next 1.7 miles were steeply uphill again towards Mount Williamson. My pace was slow, the snow hiking had zapped the energy out of me a little. It was also completely in the shade already but with great views back towards Throop Peak. I was relieved when I finally reached the highest point. If I had had more time left, I would have taken the route all the way to the summit of Mount Williamson, but sunrise was minutes away, so I kept going.

![Crystal Lake area (34°20’27.348″ N 117°49’26.604″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9256-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The South Fork Big Rock Creek drainage (34°21’24.858″ N 117°50’59.55″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9284-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The snow covered northern side of Mount Islip and Throop Peak (34°21’44.274″ N 117°51’19.434″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9295-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail switchbacked down a tonalite ravine west of Mount Williamson. Views of the fire impacted Pleasant View Ridge and Winston Peak in the distance were breathtaking. I crossed Highway 2 one last time. The PCT clung to the southern slope of Kratka Ridge. The sun had set now. I had about a mile to go. And the bike ride back would be 13 miles with 1,400 feet of elevation gain – pretty much all in the dark and when I was fairly exhausted. I was not looking forward to that. But what alternative did I have? Maybe I could hitchhike, but in the last 10 minutes not a single car had come by. I was a quarter mile away from Eagles Roost and saw a car turning into the parking lot. My hopes went up! I almost ran to the parking lot, expecting that the car might leave any second. I caught the driver getting back into their car, about to leave, and shouted. I explained my situation and asked them whether they could give me a ride back to Vincent Gap. It was on the way for them anyway. After some hesitation, they agreed and asked me to get in the back. I was so relieved and grateful. It was almost completely dark now and this stranger’s helpfulness meant saving another hour or more in the dark when I was already feeling super exhaused.

![Closed Highway 39 from just below Mount Williamson (34°21’50.538″ N 117°51’30.342″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9300-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Sunset view of the Bear Creek drainage (34°21’57.006″ N 117°51’36.54″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9306-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The last sun rays above the Bear Creek drainage (34°21’54.408″ N 117°51’53.928″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/281A9313-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Within 20 minutes we were back at Vincent Gap. I was glad the day had ended the way it did. Luck was back on my side!

---

**Trail Conditions:** Several feet of snow going up to Mount Baden-Powell, but with easy-to-follow boot tracks. Often no visible trail between Mount Baden-Powell and Throop Peak, occasional deeper snow drifts. Patchy snow between Throop Peak and Islip Saddle. Mostly snow free between Islip Saddle and Eagles Roost. Significant impact from the 2020 Bobcat Fire between Little Jimmy Trail Camp and Eagles Roost.

**Parking:** There is plenty of parking at Vincent Gap.

**Crowds:** Fairly solitary. Only met 10 people throughout the day. 7 day hikers on the way to Mount Baden-Powell, one hiker who was going from Dawson Saddle to Mount Baden-Powell, and two hikers on Mount Hawkins.

**Weather:** Mostly sunny with blue skies with a few cirrus clouds. Low of 46°F / 8°C during the ascent to Mount Baden-Powell. High of 62°F / 16°C near Islip Saddle in the afternoon.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Angeles National Forest. San Gabriel Mountains National Monument. A short stretch of Pleasant View Ridge Wilderness. Annual Interagency Permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 4.3

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Superb  
Logistics: Easy
