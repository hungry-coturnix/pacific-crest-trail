# CA-78 to Oriflamme Mountain

Dec 22, 2024 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [33.098417, -116.471580](https://maps.app.goo.gl/5wF9xeQ39ovpuybi8) (CA-78, at the junction with San Felipe Road)  
**End:** [32.978302, -116.524703](https://maps.app.goo.gl/MzSeoXLvWLGtse9m7) (Sunrise Trailhead)

This is a reverse hike to reduce elevation gain on the way back.

**Distance:** 18.9 mi (up to PCT mile 77.2, 2.9% completed)  
**Time:** 7 hours 52 minutes (start 6:46am, end 2:39pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 4,455 ft (low 2,241 ft – high 5,085 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 1,715 ft (start 2,263 ft – end 5,003 ft)

---

The day promised to be moody. When I arrived at Sunrise Trailhead at 6:01am to drop off my bike, I could already tell by the clouds in the west even though it was still pretty dark. The way to the start of the hike was long but I managed to arrive at the junction of CA-78 and San Felipe Road about 9 minutes before sunrise, just in time to get going when the sun actually came up.

Briefly paralleling CA-78 I crossed the dry bed of San Felipe Creek and noticed the stash of water bottles below the overpass that a thoughtful trail angel had apparently left there for through hikers in need of water. Two camping chairs and a multi-language sign reminding hikers not to forget their poles completed the picture. Given that the trail north had a reputation to be blistering hot (which I would find out on my next hike), I admired the sight for a second and moved on.

Crossing San Diego County Road S2, the trail went alongside the road for about half a mile before heading southwest in a straight line towards Granite Mountain, paralleling a barbed wire fence on the left indicating the boundary to Anza-Borrego Desert State Park. The area was covered in buckhorn cholla, teddy bear cholla, narrow leaf yucca, and agave americana. The trail crossed into the state park through a metal gate at 1.7 miles turning south.

Reaching the base of Granite Mountain, the trail turned west and started to gain elevation. The view north towards Grapevine Mountain was framed with purple hued clouds. Taking my first break after 5 miles overlooking the San Felipe Valley, I noticed a rustling in the shrubs close by. Moments later, a flock of about a dozen quail emerged foraging through the leafy underbrush – too skittish to take photos of though. The weather had become mostly overcast at this point which was not too bad given the ascent, but not great for photos.

The trail continued southwest towards the much greener hills on the western side of Chariot Canyon and crossed Rodriguez Spur Truck Trail at about 10 miles. Leaving northern vistas behind, views into Mason Valley to the southeast started opening up. The trail passed along the western flank of Chariot Mountain before briefly following a very steep dirt road that eventually joined with Chariot Canyon Road. The sun had come out again leaving me with a few more photo opportunities.

![View east while climbing out of Chariot Canyon (33°0’48.258″ N 116°32’12.618″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4226-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Flatter terrain after crossing Mason Valley Truck Trail (33°0’34.5″ N 116°32’16.134″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/281A4229-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The final major ascent for the day was from here towards the Cuyamaca Mountains and it was very steep. With small steps and a constant pace, I eventually reached the top and crossed Mason Valley Truck Trail. From here, the trail meandered south with Oriflamme Mountain coming into view. I eventually turned right onto the familiar spur trail towards Sunrise Trailhead to reach the end of the hike. I had not met a single person for the entire day.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt or sand, with lots of loose rocks.

**Parking:** There is ample parking available right at the junction.

**Weather:** Partly sunny in the morning, then mostly overcast until noon, partly sunny again in the afternoon. Intermittent mild breeze. High of 76°F (around noon, shortly before the junction with Chariot Canyon Road) and a low of 57°F (early in the morning, at the start of the hike).

**Land Manager & Permits:** Anza-Borrego Desert State Park. No permits required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.0

Difficulty: Moderate  
Scenery: Medium  
Logistics: Easy

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 20.7 mi (1 hour 15 min), 459 ft elevation gain, 16.58 mph average speed, 39.23 mph maximum speed

The bike ride back was not too difficult, but it was long. The first 3 miles towards CA-79 were mostly downhill except for a steep hill towards the end that I ended up taking on foot instead. Following the windy CA-79 into Julian was an up and down but manageable at a lower speed. The stretch from Julian to Banner was another matter entirely. The road dropped by more than 1,600 feet and was extremely windy, prompting me to take wide turns as much as the road and oncoming traffic permitted. Past Banner, the highway mostly leveled out except for one last major hill. From the top of that hill, the last stretch was downhill only and it was here that I managed to reach a maximum speed of over 39 mph. I got back to the car well before sunset to conclude the day.
