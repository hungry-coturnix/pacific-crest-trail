# CA-138 to Broken Arrow Road

Mar 28, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.776588, -118.607031](https://maps.app.goo.gl/HTYMFertGCZN7Aom9) (269th Street West & CA-138)  
**End:** [34.899877, -118.455083](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Xq7YFx9D9msao42KA) (Aqueduct Road & Broken Arrow Road)

**Distance:** 17.6 mi (up to PCT mile 535.1, 20.2% completed)  
**Time:** 6 hours 4 minutes (start 6:55am, end 12:59pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 560 ft (low 2,864 ft – high 3,161 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 495 ft (start 3,040 ft – end 3,105 ft)

---

It was the tail end of a historic heat wave in California. March temperatures in Southern California had been well above 90°F / 32°C. It had started to cool a little but it was still uncomfortably hot for hiking. Today’s section was – not unreasonably – called the “death march” by thru hikers because of its long shadeless walk through the unforgiving and often brutally hot California Desert. Many thru hikers opted to do this section at night when temperatures were much more bearable. I had packed an extra bottle of water and was optimistic that I could do this hike during the day.

![The Tehachapi Mountains from Highway 138 (34°46’34.848″ N 118°36’28.122″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/281A1597-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

Today’s hike also marked another amazing milestone: I have hiked 20% of the Pacific Crest Trail. It has been 1.5 years now since I embarked on this adventure at the Mexican border and the trail has had a lot of experiences for me – great and not so great ones. I am still ahead of my originally planned schedule, but I also know that I will need to pause this coming winter when I’ll be in the heart of the Sierra Nevada. The last 10% since Doble have passed in a whim. The San Bernardino and San Gabriel Mountains were spectacular, demanding, and memorable. I have had the most beautiful snow hikes as well as scorching hot days. And now I am about to enter the Sierra Nevada, one of the most beautiful stretches along the Pacific Crest Trail. Similar to the 10% milestone, I’ll share some trail statistics at the end of this post.

![The California Aqueduct with the Tehachapi Mountains in the distance (34°47’16.668″ N 118°35’32.91″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/281A1609-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I had purchased a new bicycle after losing the old during the last trip. And I had also bought a new car that would make it easier to drive rugged dirt roads and to bring my bicycle along without having to disassemble it every time. The end of today’s hike was off pavement on a dirt road in northern Antelope Valley and I was excited to see how my new 2026 Subaru Crosstrek Wilderness would do. I had stayed in Frazier Park again and left the hotel at 5:32am. Turning north from CA-138 onto 170th Street West, I still had a few miles of paved road before turning left onto Broken Arrow Road, a sandy dirt road entering a large area with wind turbines. Sunset was still half an hour away, but I could see enough of the terrain. The Subaru did very well on this kind of surface. About 1.5 miles later, I reached Aqueduct Road and the intersection with the PCT. There was no easy way to chain my bike to something at this intersection, so I turned left and crossed Cottonwood Creek over a concrete bridge and reached a small maintenance lean-to with a hand rail, the perfect spot to leave my bike. This time, I didn’t have to get my bike out of the trunk and fit the front tire back in. I could just take the bike off the bike rack on my trailer hitch – it was so much easier and faster!

![A Tufted Evening Primrose on the berm next to the California Aqueduct (34°47’16.518″ N 118°35’31.368″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/281A1612-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I left again at 6:21am, making my way back to the same spot where I had parked last time, just off CA-138. When I arrived at 6:46am, it was just a minute before sunrise. The clouds to the east were orange and yellow. It was already 58°F / 15°C, but I decided to keep my hoodie on for the time being. After having another piece of baguette for some energy, I walked back to the intersection with the highway to get back onto the PCT. The “trail” was crossing a field along a fence and was covered in high grass. I suspect most thru hikers probably walked the paved road instead of taking this artificial detour. There were lots of California Poppies along the way, but their buds were still closed for now. Crossing Lancaster Road, I passed by a shuttered elementary school abandoned long ago and reached the end of the pavement. Behind the berm up ahead was the California Aqueduct. The view was great. The Tehachapi Mountains were in the distance, not covered in snow anymore like last time. And everything in between was still green.

![Walking on the Los Angeles Aqueduct (34°48’13.83″ N 118°35’14.058″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/281A1622-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![The Los Angeles Aqueduct is a pretty rusty pipe (34°48’43.308″ N 118°35’14.376″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/281A1631-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The PCT followed the aqueduct on the berm for about a mile before reaching Three Points Road and crossing over a concrete bridge. After briefly following the northern side of the aqueduct, the PCT turned due north at the Los Angeles Aqueduct which was intersecting with the California Aqueduct here. The trail was technically next to the aqueduct following a dirt road, but I decided to walk on top of the aqueduct itself which was a large rusty pipe sticking slightly out of the ground. The views from there were stunning and I could see several miles ahead. About 2 miles later, I reached the lowest point of today’s hike. There were two dogs coming to greet me. Just a bit further, I crossed from Los Angeles County into Kern County, only the fourth county since the Mexican border. Both sides of the aqueduct were lined with a sprawling “forest” of Joshua trees.

![A very flat stretch of Aqueduct Road (34°50’4.434″ N 118°35’8.49″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/281A1641-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![A fine Joshua tree specimen (34°50’12.198″ N 118°34’32.19″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/281A1646-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The PCT eventually turned right following the Aqueduct Road for the next 6 miles. This was the access road for the aqueduct and half concrete road, half dirt road. And it was exceptionally flat! I passed by a few residences. A kind soul had also left about 10 bottles of water at their driveway for thru hikers. There were a few shrubs and the occasional Joshua tree along the way but it was somewhat monotonous. The views left to the Tehachapi Mountains and right down Antelope Valley were far reaching. I passed a house where a few men were tearing the roof apart. They waved at me and one of them even yelled “Do you wanna work a little?” which I politely declined. It was quite hot now and I shed my hoodie. There was no shade and the sun was burning relentlessly. I reached a junction with another dirt road and the PCT turned right onto an extremely sandy dirt road. I could hear several motorcyclists in the vicinity plowing through the ravines left and right of this dirt road. The road was straight like an arrow, only making a slight turn left at some point. I took my third break in the small shadow of a lone Joshua tree before tackling the final few miles.

![Expansive views across Antelope Valley (34°51’21.846″ N 118°29’58.074″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/281A1660-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Thistle Sage and California Goldfield along the Pacific Crest Trail (34°51’16.56″ N 118°29’39.792″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/281A1663-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail turned left onto another dirt road and headed up a small canyon. After cresting a ridge with great views across the wind turbine farm ahead, it joined the Los Angeles Aqueduct again. The road was once more half concrete, half dirt. A few bends later, I passed the lean-to with my bike, then crossed Cottonwood Creek over the concrete bridge. There was some water flowing at the bottom, but it looked very silty. Shortly after, I reached the junction with Broken Arrow Road where I would continue my hike next time. I returned to my bike and rested a bit in the shade.

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt roads and paved / concrete roads. No trail tread throughout the entire hike. Some of the dirt roads are very sandy. Completely exposed to the sun with no shade except for the small shadow of the occasional Joshua Tree.

**Parking:** There is room for two cars along 269th St W.

**Crowds:** Absolute solitude. Didn’t meet a single hiker throughout the day. However, there were a few motorcyclists on the Aqueduct Road.

**Weather:** Sunny throughout the entire day with a light intermittent breeze. Low of 58°F / 15°C in the morning at the start of the hike after sunrise. High of 86°F / 30°C in the scarce shade around noon.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Mostly private land. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.0

Scenery: Moderate

The bike ride back was almost identical to the hike. Bikes are usually not allowed on the PCT, but today’s hike had been almost entirely along dirt and paved roads. Therefore it was the shortest way back to my car. The only thing I was not looking forward to was the heat. It was already 86°F / 30°C when I put on my helmet. I had saved 16.9 oz / 500 ml of water to drink along the way, hoping that the bike ride would only take about an hour. But I was very wrong.

The first mile was easy on the concrete portion of the Aqueduct Road. But then it turned back into a sandy dirt road that made it impossible to ride. I had to push my bike several times for longer stretches. Progress was slow. My mouth was parched and I was contemplating whether I actually had enough water now. I had half of my water bottle, but only 10 minutes later, my mouth felt just as parched as before. The heat was brutal and the absence of shade made it worse. The miles dragged on and I finally reached the concrete Aqueduct Road again. At least I wouldn’t have to push my bike anymore. But now there was also a strong headwind making it equally difficult to ride fast. I had the second half of my water bottle and still felt parched – with half the way still to go! I remembered the water bottles at a driveway I had seen earlier. And while I would normally not count on that and leave them to actual thru hikers, this felt like enough of an emergency to actually take some of this water. Another slow 4 miles later, I reached the spot and eagerly gulped down almost a quarter gallon of water. I was very grateful for this kind soul who had deposited water here.

The next 2 miles were still on the Aqueduct Road, then I turned south onto the dirt road. And it was incredibly sandy again. More pushing. Past the lowest point, the dirt road became firmer again so I could hop back on the bike. I turned west onto W Avenue B and then onto Three Points Road which soon enough became paved. Two more turns later, I was back at my car, but my energy reservoirs had been completed depleted. This bike ride had taken way longer than I anticipated. I didn’t think I had a whole lot more in me.
