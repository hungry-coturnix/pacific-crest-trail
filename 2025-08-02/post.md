# Onyx Summit to Doble

Aug 2, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.192210, -116.719179](https://maps.app.goo.gl/8witWARqmNiREcyBA) (Onyx Summit)  
**End:** [34.302287, -116.823104](https://maps.app.goo.gl/ZauHfSUhpzqQLQ8n9) (Holcomb Valley Road)

**Distance:** 20.6 mi (up to PCT mile 268.1, 10.1% completed)  
**Time:** 8 hours 24 minutes (start 6:30am, end 2:53pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 1,902 ft (low 6,762 ft – high 9,110 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 3,529 ft (start 8,504 ft – end 6,877 ft)

---

Today’s hike marked an amazing milestone: I have hiked 10% of the Pacific Crest Trail. It has been almost 10 months now since I embarked on this adventure at the border to Mexico. And I am slightly ahead of schedule. At the same time, it’s hard to believe that I am still so far south in California. The trail has given me a new purpose and it has become a constant source of excitement and happiness – despite the physically very demanding and exhausting nature of hiking it. Every time I embark on another day hike, I am full of giddiness and joy. The trail so far has exceeded my expectations in terms of beauty and scenery. I never thought that the Southern Californian desert could be so spectacular. Not a single day has been disappointing. The views are far reaching and mesmerizing. And there is way more solitude than I expected, most of the hikes had very few if any people.

Starting from Onyx Summit, today’s hike was on the eastern edge of Bear Valley. I have really come to like Big Bear over the last 3 trips. It’s a beautiful valley surrounded by mountains. It’s very green. And the temperatures are actually tolerable at this time of the year. Every time I drive up CA-18 from the Mojave Dessert, it’s stunning how quickly the landscape changes. Crossing over Nelson Ridge and seeing Bear Valley is truly spectacular. And all of it is so close to the Los Angeles metropolitan area.

![Bear Valley and the Mojave Desert in the distance (34°11’57.852″ N 116°42’54.63″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6674-Pano-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x526.jpg)

I had stayed again in the same room as the last two trips. Since Bear Valley actually has a surprisingly good Uber coverage, I opted for an Uber ride instead of a bike shuttle. The pickup spot on Holcomb Valley Road, right next to the county dump, had no cellphone signal, so I was a tad anxious again. As I was waiting, the sun started to rise over the mountains, painting the hillside in front of me in orange hues. My driver Mustafa arrived shortly after 6am. We ended up having a very engaged conversation, he quizzed me a lot about hiking and the PCT in particular. We talked about dangerous animals and poisonous plants. We even chatted about a virus that alters the brain chemistry of mice so that they get less cautious and end up eaten by a cat which is the target host of the virus.

![The expansive Mojave Desert (34°11’32.61″ N 116°42’33.444″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6646-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![View from Onyx Peak (34°11’31.674″ N 116°42’32.52″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6657-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

When we arrived at Onyx Summit, there were lots of big signs and a very large container of water. It was the day of Tour de Big Bear, a biking event around the Big Bear Valley, and one of routes was through Onyx Summit. But it was still too early in the day, so the summit was otherwise deserted. Mustafa dropped me off at 6:30am. 100 yards up the old dirt road and I was back on the PCT. At the junction, the water cache left by trail angels was about half of what it had been last time, a mere 2 gallons left for hikers.

![San Gorgonio Mountain in the distance (34°11’51.954″ N 116°42’50.652″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6669-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

After a mile, I left the PCT to hike up to Onyx Peak via Pipes Canyon Road. Views in all directions gradually opened up. Northwest across Bear Valley and Baldwin Lake, east across the desert, and southwest towards San Gorgonio Mountain. It was still cool, but the sun was already fairly intense. The peak itself had lots of fenced telecommunication facilities. I walked around them to get the best views before making my way back to the PCT. It was now a long downhill stretch to Broom Flat Road. On the other side of that road was a cooler with a label “PCT YUM BOX”, but it only had a bit of trash in it left and had not been restocked in a few months.

![A marker to Arrestre Trail Camp (34°13’40.728″ N 116°45’5.238″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6690-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Balky Horse Canyon (34°14’17.832″ N 116°44’50.994″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6698-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The trail was now paralleling Arrastre Creek which was completely dry. At 8.6 miles, I reached Arrastre Trail Camp, a nice area with picnic tables and a faucet that was not working. I lingered for a bit before continuing. A quarter mile further, there was a spring that was still flowing with a small trickle. The trail soon reached Balky Horse Canyon Road and followed the dirt road for a bit. Several ATVs had passed right before I reached the road and I could still hear them ahead for a while. Crossing Arrastre Creek, I left the dirt road and the PCT stayed on the east side of what was slowing becoming a deeper canyon. After crossing the dry canyon once more and gaining elevation, I was rewarded with expansive views east into Bighorn Mountain Wilderness and the Mojave Desert.

![Views towards Tip Toe Mountain (34°14’20.58″ N 116°45’5.358″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6704-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![View towards Erwin Lake (34°14’56.322″ N 116°45’46.218″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6709-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

At the top of the ridge, I could see west towards seasonal Erwin Lake. Now at the top of Nelson Ridge, the trail alternated between views east to Tip Toe Mountain across the Helendale Fault and west towards Baldwin Lake. I met a few more hikers, including a couple that had just parked on a dirt road right next to the trail, walked a quarter mile to a view, and asked me to take a photo of them. The trail was very exposed with little to no shade and the sun was burning relentlessly.

![The first Joshua Tree on trail (34°15’21.294″ N 116°46’1.584″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6715-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Little shade, but great views east (34°15’25.638″ N 116°46’3.552″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6718-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Panorama northeast towards the Mojave Desert (34°16’48.4366″ N 116°46’44.2226″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/281A6728-Pano-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x453.jpg)

At 19.1 miles I reached CA-18, the highway I had taken in and out of Bear Valley. There was a senior sitting under a tree next to his pickup truck with a Great Dane lying next to him on a pillow. We chatted for a moment and even though I explained it twice, I am not sure he understood the concept of day hiking the Pacific Crest Trail. Crossing the highway, the trail zigzaged up the hillside to get to a pass with great views northeast towards Silver Peak. The trail stayed on the east side of the ridge for a while before cutting across and descending towards Holcomb Valley Road. From there it was a minute to get back to my car.

Since it was the 10% milestone this time, I also wanted to share some aggregate statistics and some data that I don’t usually post.

* Total PCT distance: 268.1 miles / 432.5 km
* Total hiking distance: 350.9 miles / 564.7 km
* Total hiking time: 156 hours (across 17 hikes)

* Total driving distance: 13,200 miles / 21,200 km

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly in good condition. Dirt and loose rocks. The portion of the PCT between Erwin Lake and CA-18 is seeing regular horse usage.

**Parking:** There is plenty of parking 50 yards north of the PCT next to the dump site.

**Weather:** Sunny with blue skies and no clouds. 59°F / 15°C in the morning at the beginning of the hike. High of 109°F / 43°C in the early afternoon.

**Land Manager & Permits:** San Bernardino National Forest. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.6

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Very Easy
