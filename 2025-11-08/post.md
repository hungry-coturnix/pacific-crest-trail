# Swarthout Canyon Road to Acorn Trail

Nov 8, 2025 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [34.296380, -117.506981](https://maps.app.goo.gl/p7YAczhsZeWeAvHs8) (Swarthout Canyon Road)  
**End:** [34.354433, -117.641746](https://maps.app.goo.gl/kTCm35cZ8qTXBLQH8) (Acorn Drive & Finch Road)

**Distance:** 21.1 mi (up to PCT mile 363.9, 13.7% completed)  
**Time:** 8 hours 41 minutes (start 6:16am, end 2:57pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 10,128 ft (low 3,546 ft – high 8,545 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 7,438 ft (start 3,582 ft – end 6,272 ft)

---

I was back in Hesperia one last time, eager to hike the next section of the PCT even though I knew this one would be somewhat arduous. I really needed the distraction from all the changes at work and this was a welcome albeit exhausting way to process things. No Uber ride this time. I was getting further away from any major city and the bike ride back seemed manageable.

![Sunrise over Cajon Mountain (34°17’36.7979″ N 117°30’30.0054″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8690-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

With daylight savings time over, sunrise was at 6:17am, so that meant getting up early. The usual giddiness had me wake up before my alarm at 5am and by 5:40am I was in Wrightwood on Acorn Drive dropping off my bike. The first tree I tried to chain my bike to had too much circumference and I had to look for a smaller tree. At 6:06am I was parked in one of the small pullouts along Swarthout Canyon Road about 100 yards from the PCT.

![Cajon Canyon and Ralston Peak from Upper Lytle Creek Ridge (34°17’26.16″ N 117°31’37.524″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8720-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Cleghorn Mountain in the distance looks so small (34°18’44.226″ N 117°34’9.03″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8756-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The path up ahead was clearly visible – from Lone Pine Canyon up to the exposed top of Upper Lytle Creek Ridge. The ridge and the canyon had burned during the 2016 Blue Cut Fire. Trees would take much longer to return and the shrubs were not tall enough to provide any shade. Fortunately, the weather forecast was not too bad and with rising altitude the temperature would drop anyway. About 5 minutes into the hike I heard a gunshot from somewhere north. It immediately reminded me of the hike to Silverwood Lake where I had encountered a hunter in the morning. Lo and behold, about a mile up the switchbacks of a side canyon, I ran into a hunter in camouflage clothing with a large rifle. Little did I know that I would encounter 10 more throughout the day, at least one of them with an automatic rifle.

![Barren Lone Pine Canyon (34°18’47.364″ N 117°34’9.246″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8761-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Snow dusted Mount Baldy and Pine Mountain (34°19’2.694″ N 117°35’42.768″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8790-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The switchbacks up the ridge felt endless. I could still see my car about a mile away even though I had already hike more than three. I was about as high as Ralston Peak across Lone Pine Canyon to the northeast and views towards Cajon Canyon were getting better with ever switchback. After crossing Sharpless Ranch Road 3N29 the switchbacks abated and the trail was following the outline of the ridge while heading northwest. Every now and then I would almost reach the top of the ridge and get a glimpse to the west to Mount Baldy and Pine Mountain, both still with some residual snow on their northern slope from the storm 3 weeks ago. The contrast with Lone Pine Canyon to my right was striking. Lone Pine Canyon looked barren and dry and rather uninviting.

![San Jacinto Peak and San Gorgonio Mountain are faintly visible in the distance (34°19’15.366″ N 117°35’41.454″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8800-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![View across Upper Lytle Creek Ridge (34°19’30.072″ N 117°36’37.866″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8831-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

After 9 miles, I crossed Upper Lytle Creek Divide Road and followed the southern side of the ridge, circumnavigating an unnamed peak just east of Gobbler’s Knob. I passed the end of a dirt road with half a dozen parked trucks and several large tents set up, but no people around. Almost the entire hike so far had be a constant uphill and I could feel my energy slowly draining. Up ahead were more switchbacks. But the views had also improved significantly. I could see far to the east, with San Jacinto Peak and San Gorgonio Mountain looming in the distance. Cajon Canyon and the Cleghorn Ridge seemed small from up here. About 2 miles west of Gobbler’s Knob I reached the end of the Blue Cut Fire burn zone. Finally some pine trees – a nice change of scenery. I left San Bernardino National Forest which I had crossed over so many hikes and entered Angeles National Forest.

![Lytle Creek Canyon (34°19’38.004″ N 117°37’9.642″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8844-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Finally in pine forest looking back east (34°19’49.056″ N 117°38’6.324″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8849-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

The PCT skirted the end of forest road 3N06 and followed the outline of Wright Mountain to the north. To the south was Devil’s Backbone, a ridge that would lead to Mount Baldy via Pine Mountain and Dawson Peak. Pine Mountain still had a surprising amount of snow on its steep northern slope. Wright Mountain was also the high point of today’s hike at over 8,500 feet. Rounding the south side of Wright Mountain, I entered the area burned about a year ago by the 2024 Bridge Fire. The devastation was disheartening. Charred trees as far as the eye could see with little green remaining. Up ahead along Blue Ridge, a lot of those burned trees had been felled, I suspect to reduce risk from future storms that could uproot any of these dead trees. While most of the ground was still black dirt, a not so welcome visitor had taken root as well – I encountered the first poodle dog bush, a severe skin irritant with a pungent smell that opportunistically grows in areas disturbed by fire.

![Pungent Poodle Dog Bush (34°19’55.53″ N 117°38’20.634″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8856-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![Trees burned by the 2024 Bridge Fire (34°19’58.332″ N 117°38’24.198″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8857-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

![A memorial plaque for two hikers (34°20’10.512″ N 117°38’20.412″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/281A8861-DeNoiseAI-standard-1024x683.jpg)

I passed a memorial stone for two hikers who died in the area in 1983 and reached Acorn Trail, my path back to Wrightwood. I continued for another half mile on the PCT to ensure I would have a continuous path with my next PCT hike. The landscape was surreal and it was sad to see so much burned forest. Back at Acorn Trail, I had 3 miles of descent ahead of me. The trail was steep and switchbacked numerous times. On the northern side of the ridge, the trees were not as severely burned. The sun was already low and casting a long shadow across the valley. I reached the end of Acorn Trail and continued on paved Acorn Drive past residential homes. I was at my bike again at 3pm.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt and rocks. Somewhat overgrown at times between Sharpless Ranch Road 3N29 and Sheep Creek Truck Road 3N31. The area up to Gobbler’s Knob burned during the 2016 Blue Cut Fire and is very exposed with no shade. The area past Wright Mountain burned during the 2024 Bridge Fire and is extremely scarred with lots of downed trees.

**Parking:** There are a handful of pullouts off Swarthout Canyon Road.

**Crowds:** Fairly solitary. Met 13 people on trail, 11 hunters with rifles and 2 southbound thru hikers.

**Weather:** Sunny with blue skies and no clouds. Low of 58°F / 14°C in the morning at the beginning of the hike. High of 86°F / 30°C around 11am on the exposed ridges past Gobbler’s Knob.

**Land Manager & Permits:** San Bernardino National Forest. Angeles National Forest. San Gabriel Mountains National Monument. A very small stretch of Sheep Mountain Wilderness. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.2

Difficulty: Very Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Easy

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 9.2 mi (33 min), 353 ft elevation gain, 16.6 mph average speed, 36.7 mph maximum speed

The bike ride back was easier than I had expected. Through most of Wrightwood, it was predominantly downhill with a few stop signs. Only on the first mile or so on Lone Pine Canyon Road, it was uphill but in a manageable way. Once I was past the saddle marking the boundary between the two national forests, it was rapidly downhill by 3,000 feet. I was zooming down the road at over 35 mph / 56 kmh. My grip on the handle bar was very firm since every little bump could have been dangerous. I slowed down when I reached unpaved Swarthout Canyon Road and completed the remaining half mile back to my car at a much more modest speed.
