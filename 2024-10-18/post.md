# Southern Terminus to CA-94

Oct 18, 2024 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** 32.605475, -116.483161 (CA-94, just west of Campo, CA)  
**End:** 32.605475, -116.483161 (CA-94, just west of Campo, CA)

**Distance:** 4.5 mi (up to PCT mile 2.3, 0.09% completed)  
**Time:** 1 hour 43 minutes (start 5:05pm, end 6:48pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 678 ft (low 2,471 ft – high 2,915 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 678 ft (start 2,471 ft – end 2,471 ft)

---

And so the journey begins. I am following in the footsteps of thousands of hikers before me who have embarked on this 2650 mile endeavor from Mexico all the way to Canada. This is the very first section of the Pacific Crest National Scenic Trail.

Despite meticulous planning, the day didn’t go quite the way I had envisioned. I left home shortly after 7am, expecting to arrive in Campo around 3pm for a leisurely stroll to the Southern Terminus Monument. But how could I forget that traffic through Los Angeles is always unpredictable. Shortening my lunch break, I finally arrived at the starting point of my hike at 4:58pm. Sunset was just an hour away but this was going to be enough time if I walked briskly. I put on my gear in record time and headed south towards the border.

The trail crested the hills west of Campo and rewarded me with the first views of the Laguna Mountains before dropping into Campo and paralleling the paved and later unpaved Forest Gate Road. I passed several old bungalows, abandoned and decaying, not the most pretty sight. Leaving the village limits, the border wall appeared on the horizon, a tall and intimidating scar in the landscape. Of course I had seen photos and videos of the wall, but actually standing right in front of it was a different experience altogether. It almost felt threatening.

![Campo shortly before sunset (32°36’24.522″ N 116°28’30.09″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/281A3739.jpg)

![The border wall looming in the distance (32°35’32.88″ N 116°28’1.542″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/281A3744.jpg)

I reached the Southern Terminus Monument at 6:44pm and met two thru hikers who were sitting in the dirt and enjoying a can of beer. This was the end of their months long journey from Canada. One of them, trail name “Popeye”, was complaining about his ankle and very relieved that this was the end. Both of them ultimately got picked up by Dave, a retired veteran and trail angel, who was giving them a ride to San Diego.

The monument had two trail registers, both of them with very recent additions. I usually don’t sign trail registers, but this felt like a worthy occasion so I left two lines in one of them. The view from the southern terminus was not exactly what I had expected. I guess it was somehow flat in my imagination and much more like a desert. Instead the views were vast across the Laguna Mountains and the valley in front of them. This was the Pacific Crest Trail after all, so I shouldn’t have been surprised to see mountains.

![View north from the Southern Terminus towards Campo (32°35’23.7355″ N 116°28’1.1465″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/281A3746.jpg)

![The trail register at the Southern Terminus Monument (32°35’23.274″ N 116°28’1.14″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/281A3757.jpg)

The sun was setting in the west between the border wall and Hauser Mountain. The air was a little hazy, but that only turned the sunlight into a warm orange and red. I stayed at the monument for about half an hour, just past sunset before making my way back. The bushy landscape revealed a multitude of rabbits who were now out and about, escaping my unwelcome presence.

![The Southern Terminus Monument (32°35’23.274″ N 116°28’1.14″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/281A3751.jpg)

Halfway back, I had to put on my head lamp as it was getting dark. The cicadas were chirping. For a split second I saw two eyes reflecting the light before scurrying across the trail and into the bush. Was that a coyote? I arrived back at my car at 6:48pm. This was my first 2.3 miles on the Pacific Crest Trail, the first of many hikes over the next few years.

Addendum: I had seen a Border Patrol car while hiking south but no presence right at the border. However, on the drive to the hotel in Boulevard, I got stopped by a border patrol car. My first time getting stopped by a law enforcement officer. They asked where I was coming from and where I was going. And after I told them about my PCT adventure, they let me go.

**Trail conditions:** Mostly dirt and rock, with a few sandy stretches and about 250 yards of paved road in Campo.

**Parking:** The dirt roads leaving CA-94 to the south are all private property. However, the shoulder is wide enough for a few cars to park right where the PCT crosses the highway.

**Weather:** Somewhat hazy, with a high of 74°F (at the beginning of the hike) and a low of 57°F (at the end of the hike).

**Overall Rating:** 4.0

Difficulty: Very Easy  
Scenery: Medium
