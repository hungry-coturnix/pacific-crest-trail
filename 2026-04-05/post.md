# Tehachapi Willow Springs Road to CA-58

Apr 5, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Southern California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/southern-california/)

**Start:** [35.052179, -118.358599](https://maps.app.goo.gl/eYjVq657vSk77Aat5) (Tehachapi Willow Springs Road)  
**End:** [35.100060, -118.292786](https://maps.app.goo.gl/K51jGcersQAGd7t49) (CA-58 at Cameron Road Exit)

**Distance:** 8.2 mi (up to PCT mile 566.5, 21.4% completed)  
**Time:** 3 hours 15 minutes (start 6:57am, end 10:12am)  
**Elevation Gain:** 965 ft (low 3,805 ft – high 4,776 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 1,260 ft (start 4,139 ft – end 3,844 ft)

---

When I woke up, I was still somewhat tired from the day before. But fortunately, today was going to be much shorter than usual. It was about 8 miles to highway 58, the next stretch after that would require some backpacking. I arrived at the Cameron Road Exit, just north of highway 58 at 6:27am. It was a few minutes before sunrise and still very cold. I chained my bike to one of the sign posts and quickly hopped back into my car. The drive to Tehachapi Willow Springs Road was through Cameron Canyon. It gave me a taste of how much elevation gain I would need to tackle on my bike back and I was not very thrilled. But I pushed that thought aside for the time being.

I parked in a large pullout at the intersection of Tehachapi Willow Springs Road and Cameron Canyon Road. The sun was barely above the horizon. There was no wind yet. Most of today’s hike was across private land again, an easement marker also indicated as much. And there were lots and lots of wind turbines. The PCT wound through some of the grassland, then headed up one of the ridges. The views south were stunning. Wind turbines as far as you could see. The trail turned generally east, passing through several gates and past a few cows (which did not look very thrilled to see me).

To the left was Cameron Canyon now with another green ridge on the other side. In the distance, I could already see the Sierra Nevada, north of Tehachapi Pass. I had not realized earlier how high up from the canyon bottom I actually was. After crossing another gate, I could see the long sequence of switchbacks ahead of me. Highway 58 and the railroad tracks next to it were constantly visible. And several trains actually passed as I made my way down the mountain. Eventually, I reached Cameron Canyon Road and turned right. The last 1.5 miles was a road walk. I crossed the railroad tracks and soon after the highway itself via the overpass. My bike was waiting for me and it wasn’t too hot yet.

This also marked the end of the PCT California Section E – 112 miles from Agua Dulce through hot and dry terrain. There had been little shade along the way and several stretches were severely impacted by wildfires. Lots of great views had surprised me but overall, this section had also been less scenic than the previous ones. I was lucky to hike most of this section in bearable temperatures. Ahead of me was finally the Sierra Nevada! In PCT terms, the next 100 miles would still be part of the “desert section” but soon enough I would be entering the “High Sierra”.

---

**Trail Conditions:** Mostly dirt, occasionally somewhat overgrown with plenty of weed. Completely exposed to the sun with no shade. Lots of wind turbines for the first 6 miles.

**Parking:** There is room for plenty of cars in a pullout just north of Tehachapi Willow Springs Road.

**Crowds:** Absolute solitude. Didn’t meet a single hiker.

**Weather:** Sunny throughout the entire hike with a very light intermittent breeze. Low of 51°F / 11°C in the morning at the start of the hike after sunrise. High of 74°F / 24°C towards the end of the hike around 10am.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Mostly private land. No permit required.

**Overall Rating:** 3.3

Difficulty: Moderate  
Scenery: Moderate  
Logistics: Moderate

---

**Bike Shuttle:** 5.4 mi (40 min), 693 ft elevation gain, 8.03 mph average speed, 30.23 mph maximum speed

The elevation gain on the bike ride back was quite demanding. The first mile or so was downhill or flat, crossing the railroad tracks again. But then it was mostly uphill with the occasional flat or even downhill stretch. I was not great at cycling uphill, so I ended up pushing by bike several times through the steeper sections of the road. The sun was burning more relentlessly now, especially on my left ear. Not a single car passed me. I was very glad to finally reach the highpoint on the road. The last mile was downhill. When I got to my car, someone was just dropping off two PCT hikers who were continuing their journey.
