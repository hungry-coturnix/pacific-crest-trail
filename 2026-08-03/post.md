# Olancha Pass Trail to Trail Pass (Day 3)

Aug 3, 2026 — by [Tilman](https://pct.ahoh.de/author/tilman/) in [Central California](https://pct.ahoh.de/category/central-california/)

**Start:** [36.332301, -118.133937](https://maps.app.goo.gl/TALQ4XboT169h76QA) (Campsite north of Death Canyon)  
**End:** [36.448336, -118.170638](https://maps.app.goo.gl/DmW1mNZnJkUwNtfk9) (Cottonwood Pass Trailhead)

**Distance:** 16.3 mi (up to PCT mile 746.5, 28.2% completed)  
**Time:** 7 hours 55 minutes (start 6:15am, end 2:10pm)  
**Elevation Gain:** 2,823 ft (low 9,506 ft – high 10,674 ft)  
**Elevation Loss:** 2,393 ft (start 9,506 ft – end 9,936 ft)

---

It was the last day of my three day trip through Golden Trout Wilderness. The final leg of the circuit would follow the high spine of the Pacific Crest Trail northward along the eastern crest of the Sierra Nevada. Crossing dramatic high points overlooking the deep escarpment above Owens Valley, I would be descending to Diaz Creek before tackling one last climb up to Mulkey Pass and closing the loop at Trail Pass.

When my alarm went off at 5:30am, dawn was already breaking across the sky as Golden Trout Wilderness slowly woke up around me. Having packed extra water up from Death Canyon the previous evening, I had plenty left for the morning. My first target water source was Diaz Creek, roughly 9 miles ahead – but first there were more than 1,000 feet of immediate climbing ahead of me. I broke camp quickly and had a light snack, eager to knock out the bulk of the day’s elevation gain while temperatures were still manageable. It was 53°F / 12°C when I started hiking, but with clear skies, it was warming up rapidly. Another hot High Sierra day lay ahead.

![Expansive views across the heart of Golden Trout Wilderness, Templeton Mountain with Strawberry Meadow and Templeton Meadow below, Kern Peak in the distance (36°20’23.364″ N 118°7’53.598″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4683-1024x683.jpg)

The morning vistas were spectacular, stretching south toward Olancha Peak and Death Canyon, and west across the vast expanse of the Kern Plateau. Looking back, the rising sun cast a warm golden glow across the pine canopy directly behind the crest, while Olancha Peak loomed quietly in the cool shadows beyond. I stopped briefly at an adjacent viewpoint that offered expansive vistas straight across the heart of the Golden Trout Wilderness. The low morning light slowly lit up the Kern Basin, illuminating Templeton Mountain with Strawberry Meadow and Templeton Meadow spread out below, while Kern Peak rose distinctly along the distant horizon. Maintaining a steady pace, I continued uphill. A brief crossing over a high saddle offered a quick break at a remarkably scenic vantage point looking east straight down toward Owens Valley, its vast desert floor framed sharply by rugged granite outcroppings and towering conifers.

![The distant pale expanse of Owens Valley and Owens Lake from a saddle on the Pacific Crest Trail (36°21’20.83″ N 118°7’24.5988″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4690-1024x683.jpg)

![The final push up the last switchbacks to the highest point of the day (36°21’29.514″ N 118°7’32.712″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4696-1024x683.jpg)

The trail resumed its push upward, winding along a series of dry, rocky switchbacks that cut through ancient foxtail pines and granite talus near the rim. I finally hit the highest point of the day at 10,674 feet / 3,253 meters. Standing right on the precipice of the Sierra Crest, the dramatic drop-off to the east was breathtaking. Directly below, I caught a first glimpse of the South Fork Ash Creek drainage – a deep, rugged canyon incised into sheer granite walls that fell away toward the vast, pale floor of Owens Valley far below. The full view down into the South Fork Ash Creek canyon moments later was nothing short of jaw-dropping. Massive, blocky granite crags dominated the foreground while the steep V-shaped gorge plunged thousands of feet down toward the expansive white expanse of the dry lakebed below. Scattered along the very edge of the precipice, solitary foxtail pines clung tenaciously to the granite seams, framed against the sheer drop and the hazy distant horizon.

![A first glimpse of the South Fork Ash Creek drainage (36°21’33.396″ N 118°7’32.232″ W](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4697-1024x683.jpg)

![The dramatic view of the rugged South Fork Ash Creek canyon (36°21’40.6748″ N 118°7’34.6247″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4700-1024x683.jpg)

After taking in the dramatic drops into Owens Valley, the PCT began turning northwest toward the interior plateau. Looking back over the trees toward the southwest, another sweeping perspective opened up across Templeton Meadow nestled deep within the basin, with the pale granite peak of Kern Peak rising sharply on the horizon. The trail descended at a moderate gradient, passing above a seasonal spring feeding Ash Creek that I skipped. The path leveled out for about a mile before reaching the crest south of Mulkey Meadows, then continued dropping down towards a saddle west of Diaz Creek. Across the forested drainage, the prominent peak of Trail Peak came into view through the pine trees, serving as a clear reminder of the final climb waiting on the other side.

![Solitary foxtail pines perched between the massive granite slabs on the edge of the crest (36°21’44.556″ N 118°7’38.484″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4713-1024x683.jpg)

![Templeton Meadows with Kern Peak in the distance (36°23’5.136″ N 118°9’23.61″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4723-1024x683.jpg)

Dropping my backpack alongside the PCT, I grabbed my water bag and followed a well-beaten side path down to Diaz Creek. To my relief, a clear ribbon of water was winding through the low willows and sandy terrain beneath the rocky slopes. The stream was flowing well, allowing me to quickly scoop up a full bag. Sitting on my bear canister while the water filtered, I enjoyed a cold meal of [Peak Refuel Strawberry Granola](https://peakrefuel.com/products/strawberry-granola-meal) – a necessary adaptation after running out of stove fuel the night before. Rejoining the PCT, the trail cut across a wide, sandy saddle west of Diaz Creek, offering a clear view toward the forested slopes of Muah Mountain rising in the distance behind me. Ahead of me was the last major ascent of the trip: an exposed, sun-drenched climb gaining nearly 1,000 feet back up the ridge. Settling into a slow, rhythmic pace, I began the push.

![Looking at Trail Peak, halfway to Mulkey Pass (36°25’41.916″ N 118°9’20.4″ W)](https://pct.ahoh.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/281A4735-1024x683.jpg)

Halfway up the ascent to Mulkey Pass, I looked back across the canyon through weathered foxtail pines to catch a prominent view of Trail Peak standing tall against the cloudless blue sky. Mulkey Meadows was at the bottom of the canyon to my left, but mostly obscured by trees. Cresting the climb at Mulkey Pass, I pulled into the shade for my final trail break of the trip. A small, rustic wooden sign mounted to a thick tree trunk marked the junction, pointing north toward Trail Pass and south back toward Olancha Pass. Now it was just a mile left to Trail Pass to close the loop I had started. From a granite outcrop just off the pass, I caught a magnificent open view stretching across the broad, golden expanse of Mulkey Meadow far below in the basin. The switchbacks down the north side of Trail Pass felt easier than I remembered going up. Crossing Horseshoe Meadow under the bright afternoon sun, the wide, open basin stretched out ahead with the small stream meandering along across trail and the pale, sandy slopes of Cirque Peak looming prominently on the distant horizon. Finally back at my car, I felt a familiar mix of complete physical exhaustion and deep gratitude for three unforgettable days deep in the High Sierra backcountry.

**Trail Conditions:** The Pacific Crest Trail is generally in very good condition except for the stretch before and after Mulkey Pass where the trail is quite washed out. Trail Pass Trail is quite sandy north of Trail Pass.

**Parking:** There is plenty of room for cars at the trailhead.

**Crowds:** Very lightly traveled. Only met one southbound backpacker in the early morning and another backpacker coming up from Horseshoe Meadow close to the end.

**Weather:** Sunny with no clouds throughout the entire day. A light intermittent breeze after 9am. Low of 53°F / 12°C in the morning at the beginning of the hike. High of 85°F / 29°C on the ascent to Mulkey Pass. Humidity between 35% in the morning and 12% in the afternoon.

**Land Manager & Permits:** Golden Trout Wilderness. Inyo National Forest. Wilderness permit required (no quota).

**Overall Rating:** 4.2

Difficulty: Strenuous  
Scenery: Great  
Logistics: Very Easy
